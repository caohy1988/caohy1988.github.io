# Intent — one story, told once, for a leadership reader

The pack has been true for a while. It has not been a story. It reads as a chronology of lab days: what ran on the
sixth, what ran on the seventh, what ran on the eighth, with job counts and fixture plumbing beside each. A VP does
not need our calendar. They need the arc — here is the problem, here is what the product would do, here is what we
already know works, here is what is still open, here is the decision.

So this pass tells it once, in that order. Capabilities and limits replace run logs. "A connected path has run,
including through graph queries" replaces three dated paragraphs that each re-derive it. The limits stay exactly as
binding as before, but they are stated as properties of what we know — a single identity, a start we chose rather
than discovered, a refusal suite not yet exercised there — rather than as footnotes to a particular afternoon.

Three things leave the reader-facing page entirely: personal names and handles, the lab calendar, and the test
detail. Ownership becomes a role. The decision becomes an event, not a date on a wall. The evidence stays one
friendly link away, and the lab notebook stays where lab notebooks belong.

Nothing true becomes less true. If the story cannot carry a limit, the story is wrong, not the limit.
