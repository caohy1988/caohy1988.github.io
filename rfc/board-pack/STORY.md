## The scene and arithmetic

Maya Chen, VP of Finance at subscription software company Alder, checks the board pack at 8:55 a.m. before a 9 a.m. meeting. A $4 million plan to expand sales to existing customers rests on an agent's claim of 118% retention, labeled “verified.” An analyst catches the inclusion of new customers. Maya pulls the slide; the investment needs a different justification.

The agent found Finance's definition but missed the accompanying rule: count revenue only from customers there at the start. It reused a total-ARR query. Maya cannot trace which policy allowed this agent to use that asset. The “verified” label has no execution receipt connecting the query, the declared retention calculation and the reported result. These are the three unanswered questions after she pulls the slide: a missing linked rule, access not explainable, execution not proven. The access gap is missing explanation, not evidence of forbidden access.

New sales hid a decline among the opening customer cohort. All amounts below are annual recurring revenue (ARR), measured at the opening and close of one illustrative quarter.

| Input | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Opening ARR of the starting customer cohort | $10.0m |
| Closing ARR from the same customers, including expansion, contraction and churn | $9.6m |
| Closing ARR from new customers | $2.2m |

The wrong calculation is `(9.6 + 2.2) / 10.0 = 118%`. Correct retention is `9.6 / 10.0 = 96%`: a 4% decline among existing customers. No query, receipt or attestation produced these hypothetical figures. The narrative does not assert the investment itself is necessarily bad.

## Why the graph matters before the calculation

Maya's agent needs a connected set of context: the approved retention definition, its starting-cohort rule and its declared computation. Citing the definition alone leaves room to use an unrelated total-ARR query. The proposed runtime retrieves that connected context through an explicit query against a pinned OKF publication, then separately runs and accounts for the metric computation.

The BigQuery case begins with knowledge retrieval. It is especially strong for analytical agents whose relevant facts already live in BigQuery, on Enterprise or Enterprise Plus capacity, within latency, freshness, concurrency and cost budgets the customer has agreed to. No customer has agreed such a budget yet. The page makes no claim about where fictional Alder keeps its revenue tables.

## Three distinct advantages — proposed runtime

Keep the service roles in one short introduction: Knowledge Catalog discovers/governs; OKF authors the graph. Replace the page's former role cards with three paired comparisons of **KC + OKF** and **+ BQ runtime**. Each pair carries one story takeaway.

1. **Replayable context:** KC discovery and authored OKF relations leave context assembly to the retriever. The BigQuery projection, pinned publication and explicit SQL/bounded walks select the retention definition, cohort rule and declared computation together. This controls the context received by the agent, not the computation it ultimately executes.
2. **Explainable access:** custom entries use an EntryGroup access boundary today; that is not a universal statement about all KC permissions. The proposed runtime joins identity, policy and projected assets, binds the authenticated requester to the execution identity, enforces access along the retrieval path and records returned nodes. The payoff is explaining why Maya's agent could use the total-ARR asset. No unauthorized access is asserted.
3. **Verifiable execution:** finding Finance's declaration does not establish that retention was computed. Run the metric and validate a job ↔ context ↔ result receipt against that declaration. A total-ARR substitution or missing evidence leaves the claim unproven. This closes Maya's actual near-miss and is distinct from context replay and permission evaluation.

Access relationships stored as metadata are not authorization controls by themselves. Source permissions must stay current; metadata visibility does not grant access to the underlying file. A shared service account alone does not identify the requesting user or agent. Per-node enforcement and the requester/execution binding are proposed integration work, not a built property of Graph-over-OKF. Historical context pins must never bypass current authorization.

## Determinism is a contract, not a slogan

“Same inputs → same context and result shape” requires a deterministic query, fixed publication, query parameters, fact versions, authorization scope and stable ordering. Avoid volatile functions, unstable tie handling and unversioned external inputs. Graph-walk bounds and the returned schema are explicit. An immutable context publication alone does not freeze mutable fact tables or access policy.

Vector similarity ranks likely passages; an LLM can choose different excerpts or paths. Vector retrieval is not inherently nondeterministic. The distinction is explicit, inspectable traversal semantics over versioned graph inputs. The guarantee covers context retrieval, not identical model answers, business truth or permission bypass.

A retrieval receipt records how context was selected. It does not prove that the retention computation ran or returned 96%. The pilot must test both the retrieval contract and the separate computation-evidence boundary. Withhold execution evidence and the number stays unproven.

## Capacity and the first-workload route

Graph walks (GQL) require Enterprise or Enterprise Plus capacity; ordinary relational SQL and vector retrieval also run on-demand. A projected graph can be the first workload on such capacity after its inputs are loaded, but the strongest case remains a customer whose facts are already in BigQuery, and the page says so. If no graph or facts enter BigQuery, no BigQuery retrieval occurs.

The OKF bundle remains the authored source. Catalog and BigQuery are projections with different roles. Do not imply an implemented Catalog-to-BigQuery import or change the full RFC's source-bundle authority. A source pin is not a working live KC discovery integration; the 2026-09-07 runs read a live catalog entry to confirm which publication to pin, which is narrower than an implemented Catalog-to-BigQuery import.

## What the early experiments establish (2026-09-07)

Three recorded experiments exist, all run on invented gross-margin data for a company called Acme. None ran the Alder story or produced its retention figures.

- **Receipt check** ([recorded evidence](https://github.com/GoogleCloudPlatform/BigQuery-Agent-Analytics-SDK/blob/6719eb535667963fa640dd4535e508b550eb6cb1/examples/okf_attested_computation/evidence/receipt/report.md)): a BigQuery job owned by the caller, the job and result read back from BigQuery itself, verification in a fresh process, and a consumer that released the result only after the check passed. Wrong-query, wrong-parameter, tampered, replayed and missing-evidence cases released nothing. This is one worked example, not a library feature, product release or independent verifier. A passed check is an evidence verdict for that example only.
- **Graph walk** ([recorded results](https://github.com/caohy1988/caohy1988.github.io/blob/bb88df40014dc8030b42165308840c62337af429/rfc/spikes/bq-graph/evidence/report.md)): run on a measured Enterprise reservation on 5 September 2026. Starting from a retired definition it followed two links to the sanctioned SQL, produced an impact list matching a relational cross-check, and handled placeholder, ambiguous and out-of-scope entries on a deliberately awkward test bundle (Acme's own backlog had no placeholders). Its report counts this as demonstrated retrieval only: the SQL it returned was found, not run. The speed benchmarks are not finished yet (0 of 9 cells complete). The reservation was recorded as torn down; that is not an audit of every job or fixture resource.
- **Access checks with a second identity (2026-09-06)** ([access case records](https://github.com/caohy1988/caohy1988.github.io/blob/bb88df40014dc8030b42165308840c62337af429/rfc/spikes/bq-graph/evidence/authz_cases.json), [summary and limits](https://github.com/caohy1988/caohy1988.github.io/blob/bb88df40014dc8030b42165308840c62337af429/rfc/spikes/bq-graph/README.md#second-principal-2026-09-06)): five checks with a separate restricted identity passed on the SQL retrieval path over the same protected tables as the walk: a hidden intermediate rule stayed hidden, a denied bundle returned no denied node or section identifiers in the reply or the error text (bundle, publication, project, dataset and table names can still appear), output was withheld while the seed store stayed readable to that identity by a plain row count (search itself was denied), no request fell back to the owner's credential, and access revoked before a cached replay failed closed. Each was graded behind an allowed request under the same identity, and the temporary grants were removed and read back. Still open: the same checks inside graph queries (they need an Enterprise capacity window this run could not open), the no-hidden-identifier check under the operator's own identity, and protection across mixed publications. These cover three of the connected-path tests below in isolation. A separate chain on 2026-09-07 later ran the same restricted identity through a chain (next bullet but one); neither changes an assessment.
- **Chains on Acme from a live catalog read (2026-09-07)** ([chain record](https://github.com/caohy1988/caohy1988.github.io/blob/50ecaa8976cb5fb633fb1c2b11b70eba3bbcd939/rfc/spikes/bq-graph/evidence/catalog-chain/live-20260907T153329Z-21d91539/chain_live.json), [how these runs work and their limits](https://github.com/caohy1988/caohy1988.github.io/blob/50ecaa8976cb5fb633fb1c2b11b70eba3bbcd939/rfc/spikes/bq-graph/README.md#catalog-seeded-chain-live-2026-09-07-slice-b), [what each of the ten owned-lifecycle cases showed](https://github.com/caohy1988/caohy1988.github.io/blob/50ecaa8976cb5fb633fb1c2b11b70eba3bbcd939/rfc/spikes/bq-graph/evidence/catalog-chain/b2-20260907t162636z-cd45/b2_summary.md), [the job-by-job identity readback](https://github.com/caohy1988/caohy1988.github.io/blob/50ecaa8976cb5fb633fb1c2b11b70eba3bbcd939/rfc/spikes/bq-graph/evidence/catalog-chain/b2-20260907t162636z-cd45/job_identity_followup.json)): the chain started at a live read of a catalog entry and ran under one operator holding both legs, so the access step was still not exercised against a second identity or a policy decision, and retrieval still used plain SQL on demand rather than the graph walk. The read listed the entry group, matched one entry by name and fetched its runtime aspect; the returned pin was checked field by field against configuration already trusted, so a catalog entry can confirm which publication to use but cannot point the chain at a different project or dataset. The chain then resolved the pin against the stored publication, compiled the pinned source as its trusted copy, retrieved the declared gross-margin calculation and its SQL through governed retrieval, checked every returned node, edge and governance field against that trusted copy, handed the calculation to the receipt check, which ran the job under the caller and verified it in its own process, and released the number only after that check passed. A substituted query was refused by the verifier; a mismatched declaration was refused before anything ran; all 23 jobs that run submitted shared one requester. Nine more chains ran the same way — same operator, same plain SQL — against an entry and dataset created for the test, because the original entry must not be republished or deleted: a head moved to a second version while a request was in flight and the request still served the version it had pinned, exactly; a withdrawn publication, removed catalog metadata (the entry’s runtime pin, with the authored entries checked and left intact — not an authored rule) and two pins that did not exist each stopped the chain at the step that failed, the metadata case at the very first step before any retrieval ran; a restored pin connected again; and two payloads tampered with in our own client, after BigQuery had returned them, were refused before the receipt check ran (BigQuery did not return a torn publication). The test entry and dataset were deleted with an absence readback, the original entry was unchanged, and all 170 jobs those runs submitted were read back one at a time, each finished, under one identity. Not shown: a second identity or policy decision (one operator on both legs), graph queries (plain SQL on demand, not the graph walk above), an independent attester, a customer's catalog (ours, seeded earlier for the experiment), Alder's retention (Acme's invented gross margin), and Maya's total-ARR swap (the receipt example's fixed formula swap; the receipt still runs on that example's own small fixture). An earlier chain on 2026-09-06 covered the same middle segment from a starting point chosen by hand rather than a catalog read. It changes no assessment.
- **A chain under a restricted identity (2026-09-07)** ([chain record](https://github.com/caohy1988/caohy1988.github.io/blob/ad18b075524d7b752c5782da17c29e70cee05fb7/rfc/spikes/bq-graph/evidence/chain/chain_live_restricted.json), [how it works and its limits](https://github.com/caohy1988/caohy1988.github.io/blob/ad18b075524d7b752c5782da17c29e70cee05fb7/rfc/spikes/bq-graph/README.md#live-restricted-pass-slice-b-2026-09-07), [the job-by-job identity reconciliation](https://github.com/caohy1988/caohy1988.github.io/blob/ad18b075524d7b752c5782da17c29e70cee05fb7/rfc/spikes/bq-graph/evidence/chain/job_audit_chain_live_restricted.json)): a separate chain ran retrieval and the receipt check under the deliberately restricted identity rather than the operator, whose credential only granted, revoked, restored and read back job identities. The temporary grants and row-policy grantees were restored and read back. Not shown: a catalog read (this chain used the hand-chosen fixture seed), graph queries (plain SQL on demand), an independent attester, and real cohort data. Receipt work should not wait for the graph benchmark. We reassess the opportunity at the 2026-09-19 checkpoint; it narrows if budgets or Enterprise cost prove unacceptable, simpler retrieval meets the need, semantics, authorization or publication guarantees fail, or result evidence cannot be bound and enforced.
