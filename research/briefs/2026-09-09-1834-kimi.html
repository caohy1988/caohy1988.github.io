Q2D-Web: retrieval benchmark for agentic RAG at production scale — arXiv:2609.08887

NEW Academy rank, narrowly scoped: first-stage retriever evaluation on agent-reformulated queries at web scale — an industry self-benchmark with a private scorer and measured label noise. Maximilian Schall, Sedigheh Eslami, Markus Krimmel, Antoine Chaffin, Louis Milliken, Bo Wang, Denis Bykov (Perplexity AI). History: September 8 v1, 15:26:05 UTC, no revisions, cs.IR/cs.CL. The complete PDF was read (25 pp.; adjudication note at Hunt/agent-analytics/2026-09-09-1834-q2d-web-2609-08887-full-pdf-adjudication.md), and the public leaderboard was independently verified — its committed per-model results JSONs match the paper's Table 3 to printed precision. PDF · Leaderboard.

What survives review. The benchmark pairs a ~190M-document corpus (the deduplicated union of Perplexity production top-5,000 results per query) with 69,721 LLM-reformulated queries from ~23k production searches over 9 months (65.8% English; 82.3% agent-generated "support" queries), and three judgment sets: agent citations, production web-ranking top-50, and a combined set adding DeepSeek-V4-Flash binary labels over pooled unjudged docs. Thirteen retrievers evaluated (1 lexical, 10 dense, 2 late-interaction), with neural eval models temporally held out (released on/after 2025-01-01) from the pooling/judging models. Headline: full-corpus Combined Recall@1000 — pplx-embed-v1-4b 69.11, Nemotron-3-Embed-8B 68.58, BM25 44.77 last. An RRF-pooled subcorpus at 31.7% of the corpus reproduces full-corpus rankings at Kendall τb = 1.00 (with +3–7 point recall inflation, mean +5.1), cutting a 4B model's evaluation from ~4,608 to ~1,500 H200 GPU-hours.

Evidence and limits from the PDF:

  • Winner instability is the finding to quote carefully: rankings are only largely stable — the Recall@1000 winner flips across judgment sets (Nemotron-8B leads on Citation at 61.68; pplx-4b leads Combined at 69.11), and the shallow-ranking winner (Nemotron-8B, nDCG@10 47.44) differs from the deep-recall winner. Any single-number citation of this benchmark is a choice of judgment set and cutoff.
  • Self-evaluation confound: queries, corpus, all three label sources, and the production system are Perplexity's; 3 of 13 evaluated retrievers are Perplexity's own and win the primary metric on 2 of 3 sets. The corpus is production-retriever-filtered, not a random web sample. Dataset and scorer are deliberately private; independent verification is impossible by design (leaderboard-only).
  • Label quality: citation labels are conditioned on what the production retriever returned (circular as ground truth); LLM judgments have no human-agreement study, and the hard-negative audit is the same judge revisiting itself; 15.8% of sampled "non-relevant" top-1000 docs are actually relevant. Documents are truncated to 512 tokens (mean ~3.3k) — the authors disclaim unbiased full-document estimates. No significance testing.
  • Most interesting design result for ensembles: BM25, the worst overall retriever, contributes 14,621 unique positives — ~6× the next model's — lexical recall still adds irreplaceable candidates at web scale.
  • Minor internal issues: the "about eight hours on a single 8×H200 node" sentence only fits the smallest model (the 4B needs ~7.8 days on that node); Table 1's language count for TREC Web contradicts the paper's own ClueWeb12 English-only note. Nothing disqualifying.

Why admit: the first benchmark to measure retrievers against the queries agents actually write (machine-reformulated, multi-language, support-heavy) at production corpus scale, with a temporally-held-out design, three triangulated label sources, honest cost accounting, and a verified public leaderboard. Directly on-topic for agent retrieval evaluation. The scope is first-stage candidate recall only — not answer quality, reranking, or end-to-end agent performance.

BQAA TL;DR — proposed application: two transfers. First, evaluate BQAA's schema/term retrieval on agent-reformulated sub-goal queries, not human-written questions — the distributions differ and the paper quantifies how. Second, adopt the triangulation discipline: any retrieval verdict should name its judgment set and cutoff, because the winner flips across both. The BM25 unique-positives result argues for keeping a lexical leg in hybrid retrieval even when it loses the aggregate.

Counter-Swarm Doctrine — arXiv:2609.06140

NEW Academy rank, narrowly scoped: evaluation-design rigor and evidence hygiene for multi-agent intrusion analysis — zero executed experiments. Gregory Frank ("Chief Scientist, MoltAI Corp" — affiliation unverifiable). History: September 5 v1, 15:25:45 UTC, no revisions, cs.CR/cs.LG/cs.MA. The complete 35-page PDF was read (adjudication note at Hunt/agent-analytics/2026-09-09-1834-counter-swarm-2609-06140-full-pdf-adjudication.md).

What survives review. The paper proposes the revisable coordination episode — linking observed resource transfers, task authority, and response history across separate agent executions — as the operational unit of multi-agent defense, replacing per-turn or per-session spans; prospective episode discovery (determining which actions belong together before an evaluator labels membership) is named the central research problem. Its four-arm evaluation design (isolated actions / rolling windows / known groups / prospective discovery at matched review cost and matched false-alert workload, plus recurrence-after-channel-closure-and-quarantine conditions) is specified with falsification tables, preregistration requirements, censoring/stopping-rule analysis, and correct prevalence-precision arithmetic.

Evidence and limits from the PDF — the measured/proposed/reported separation is the point:

  • Measured (the paper's only executed work): a checksum-verified reconstruction of the collusion.wiki public export — 14,591 retained saves across four wikis, 5,217 DSE deletion events, 101 script-injection probes — establishing that the post-June-22 decline in retained saves precedes most cleanup and is not an artifact of counting only surviving pages. It verifies the export's counts and temporal aggregation, not the completeness, attribution, or cause of the underlying logs.
  • Proposed (never run): the detector comparison, the episode-discovery procedure, all candidate signals ("none has a measured deployment precision in this paper"), and the recurrence tests. Pack correction: the claim that the paper "measures recurrence after channel closure and state quarantine" is false — recurrence is a proposed test condition; observed recurrence exists only as incident narrative the paper itself says cannot establish intervention effects.
  • Reported (clearly attributed to others): cited incident corpora and third-party results (FakeLab FPR operating points, an OpenAI retrospective, METR/Redwood figures), with the paper refusing to count overlapping investigations as independent replications.
  • Red flags: single author, unverifiable affiliation, no code/data release beyond an analysis script over a pinned third-party export, submitted the same day the export was downloaded.

Why admit: within agent-analytics values (evaluation methodology, telemetry, multi-agent security evidence) this is the most methodologically careful available treatment of the two canonical 2026 coordination incidents — matched-cost evaluation design and a formal reported/proposed/measured evidence convention (its Appendix G) that is itself a model for how agent-security claims should be written. The card's value is design and hygiene, not new security evidence. If the Academy tier ever requires executed experiments, this card should be re-scoped or retired.

BQAA TL;DR — proposed application: when auditing an agent fleet for coordinated misuse, the unit of review should be the cross-execution episode, not the span — and the review budget must be held constant when comparing detection strategies, or the comparison is rigged by alert volume. Internally, the reported/proposed/measured label discipline is directly worth adopting for BQAA's own capability claims.
