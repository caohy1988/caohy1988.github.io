Read this first

Amazon Neptune Graph Sessions is the one Industry NEW rank. The GA compute mode lets an agent, user or task have its own graph starting at 4 or 8 s-NCU, with up to twice that working-memory headroom. It scales to zero after 15 idle minutes, retains the graph and data, and resumes in seconds. This is a useful state-lifecycle and cost control for GraphRAG and per-session analytics, not evidence of better answers: BQAA should measure resume latency, query success, memory consumed and cost per completed task under its own workloads.

The Academy reads offer three distinct levers. Black-box test selection can cut a specific GPT 5.2 Codex SWE-bench Verified run from $225 to $47 with 0.8 percentage points of success-rate error, while a difficulty-judged subset finds 53.0 failures versus 27.4 for random selection. Those are different evaluation objectives, not one universally superior sampler. RecordRoute improves failure attribution by diagnosing from a selected record, but remains supervised and has weak absolute localization on natural failures. TokenProv joins signed authorization, provider acceptances and receipts into an auditable bill; its guarantees stop at the authenticated evidence model and do not cover a fully consistent colluding transcript.

1. Amazon Neptune Graph Sessions GA introduces per-agent graphs that scale to zero and resume with state intact

Primary: https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/amazon-neptune-graph-sessions-generally-available (GA; AWS What’s New feed pubDate 2026-11-27T19:00:00Z).

Neptune Graph Sessions is a compute mode for Neptune Analytics designed for agents and other workloads whose graphs are small, numerous and hard to size in advance. An application can allocate a separate graph to an agent, user, conversation or task instead of paying the 16 m-NCU minimum of a provisioned graph. The starting choices are 4 or 8 s-NCU; each includes headroom to 8 or 16 s-NCU respectively, and AWS bills for the memory the graph actually uses.

After 15 idle minutes, the graph scales to zero automatically. AWS says the next query resumes it in seconds without reloading the retained graph or data. The graphs are created through the console, CLI or SDKs and queried with openCypher like other Neptune Analytics graphs.

Why it ranks. Per-task graph isolation, explicit memory units and an idle/resume lifecycle are concrete controls for agent memory and per-session analytics. BQAA should record graph/session identity, requested and observed memory, idle interval, resume latency, query outcome and infrastructure cost beside model cost, then compare dedicated sessions against a shared graph. AWS supplies no independent accuracy, latency or cost benchmark in this announcement; “resumes in seconds” is a vendor statement, and GraphRAG quality remains workload-dependent. 1. Where Did My Tokens Go? Verifiable Token Billing for LLM Services — https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31806

Full PDF read: yes — entire 18-page PDF read via pdftotext, including the formal model, protocol, security analysis, evaluation, conclusion, references and Appendices A–C. arXiv v1 is dated 2026-11-24; no catalog card exists for this id.

Problem and method. TokenProv starts with signed user authorization and an authenticated lifecycle DAG, then uses bilateral harness–broker and broker–provider handoffs to bind intent, dispatch and provider acceptance to the same call. Settlement requires a one-to-one match between observable provider acceptances and receipts, including independently retained handoffs that expose omitted calls. Provider assurance binds deployment, cache-dependent execution, usage and price version through either an attested state transition or a committed-witness proof; proof generation can be deferred until audit (§3–4; Algorithm 1).

Results to reuse. Across five models, signed-receipt TokenProv reaches 94.4–107.4% of Vanilla mean throughput, with 54–66 ms/request of online non-LLM work (§6.2; Figure 4 and Table 6). In an eight-call replay, deferred scoped proofs reduce foreground protocol time from 1.20 s to 8.65 ms (99.28%), while total replay time is 1.34 s versus 1.32 s with immediate proving (§6.3; Figure 7 and Table 11). The auditor rejects all 1,600 validly signed adaptive attack queries and accepts all 100 benign controls; separate splicing and first-failure tests also reject/localize their fixtures (Table 4).

Qualifications. The serving test uses co-located Python roles, a local ledger, synthetic fixed-length calls and five 1.7B–14B models, not commercial provider invoices (§6.1). The scoped Groth16 circuit checks an accounting commitment, integer charges and request-hash equality, not general proof of every provider’s full inference pipeline (§6.3; Appendix C.2). Deferral shifts proof work rather than eliminating it. Most importantly, the guarantee is relative to authenticated evidence: H+B hidden execution and H+B+P consistent-transcript controls each accept 500/500 trials, so fully consistent collusion remains outside detection (§6.5; Table 15(b)). BQAA can reuse the evidence schema—fresh call ids, signed policy/pricing versions, cache-use buckets, acceptance/receipt uniqueness and independent handoffs—but should mark missing or mutually consistent evidence as incomplete rather than “verified.”

2. RecordRoute: Auditing Agent Failures with Localization-Guided Diagnosis — https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33022

Full PDF read: yes — entire 13-page PDF read via pdftotext, including the method, all benchmark and generalization experiments, ablations, conclusion, references and Appendices A–C with efficiency and LLM controls. arXiv v1 is dated 2026-11-24; no catalog card exists for this id.

Problem and method. RecordRoute strips identifying headers, represents each record with word and character TF-IDF plus query overlap, and learns pairwise preferences within each failed trajectory. The highest-scoring record supplies actor and step; a separate class-balanced linear SVM diagnoses the error mode from that record’s text, metadata and actor rather than from the pooled trace (§4). Training is supervised on labeled failures, not a zero-shot root-cause oracle.

Results to reuse. On 6,257 controlled-injection Who&When Pro failures, RecordRoute reaches 90.86% pooled exact-step and 77.47% pooled joint accuracy (Table 6). With actor and step predictions fixed, selected-record diagnosis scores 77.47% joint accuracy versus 69.28% for concatenated full-trace diagnosis and 64.95% for learned aggregation (Table 3). On natural failures it reaches 27.06% exact-step accuracy on TraceElephant versus 18.81% for ASCon and 23.85% for Qwen2.5-14B; on CatchBench POST it reaches 37.46% Top-1, 77.78% Top-3 and 0.590 MRR (Table 1). The selected record contains a median 19.77% of preprocessed trace tokens (§1).

Qualifications. Natural-failure Top-1 accuracy remains low in absolute terms, and the system requires benchmark-specific labeled training. Who&When Pro and TraceElephant record bodies are truncated to 800 characters (Appendix A). In unseen-framework evaluation, macro exact-step accuracy is 54.97% versus StepFinder’s 44.41%, but pooled accuracy is 49.03%, slightly below the 49.38% last-step baseline (Figure 3; Table 10). On a separate 128-case LLM control, local diagnosis cuts API input tokens by 54.86% and 57.00%, excluding locator training and inference (Appendix B.2). BQAA should retain the selected record and model confidence beside the full trace, evaluate task/framework-held-out slices and show top-k review queues rather than treating a predicted origin as causal truth.

3. Evaluating More with Less: Black-Box Test Selection for AI Agents — https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34617

Full PDF read: yes — entire 21-page PDF read via pdftotext, including all selection strategies, experimental protocol, both RQs, cost and sensitivity analyses, threats to validity, conclusion and references. arXiv v1 is dated 2026-11-25; no catalog card exists for this id.

Problem and method. The study compares 14 pre-execution selection strategies plus a historical-performance baseline across ten agent benchmarks with 12–101 published agent configurations each. Task-side strategies use categories, lengths, textual diversity, embeddings, heuristics or independent LLM difficulty judgments; the target agent need not be executed to construct the ordering. The two objectives are deliberately separated: estimate full-suite Success Rate or expose failures early (§3–4).

Results to reuse. Category Partition has the lowest median success-rate estimation error on five of ten benchmarks at a 20% budget and significantly outperforms Random overall (§5.1; Table 3 and Figure 1). For GPT 5.2 Codex on SWE-bench Verified, it selects 100 rather than 500 tasks, cuts recorded execution cost from $225 to $47 and has 0.8 percentage points of absolute error (§8); across all 13 Verified agents, the corresponding medians are $47 and 1.8 points (Table 5). For early failure detection, Factor-Based LLM-as-Judge finds a median 53.0 failures at the same budget versus 27.4 for Random (Table 4), but its selected tasks cost $80 versus $47 for Category Partition (Table 5).

Qualifications. The authors reuse published outcomes rather than rerunning agents; binary conversion thresholds, oracle errors and environment differences can affect the results, and SWE-bench Mini overlaps Verified (§7). Judge contamination is not ruled out, prompts are not systematically varied, and difficulty may depend on tool behavior rather than task text. Sonnet judgments cost $45.50 across all ten benchmarks, reusable across agents but not free (Table 6). A failure-seeking subset is intentionally biased and must not be used for an unbiased success-rate estimate; Random significantly beats all six difficulty methods for that objective (§5.1). BQAA should version the selection strategy and seed, report execution plus judge cost, and publish separate “representative estimate” and “failure discovery” panels with full-suite runs reserved for calibration.
