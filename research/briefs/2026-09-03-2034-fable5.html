2. From Question to Evidence: A Small Analytical Algebra for Governed Data Analysis (listed as "MasterControl Seventeen Every Time"; submitted Sep 2, cs.AI; MasterControl AI Lab)

The paper opens with a question that should be boring, "how many approved temperature-excursion deviations occurred last week," and observes that producing 17 is easy while preserving what 17 means is not: calendar week versus trailing seven days, a controlled category versus free-text similarity, deviation records versus observation rows, voided records in or out. It therefore defines the answer as a contract R = (m, v, E), where m is the accepted governed meaning, v the value or table, and E role-labeled supporting evidence, with a separate execution certificate recording data snapshot, policy version, program version, kernel versions and serialization rules. The architecture lets a language model interpret the user's wording into a canonical request but never choose the analytical method; a deterministic policy maps the request to a pre-written, reviewed program over eight typed operation families (RESTRICT, SHAPE, RELATE, AGGREGATE, COMPARE, WINDOW, RANK, SIMILAR), or returns clarify/reject when no rule covers it. Four theorems establish that the relational core is complete for finite-domain first-order specifications, that the extended language is complete for the stated analytical class, that a supported policy family can be executed exactly without model-composed programs, and that fixed governed state gives evidence-preserving replay, with the explicit remark that "operational telemetry such as latency and run identifiers is not part of analytical equality." The experiment runs 440 episodes over eleven task types on a synthetic quality-manufacturing dataset: Qwen3-8B, Ministral-3-8B and Granite-4.1-8B plan and write SQL at runtime with a tool budget of eight, versus Qwen3-8B interpreting intent only with policy executing the program. The primary metric is exact contract accuracy across the development snapshot and four held-out variants (multiplicity and absence, time boundaries, empty populations and zero denominators, ties, scores and nulls), so a procedure had to implement the function, not reproduce one visible number. Result: 0 of 330 runtime episodes were exact (220 rejected, 55 exhausted the budget, 55 returned a final program), against 110 of 110 for policy execution; five Ministral episodes returned the right count on the visible snapshot but labeled evidence with the wrong role and used a grouped aggregate that returned no row for an empty population. The authors are careful that this is configuration-specific (small models, no native function calling, a fixed budget) and not a claim that agents cannot do analysis, and they add that at temperature zero several returned procedures were repeatable across runs and wrong: "repeatability and correctness are different properties."

TL;DR for BQAA. Every conversational-analytics or data agent BQAA will observe is the runtime-planning side of this table, and the paper's failure mode, correct scalar with the wrong population, is invisible to an event record that stores the answer and the SQL text alone. Schema note: a data-agent answer event should be able to carry an evidence reference (query hash, evidence-role row counts, or the qualifying record ids) and certificate fields (data snapshot or table version, policy or prompt version), so that "right number, wrong records" is a join against ground truth rather than a code review. Second note, for the metrics vocabulary: run-to-run stability at temperature zero must not be surfaced as a quality signal, because this paper's repeatable procedures were the wrong ones.

3. It's the Problem, Not the Path: Budget and Difficulty Confounds in LLM Reasoning Trajectories (submitted Sep 3, cs.LG; cross-listed to cs.AI in the Friday batch)

Two beliefs about reasoning traces are tested with the control each lacks: that traces contain breakthrough moments, and that a run's fate is legible from its first tokens. For the first, the author builds a restart-controlled truncation probe on 178 problem-model cells (89 MATH problems, Gemma-4 E4B and Ministral-3 3B in 4-bit, 16K-token instrumented traces) and compares per-anchor continuation solve rates against from-scratch restart curves at matched total generated-token budget; exactly 1 of 178 cells survives as prefix-limited, most apparent breakthroughs are the point where the remaining work began to fit the budget, and one model's unsolved problems dissolve with budget (0% to 79%) while the other's stay flat, which is a diagnostic for compute-starved versus capability-limited failures. The second result is the one that transfers to analytics. Pooled AUROC decomposes into a within-problem and a between-problem term, every question-only predictor has within-problem AUROC of exactly one half, and with one attempt per problem the within term has zero weight, so a pooled AUROC on outcomes cannot by itself attribute any value to information in the attempt. On 192,315 public DeepSeek-R1 generations a trace-blind proxy, the leave-one-out pass rate of the problem's other attempts, reaches AUROC 0.873, inside the 0.79 to 0.95 range that hidden-state probe papers report without a question-only baseline; on GPQA-diamond samples it reaches 0.917. A faithful reconstruction of the closest published early-window positive recovers pooled AUROC 0.849 at four tokens and sits at 0.496 within problem across ten anchors, and a pre-registered test on the author's own cohort finds early-window dynamics add +0.026 AUROC over a difficulty-only baseline with a confidence interval straddling zero. The paper's own limits are stated plainly: two small quantized models, mathematics-only dissection, absence of detected gain rather than demonstrated equivalence, and a small within-attempt residual concentrated in three low-failure problems where failing samples were four to six times shorter than successes. Every decision rule was frozen before outcomes were observed, gate failures included.

TL;DR for BQAA. Any dashboard, eval or example query that claims to predict session or task failure from early events (first tool call, first latency, first N tokens) must be reported against a task-only baseline (the historical pass rate for the same task or task cluster) and evaluated within task; otherwise it is a difficulty detector wearing an early-warning label, and the numbers will look excellent. Schema note: this requires a stable task identifier or task-cluster key on session rows and more than one attempt per task in the analyzed window; without a task key the within-task term cannot be computed at all. The restart-versus-continue framing also gives agent retry analysis a clean question the event table can already answer: at matched token budget, did continuing the trajectory beat a fresh start.

4. Abs-read, not carded

  • DRACO: Fine-Grained Credit Assignment with Dynamic Rubrics for Long-Horizon Agent Training (introduction and method read in full): an RL method for the outcome-blind setting, so not a card, but the eval-record shape inside it is worth one line. The frozen judge returns, per criterion, pass, fail or not-applicable plus a justification and the steps responsible; a step's quality is the pass fraction of the criteria that cite it. That is a rubric-verdict row with a repeated step-citation field, which is another citation for the failure-step pointer reserve from 1001.
  • Privacy-Preserving Topology-Guided Safety for LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems via Federated Graph Learning: trains a graph detector on judge-labeled episode graphs of inter-agent communication and federates across operators because no silo sees the full attack distribution. Safety method; the input is the multi-agent message graph BQAA already records.
  • The Civilization Framework: a "temporal-weight effect" where an upstream claim arriving first captures 54.2% of answers versus 31.6% when it arrives after the receiver has sealed its own answer; the registration classifies the round as inconclusive because a tool-use check failed its call-budget condition. Protocol paper with an honest preregistration footnote.
  • PatchBench: agents pass proof-of-concept validation by patching on the crash stack trace rather than the root cause, and PoC-only validation inflates solve rates 1.83x across 11 agents. Benchmark design; the lesson is the same as item 2, a passing scalar with the wrong mechanism.
  • Counterexamples as Feedback for Agent Self-Correction (turns-to-success as the metric), When Users Don't Ask (conversational memory retrieval benchmark), Do GUI Agents Know When Not to Act?, ESPO, Caught in the Story, Reducing Catastrophic Risk from AI with Systematic Monitoring, Value-Preserving Architectures, Environment Evolution for Terminal Agents: benchmarks, prompt optimization, a policy framework, an architecture essay and training-environment synthesis. None touches the event record.
