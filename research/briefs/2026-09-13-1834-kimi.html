1. NEW rank: agent self-reports capture ~9% of what the agent did — and lie hardest exactly when execution went off-plan

Plans They Abandon, Reports They Author: The Narrative Layer of Autonomous Agents, Obada Kraishan, Kulsawasd Jitkajornwanich (Texas Tech, College of Media and Communication; cs.HC). v1 2026-09-10 21:00:59Z, announced in the Sunday 13 Sep cycle (in-window). Full text read including limitations. (PDF)

The first large in-the-wild measurement of the gap between what coding agents did and what their closing summaries say. Corpus: the public SWE-chat dataset (arXiv:2604.20779) — 5,851 real developer sessions, 355,942 tool calls, 205 repos (82.9% Claude Code, 10.6% OpenCode, 3.6% Codex, 1.0% Gemini CLI). Findings, precisely scoped:

  • Omission: a self-report referenced 9.4% of executed actions — mean omission 0.906, 95% CI [0.903, 0.910], n=5,115 (pack correction: the paper's computed figure is 9.4%, "roughly one action in eleven"; the pack's 9.1% is 1/11 arithmetic).
  • Recoverability: reconstructing the action-type sequence from the report alone yields F1 = 0.202 (Mdn 0.143) — pack correction: the "reader" is a pinned LM probe (claude-haiku-4-5), not a human reader study; no human study exists.
  • Plan drift (the novel result): reports track the log when the plan was followed, but as execution diverges from the stated plan the report drifts back toward the plan — repair index positive in 38.7% of sessions, divergence→repair b=0.771 [0.648, 0.895], ρ=.548. The summary is least faithful precisely when the session is most worth auditing.
  • Methodological honesty worth emulating: two LM-assisted measurement steps were hand-validated; the plan extractor passed (F1 0.814) and the claim adjudicator failed (κ=.185) — so the authors quarantined the commission-rate finding as a descriptive lower bound and drew no inference from it.

Mandatory qualifications: omission was not strictly invariant to human correction (sessions later modified by developers had ~1.6pp higher omission, p<.001 — the pack's "invariant" compresses several tests). No code/data release yet (and §1 vs §4 contradict each other about whether it exists). Extractor/adjudicator/probe are all Claude-family on a mostly-Claude corpus (self-preference risk, authors-flagged). Divergence/repair analyses run on the 2,646/5,851 sessions with a recoverable plan — non-random. Two-author HCI paper; stats hygiene (cluster-robust SEs, Holm correction, bootstrap CIs, fixed seed) is nonetheless above typical ML-preprint standard.

BQAA/ADK application: the empirical foundation for the rule "never evaluate from the agent's own summary." Concrete imports: claim-to-tool-call linking in eval dashboards; special handling for outcome claims ("all tests pass") with no verifying action in the log; treating any LLM-adjudicated metric as suspect until hand-validated (theirs failed at κ=.185 — and they published the failure).

2. NEW rank: Berkeley Sky's Two-Gap Framework — why a green eval can never certify production behavior

Reality Is the Final Verifier: On Two Key Gaps in Agentic Software Engineering, Alexander Krentsel, Shubham Agarwal, Mert Cemri, Shu Liu (equal contribution), Sidharth Sankhe, Ziming Mao, Matei Zaharia, Ion Stoica (UC Berkeley). v1 2026-09-10 17:58:48Z, announced in the Sunday cycle (in-window). Full text read including appendices. (PDF)

A framework/position paper — zero experiments, carded explicitly as such — that gives a precise vocabulary to something this catalog keeps rediscovering piecemeal. The Requirement Gap (requirements R incompletely approximate stakeholder intent I) and the Model Gap (the environment/evaluator model M approximates the real world W) together explain why even a formal proof that program P satisfies R under M cannot guarantee stakeholders accept the deployed behavior. Reward hacking = false acceptance exploiting gap omissions; hallucination = the agent widening the gaps by fabricating requirements or environment assumptions. Pack corrections: the paper argues this, it does not "prove" it (§3 is informal impossibility argument; the paper itself concedes gaps can be certified closed in bounded domains); and there is a third, internal evaluation gap (§2.4) the pack omits. Prescriptions: an assurance-revision loop (preserve post-deployment evidence → diagnose P/R/M/E/safeguard → revise → rerun → bounded redeploy), versioned runtime monitors as executable assumptions backed by independent telemetry, and evaluation designed as a fidelity/cost cascade reporting a speed–cost–assurance Pareto frontier — with deliberately hidden requirement omissions and drifted workloads so the workflow, not the task author, finds the gaps.

Mandatory qualifications: no empirical content at all — Table 2's "illustrative cases" are handpicked prior incidents (self-citation cluster included); the framework synthesizes known ideas (Goodhart, incomplete contracts, assurance cases) and says so; v1 preprint, three days old. (HTML table bodies were extraction-invisible; only prose and captions verified.)

BQAA/ADK application: the mapping is one-to-one: a golden-query test set is R, a sampled-dataset/synthetic workload harness is M, an LLM-judge or result comparator is E — and passing E under M cannot certify correctness in production BigQuery. Adopt the vocabulary in eval reports; budget human judgment against the requirement gap and faithful (expensive) evaluation against the model gap as the two named bottlenecks.

3. NEW rank: harness vs model, finally isolated — no average native-harness edge, a post-hoc sign flip, and a cost analysis that ate itself

Harness or Model? Isolating the Harness Effect in Agentic Coding with a Contamination-Controlled Private Suite, Mohsen Arjmandi (single author, no affiliation visible). v1 2026-09-08 21:24:40Z, announced in the Sunday cycle (in-window). Full text read including appendices. (PDF)

Holds the model fixed and swaps only the harness — claude-agent-sdk vs deepagents on Opus 4.8, openai-codex SDK vs deepagents on GPT-5.5 — over 80 paired tasks from a private, contamination-controlled 256-task suite (179 repo tasks from 4 private codebases + 77 post-cutoff contest tasks; cutoff registry frozen before any scored run; 792/800 runs graded by an isolated oracle). Verified findings:

  • No average harness effect: −1.25pp for Opus 4.8 (48.8% vs 50.0%, task-bootstrap 95% CI [−10.0, +7.5]) and +1.25pp for GPT-5.5 (55.6% vs 54.4%, CI [−4.4, +6.9]).
  • Task-strata divergence: native trails by 9.0pp on 61 repo tasks but leads by 23.7pp on 19 contest tasks (label-permutation p=0.003) — pack omission: the partition was chosen after seeing the data; the paper itself labels p=0.003 "evidence for a designed test rather than a result of one," and the multiplicity family was changed post-review (q 0.10 → 0.013). Quote this split only with its post-hoc label.
  • Completion ≠ correctness: 22 of 81 wall-clock-cancelled runs had already produced a passing patch.
  • Cost: neutral harness 1.63× per solved task on Opus at list prices (1.34× at SDK-reported cost), 1.18× on GPT-5.5 — pack omission: 58 Anthropic runs left no usage record, and allocating that ~$288 residual moves the Opus ratio across 0.7–2.3, so the billed ordering is unresolved.
  • The telemetry lesson: this v1 is itself a corrected revision — the August manuscript's cost conclusions were withdrawn after a cache-token double-count (SDKs disagree on whether reported input tokens include cache; a normalizer assuming one convention silently double-counted the other), caught only by reconciling raw per-turn events against billed spend.

Mandatory qualifications: single-author unaffiliated preprint; benchmark permanently private (unverifiable task quality); 80-task pool oversamples screening-discordant tasks (30% vs 9.4% of suite) so averages are selection-conditional; Codex cell ran in 4 GiB VMs vs 8 GiB elsewhere (deployed-configuration contrast, not pure harness code); bootstrap CIs are 11–17pp wide; 13 unexplained runner deaths.

BQAA/ADK application: three portable rules — (1) "did it finish" and "was it right" must be separate telemetry endpoints (27% of cancelled runs were secretly passes); (2) never compare vendor-SDK cost fields without reconciling against billed spend — token-field semantics differ by vendor and will silently corrupt the analysis; (3) when comparing SQL-agent scaffolds, freeze the model and treat the harness as the only variable, and expect workload-strata interactions rather than a single average effect.

4. NEW rank: GAUGE — LLM-judge satisfaction is decorrelated from task success, and judge error concentrates exactly on close calls

GAUGE: When Not to Trust LLM-as-a-Judge in User-Simulated Evaluation of Task-Oriented Agents, Umesh Bodhwani, Thanh Tran, Kai Wei (Amazon). v1 2026-09-10 20:29:28Z, announced in the Sunday cycle (in-window). Full text read including appendices. (PDF)

Tests the standard offline promotion gate — persona-driven LLM user simulator + LLM-as-a-judge transcript scoring — across 25 agent configurations from six providers on τ²-bench (114 retail + 50 airline tasks, ~3,700 transcripts) with SimulatorArena as human-grounded replication, separating ranking validity from construct validity. Verified findings:

  • 57.5% of conversations rated "satisfied" by a blind human panel failed the underlying task (23/40, p=2×10⁻⁷ against a 20% false-accept ceiling) — statistically indistinguishable from the sample's own 57.3% base failure rate: satisfaction is uninformative, not anti-correlated.
  • Ranking holds globally but collapses locally: full-ladder ρ=0.94, yet decision-disagreement on near-equal pairs (|ΔR|<0.1) is 31% vs 0.9% on wide pairs — the comparisons promotion gates exist to decide are exactly where the judge fails.
  • Remedy, correctly scoped: a "calibrate-then-trust" protocol plus a judge-free completion bit as a zero-cost truncation tripwire (ρ=0.87 vs gate 0.80 on the degradation set) — pack omission: the bit detects truncation only (3.5% of grid failures; 96.5% are semantic), overall recall 0.035, and it collapses to ρ=0.51 on the natural six-provider grid.

Mandatory qualifications: human panel is 3 lay annotators × 150 transcripts (the 57.5% rests on 40 satisfied conversations); the 31% carries 95% CI [11.6, 50.0]; τ²-bench harness modified (Bedrock swap, tolerant parser — authors state results are not leaderboard-comparable); judge self-preference measured at +0.75/7; no code release.

BQAA/ADK application: direct evidence for anchoring SQL-agent gates on verifiable oracles (query-result/DB-state checks — cheap in BigQuery) and treating judge scores as ranking-only inside a calibrated operating region. Add a zero-cost completion-bit tripwire for truncation regressions in CI, and re-audit the gate on any judge/simulator/candidate-pool change rather than on a fixed schedule.

5. NEW rank: pre-action verification — deterministic gates catch 95.8% of invalid shell commands, and location-anchored edits fail silently 99.1% of the time

Look Before You Leap: Pre-Action Verification for LLM Agents, Asaad Althoubi (single author). v1 2026-08-09 20:05:03Z, announced in the Sunday 13 Sep cycle after a moderation hold (in-window by announcement). Full text read. (PDF)

Measures cheap, deterministic, pre-execution guards against construction-defined ground truth: 9,930 shell commands across 482 tools (1,986 real from the tldr corpus + 4× mutation-generated invalids, each invalidity confirmed by independent behavioral oracles — anti-circular design) and 640 code edits over 224 Python files (23,040 perturbed trials). Verified findings: static verification catches 95.8% of invalid commands at 10.0% FPR (tool-clustered bootstrap CIs [0.944, 0.971] / [0.078, 0.122]); the syntax + binary-existence core has 0% false positives at 50% recall; content-anchored edit formats (search/replace, unified diff) fail cleanly while location-anchored formats fail silently — a one-line shift silently corrupts 99.1% of line-number edits, and function-name edits hit the wrong function 12.7% even unperturbed (duplicate-name collisions — a nuance the pack omits). A refuse-when-unsure anchor-and-verify applier records 1 silent misapplication in 8,320 trials (0.01%).

Mandatory qualifications: code/data "will-be-released" — nothing is actually available; error distribution is synthetic (real-model probing is 42 commands + 9 edits, one model, FP-side only); the 0.01% safety figure costs applicability (0.51 vs 0.98 on perturbed single-hunk edits), and aggressive fuzzy matching has a 39% silent rate on multi-hunk edits — the pack skipped both; no LLM-critic baseline (argued qualitatively); one Linux host, Python only.

BQAA/ADK application: the success / clean-failure / silent-failure taxonomy with ground truth fixed before execution is the right frame for SQL-agent actions: a wrong-but-valid query is exactly a silent failure no error telemetry catches. Recipe import: an oracle-exact deterministic pre-action gate (BigQuery dry-run + column-existence/constraint checks as the zero-FP core) plus abstain-and-refuse on ambiguity, at millisecond cost and no model call. And a concrete tooling bias: prefer content-anchored action representations over positional ones wherever agents emit edits.

6. NEW rank: GenV — a solver verdict is provably chance-level on faithful-looking wrong encodings; a generative verifier recovers ~0.96 AUROC

Beyond Solver Verdicts: Generative Reward Models for Autoformalization, Vikash Singh, Debargha Ganguly, Aman Goel, Ali Torkamani, Xiaoxue Han, Joseph Lilien, Ferhat Erata, Vipin Chaudhary (AWS + Case Western Reserve; intern-led). Pack corrections: v1 is 2026-09-10 04:47:55Z (not 17:58:32Z) and a v2 exists (2026-09-11 17:19:24Z) that the packs' "v2 404" probe missed. Announced in the Friday cycle (in-window since pre-1234; held across six packs for full-text adjudication, completed tonight). Full v1 text read. (PDF)

Formalizes Verdict-Preserving Unfaithfulness (VPU): a syntactically valid encoding that executes and matches the expected solver verdict while not being Z3-equivalent to the reference. Proposition 1: on verdict-matched pairs, any score that is a function only of the binary verdict ties every pair → AUROC 0.5 (a narrow information limit, honestly scoped — not an impossibility result for source/trace-inspecting verifiers). The fix, GenV, distills an offline Z3-equivalence oracle into a reference-free, single-forward-pass continuous score (renormalized P(Yes)/(P(Yes)+P(No)) from a frozen 27B vocab head, LoRA-only SFT). Verified numbers with corrections:

  • 0.961 AUROC for GenV+HN on the 950-row benchmark — pack omission: 298/950 rows share normalized source text with training items (disclosed by the authors); the text-disjoint 652-row subset scores 0.955. The headline is the leaky number. Baselines: verdict-only 0.500, PRM 0.756, ORM 0.762, self-consistency 0.863.
  • Zero-shot transfer: 0.964 ProverQA / 0.925 MALLS / 0.915 ProntoQA / 0.842 ProofWriter / 0.830 FOLIO — but 0.642 on LogicNLI; "consistent margin" does not hold across all styles, and OOD sets are synthetic single-edit mutations.
  • +11.3-point downstream gain (0.655 → 0.768) in agentic test-time compute — pack correction: +9.3 comes from Best-of-N itself; GenV-gated escalation adds +1.2 and verifier selection +0.9, so the verifier-specific contribution is ≈ +2.1 points.
  • Mechanistic readout: pack terminology correction — the paper's method is a "decision-projected gradient lens," explicitly distinguished from the logit lens; frozen GenV+HN + lens localizes single-edit error coordinates at 0.821 exact / 0.925 within-one (chance ≈0.14); SAE probe 0.960 AUROC (top feature retrospectively selected; diagnostic, not causal).

Mandatory qualifications: no CIs/seeds (single LoRA runs, ~12 GPU-hours); self-built 950-row benchmark from 197 problems; no code/data release yet ("upon publication"); honest negatives (static re-ranking regresses vs vote@5 at equal budget; threshold calibration degrades OOD; trails an LLM judge 0.679 vs 0.778 on panel-majority intent — it targets strict reference-equivalence, not intent).

BQAA/ADK application: the formal-logic twin of our core failure mode — generated SQL that executes and returns an expected-looking result while being semantically wrong about the question is VPU in BigQuery. Verdict-only (exit-code/result-match) judging is provably chance-level on such pairs. The GenV recipe — distill an exact offline equivalence oracle (SQL-equivalence) into a cheap single-pass verifier and use it to gate test-time compute — maps directly onto adaptive retry/escalation policy in agent analytics.

7. NEW rank: ParaRecover — outcome metrics say 90%, process reality says <60: a process-level benchmark for parallel tool-use recovery

ParaRecover: A Process-Level Benchmark for Error Localization and Recovery in Parallel Tool-Use Agents, Bowen Guan, Zhentao Yin, Yanming Shen (Dalian University of Technology). v1 2026-09-11 02:09:33Z, announced in the Sunday cycle (in-window). Full text read including appendices. (PDF)

Models multi-turn parallel tool use as layer-wise DAG execution and builds a process-level benchmark for error localization and recovery (not just final success): a taxonomy of 14 error types across structural/parameter/tool dimensions, 10,626 instances (LEVEL-1 4,033 + LEVEL-2 6,593; only ~26% from real rollouts — the rest are synthetic error injection), and the SDE rubric (Structural integrity, Diagnostic reasoning, Evolutionary strategy — 15 sub-dimensions, 13 LLM-judged + script-based DAG parsability + an execution-derived efficiency term). Verified findings across 17 models in 6 families (pack said ">10" — understated): best model scores 69.23 (L1) / 66.85 (L2) average while Pass@1 exceeds 88–92% for every model — the paper's core gap: outcome success conceals process unreliability (e.g., one model at 92.2 Pass@1 vs 39.2 evolutionary-strategy score). Implicit errors (redundant/unnecessary calls) are the hardest for every frontier model to self-diagnose. Judge validation is published: human agreement EMA 0.807 / MAE 0.121 on 400 sampled instances, with a cross-judge replication keeping rankings stable; rubric-guided SFT/DPO gains are additionally validated against judge-free execution metrics (invalid-DAG rate 10.25→5.90) — good practice against circularity.

Mandatory qualifications: simulated tool environment (an LLM "tool agent," not live APIs); no CIs/seeds/significance tests; human-mean scores are strikingly low (0.47 overall — strict or noisy golds); outcome metric near-saturated so all discriminative power rests on judged process scores; DAG model excludes loops/conditionals/async; 3-author single-institution team.

BQAA/ADK application: three portable pieces — (1) the "Thought–DAG Consistency" dimension (does the agent's narrated reasoning match the plan it actually executes?) is a ready template for detecting self-report/telemetry divergence in ADK traces; (2) judge-free process metrics — invalid-plan rate, tool-call redundancy, success × (optimal/actual rounds) — map cleanly onto BigQuery job telemetry (failed jobs, redundant queries, replan cycles after dry-run errors); (3) redundant-call blindness argues for observability-side detection rather than trusting agent self-diagnosis.

8. NEW rank: AIM — deterministic index-level memory ACLs for multi-user agents, plus a state-aware scoring trick worth stealing

AIM: A Privacy-Aware Interoperable Memory Framework for Multi-Agent Multi-User LLM Systems, Zachary Johnson, Nigel Boachie Kumankumah, Somya Chatterjee, Tejas Sathyamurthi, Min Chen, Xinyi Alice Li, Xiao Wang, Emily Morgan Gelchie, Jessica Lin, Sadid A. Hasan, Sulaiman Vesal (Microsoft). v1 2026-09-11 01:00:09Z, announced in the Sunday cycle (in-window). Full text read. (PDF)

Enforces deterministic, index-level access control separating private user-scoped memories from public shared memories in multi-agent multi-user systems (server-side visibility predicate at retrieval; writes restricted to the creating user/agent) — explicitly contrasted with probabilistic prompt-level approaches. Ships MUMBench: 672 interactions, 93 users, 4 domains, exercising memory CRUD plus 60 adversarial cross-user probes. Pack correction: the headline combines best-model-per-metric across two different backbones — 96.0% visibility classification is GPT-5.4's number; 58.8% strict and 70.5% state-aware operation accuracy are GPT-5.4-mini's; no single configuration achieves all three (n=3 runs, ± = sample SD). The genuinely portable contribution is the tiered scoring: strict exact-multiset match vs state-aware scoring, which rewrites gold expectations against the actually corrupted store state to isolate each turn's decision quality from cascading earlier errors (58.8% → 70.5% quantifies exactly that gap), vs a judge-LLM upper bound (89.5%).

Mandatory qualifications: self-built GPT-4o-generated benchmark, human-validated by the authors' own team; no external memory-system baseline on MUMBench — the only external comparison (LOCOMO) shows AIM far below Mem0 (0.280 vs 0.671 single-hop judge), on a single run vs Mem0's 10, with LOCOMO's adversarial category dropped (authors disclose all three); retrieval relevance peaks at 45%; the LLM judge has no human-correlation validation; no code/data release despite calling MUMBench "the first public dataset"; rough internal artifacts (a stray "okay" mid-sentence).
