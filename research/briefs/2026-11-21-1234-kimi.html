Academy

NEW

1. RagHarnessDebugger audits agentic RAG repair decisions and replay boundaries (arXiv:2611.22991)

Full PDF read: all 10 pages, including the harness/audit methods, instrumentation contract, experimental setup, results, discussion and references. The system freezes request-response keys, candidate evidence packages, validations and hash-linked event chains; an offline, label-free audit checks validation applicability, conclusion reachability and intervention capability before labels are joined. Across 800 development and 748 replication executions, the blocked-conclusion flag captured 88% / 86% of missed repairs while selecting 15% / 13% of executions for review (Fig. 4); the revised harness cut regressions from 5.3%→0.9% and 4.4%→1.2%, with cost down 30.5% / 32.3% (Table I). The revised-versus-first accuracy gains of +1.25 / +1.07 points have intervals including zero, and the replication contracts' initial packages and answers had been inspected earlier, so this is disjoint-contract replication rather than an untouched holdout (§IV-A, Table I).

2. GrepMem pairs lossless trajectory logs with regex recall (arXiv:2611.25123)

Full PDF read: all 17 pages, including the method, training/evaluation setup, results, limitations, ethics and appendices. GrepMem appends messages and tool outputs to a persistent plain-text log and, after summarization, lets the agent retrieve exact lines with regular expressions. With the same Qwen3.5-9B RL recipe, Table 3 reports 46.9% on BCP-Multi versus 38.1% for SUPO and 97.4% / 95.3% on ContextBench KV/Log Triage versus SUPO's 13.9% / 6.6%; Table 6 shows the ContextBench gain uses 19% more tokens than SUPO. Benefits are not uniform: AMA-Bench stream differences are not statistically significant (Table 5), GrepMem uses 869K versus 377K tokens there, and the evaluation covers controlled benchmarks and one model, with lexical-search and sensitive-history-retention caveats (Limitations, Ethics, Table 6).

3. EverydayBench evaluates live-web completion with constraints, current citations and deliverable rubrics (arXiv:2611.25146)

Full PDF read: all 32 pages, including evaluation validation, results, cost analysis, threshold sensitivity, human validation and failure-taxonomy appendices. The benchmark fixes success criteria—not acceptable answers—across 77 human-authored tasks, then combines deterministic checks, retrieval-time verification of agent-cited pages and weighted component rubrics. Across 1,771 runs and 23 configurations, only 32.30% meet the strict pass rule of overall score ≥85% and every component ≥3; the two leading configurations reach 54.55% (§4.1, Table 3). Fixed outputs fall from 68.69% to 60.95% mean score after 30 days as live evidence changes, while the full evaluator's within-trace SD is 1.41% versus 4.51% for a one-prompt judge (§4.2, Table 2); among frontier runs, “claim says more than the cited page” accounts for 9.1 deductions per 100 components (Table 8).

Six-week EV

  • Versioned telemetry-contract shadow (high EV, 1–3 weeks). Pin OpenInference 0.0.1, map a sample of agent_events to its registry, and report unmapped/renamed attributes, generated-constant drift and old/new rollup parity; do not silently promote a development-stability schema to the production contract.
  • Repair-decision audit (high EV, 2–4 weeks). Add candidate ID, evidence-package hash, validation target, acceptance rule, reachable labels, intervention capability and replay boundary to decision traces. Dashboard repairs, regressions, available-but-missed repairs, flag recall and review budget instead of optimizing final accuracy alone.
  • Lossless-trajectory retrieval shadow (medium-high EV, 3–6 weeks). Keep an access-controlled append-only trajectory log beside compaction and test exact-detail recall, answer quality, retrieval count and total tokens against summary-only operation. Include tasks where summaries should suffice, and require retention/deletion controls before raw-history persistence.
  • Completion-and-grounding evaluation (high EV, 2–5 weeks). Pre-register task success criteria, deterministic constraint checks, citation-grounding results and component rubrics; store the cited-page capture and judge time so live-web drift is separable from agent regression. Report strict completion, overclaim/missing-evidence/delivery failures, runtime and both actual and API-equivalent cost.

TL;DRs for BQAA

  • OpenInference registry: use a pinned machine-readable contract to validate agent telemetry and test rollup parity, while treating its development stability as a rollout boundary.
  • RagHarnessDebugger: trace which candidate a validation actually read and which labels an acceptance rule can reach; final accuracy hides repairs that generation or policy discarded.
  • GrepMem: a searchable raw trajectory can recover details lost by compaction, but measure task mix and total tokens because the advantage disappears on some trajectory-QA workloads.
  • EverydayBench: score completed deliverables against constraints, current evidence and component rubrics; preserve page/time evidence so web drift is not mislabeled as an agent regression.
