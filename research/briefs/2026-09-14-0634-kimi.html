1. NEW rank: DataFlex-RL — 591 runs, 12 seeds, and every "smart data" win vanishes; re-aggregating the same runs flips rankings to ρ = −0.33

DataFlex-RL: An Evaluation Platform for RLVR Data Policies, Mingrui Chen, Hengyi Feng, Meiyi Qiang, Wentao Zhang (PKU). v1 2026-09-05, announced in the Sunday cycle (in-window by announcement). Full text read. (PDF)

The most decision-relevant eval-integrity paper of the batch. 13 data-policy configurations (4 selection, 4 reweighting, 3 mixture, baseline + static control) × 12 matched seeds × 12 benchmarks on Qwen2.5-7B-Base, replicated on Llama-3.1-8B-Base — 591 runs total. Verified findings:

  • Uniform GRPO gains +7.76 points, 95% CI [7.28, 8.25] (Table 2) — but none of the 8 selection/reweighting policies beats uniform: all paired 95% CIs include zero (range −0.90 to +0.08; policy means span 0.97 pt against the 7.76-pt training gain). The three adaptive mixtures likewise all include zero (spread 0.61 pt).
  • Aggregation is a hidden decision: a math-heavy 6-benchmark summary of the same runs ranks the nine policies at Spearman ρ = −0.33 (Kendall τ = −0.28) against the domain-balanced 12-benchmark summary; summaries keeping all 12 benchmarks agree at ρ = 0.88. The flip is driven by dropping the logic domain. Qualifier the packs omit: the −0.33 is on nine Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct configs with 3 seeds, not the primary Base matrix.
  • Their own eval-integrity catch, disclosed: earlier Llama scores (14–16 pts) came from an evaluator that silently scored unsupported math tasks as zero; those runs are excluded. Plus a format-mismatch smoke test and a 13-gram contamination audit.

Mandatory qualifications: RLVR post-training on 7–8B base models, 300 optimizer steps — relevance is methodological, not topical; the null is explicitly scoped (no equivalence claim; longer training/other families untested); the scale/family sweep is 3-seed and descriptive.

BQAA/ADK application: two rules to adopt verbatim. (1) Seed discipline: any "recipe wins" comparison in a BQAA eval dashboard ships with multi-seed paired CIs or is labeled exploratory — tonight's paper is the template for how single-seed wins evaporate. (2) Aggregation registry: the benchmark subset and domain weights behind every headline number are themselves versioned configuration; changing the aggregate is a methodology change, not a re-render. And the silent-zero evaluator is exactly Opik #8233's bug class, seen from the research side.

2. NEW rank: COBRA-Skills — a contextual bandit over skill candidates cuts skill-optimization cost 55–58% and still wins the average

COBRA-Skills: Contextual Bandit-Guided Evolution for Agent Skill Optimization, Xiangyi Wang, Xiang Li, Jie Mao, Zikun Qu, Junfeng Luo, Yao Shu, Bryan Kian Hsiang Low, Zhongxiang Dai (CUHK-Shenzhen lead, with Tianjin U., HKUST(GZ), NUS — pack correction: "NUS/PKU" is wrong; no PKU). v1 2026-09-10, Sunday cycle. Full text read including appendices. (PDF)

Skill libraries for agents are currently optimized by evaluate-everything loops; COBRA instead treats candidate-skill evaluation as a contextual bandit, spending teaching/eval budget only on promising arms, plus an evolutionary mutation step. Verified numbers:

  • Cost cut 55–58% vs SkillOpt at a fixed 50-example optimization set: total optimization cost $54.10 vs $121.02 (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, −55%), $58.46 vs $133.59 (GPT-5.4-Nano, −56%), $38.98 vs $92.14 (Gemma-4-26B, −58%); cost per point of improvement cut 60–69%, driven by 67–80% fewer teaching-model tokens. Costs from fixed OpenRouter list prices.
  • Best average on all 6 benchmarks × 3 models: +13.1 / +26.9 / +22.5 pp over no-skill (73.5 / 56.9 / 68.9 avg) — but mean ± SE over 3 runs only, and it is the average winner, not the per-benchmark winner (SkillOpt takes individual cells).
  • Harness check (narrow): one model under Claude Code vs Codex harnesses — best average under both, but the Codex margin is ~1.3 pp, within noise at SE ±1–3. "Harness-robust" is one-model-two-harness evidence only.
  • Ablations support the mechanism (w/o bandit −2.2, w/o evolution −2.4 pp); self-teaching keeps 73.5→72.5 at ~half cost; cross-model skill transfer positive in 34/36 cells.
  • An eval-integrity data point inside the paper: the authors excluded OfficeQA because they could not reproduce SkillOpt's published results with the released config — disclosed, and worth quoting whenever baselines look suspiciously strong.

Mandatory qualifications: 3 runs, SE-based (no CIs); several per-benchmark deltas within noise; baseline pools/hyperparameters re-tuned by the authors per benchmark (disclosed); sample-efficiency edge uneven (one baseline pool is 53 examples vs COBRA's 50).

BQAA/ADK application: the bandit is the transferable object — budgeted allocation over skill/config candidates instead of uniform grid evaluation is directly applicable to BQAA's own skill/prompt-variant search and to customer-facing "which agent config should I deploy" tooling: report cost-per-point alongside win rate, and treat evaluation budget as a first-class experimental variable.

3. NEW rank: Zipbench — compress 100+ benchmarks to 20–40% of eval cost at Spearman ~0.98, with an honest OOD degradation number

ZipBench: Low-Cost Framework for Compressing Comprehensive Benchmarks of Large Language Models, Zhongzhan Huang, Junxin Li, Guoming Ling, Yupei Lin, Shanshan Zhong, Hefeng Wu (Bosch Research + Sun Yat-sen University — pack correction: "MilkThink Lab" is the GitHub org, not an affiliation). v1 2026-09-11, Sunday cycle. Full text read including proofs. (PDF) EMNLP 2026 Main per the repo; code live (Apache 2.0).

Only 6 real anchor LLMs run each full benchmark; pseudo-logs are synthesized by majority vote over anchor subsets; items get 5-dimensional 2PL-IRT fingerprints, K-means clustering, and a cluster-size-weighted scoring estimator. Verified results: 100+ compact proxies achieving 0.002–0.02 score error and average Spearman 0.98 on held-out LLMs at 20–40% of original evaluation cost (tiny tier: 22.5% of samples, MAE 0.015, SP 0.98; means over 100 seeded runs). Agent coverage is real: SWE-bench Verified (181 test models), Terminal-Bench 2.0 (67), Toolathlon (53), OSWorld-Verified (44), τ³-Bench (7 models each — thin). The honest counterweight: OOD extrapolation degrades to MAE 0.031 (vs tinyBenchmarks/SUBLIME baselines it still wins) — the headline fidelity is in-distribution. Theory (per-model error bound A·ε/4; rank-preservation bound) checks out under the stated IRT idealizations.

Mandatory qualifications: test-model names/versions not yet disclosed (limits independent verification); agent benchmarks compress worse (less redundancy — authors fall back to weaker ratios); continuous metrics are binarized via anchor-calibrated thresholds (real information loss); construction itself costs 6 full-benchmark anchor runs — non-negligible exactly for expensive agent evals; Spearman 0.98 is an average across the zoo.

BQAA/ADK application: a principled recipe for cheap regression loops — anchor models + IRT fingerprints + cluster-weighted scoring could cut BQAA's per-commit agent-eval spend 60–80% while provably preserving rankings. The discipline matters as much as the recipe: quote in-distribution fidelity and OOD degradation side by side, and never let a compact proxy's rank fidelity be read as per-sample signal.

4. NEW rank: LifeMem — workflow-aligned trajectory clustering for lifelong agent memory; how you cluster history measurably changes downstream performance

LifeMem: Enabling Lifelong Experience Reuse for LLM Agents, Yutong Li, Wei Su, Zeming Liu, Wanxiang Che, Heyan Huang, Haifeng Wang, Yuhang Guo (Beijing Institute of Technology + Beihang lead, with HIT and Baidu co-affiliations — pack correction: "HIT/Baidu" misattributes the lead). v1 2026-09-11, Sunday cycle. Full text read. (PDF)

Clusters historical trajectories by workflow structure (MRL-based adaptive splitting; human-rated cluster purity 91%) rather than semantic similarity, and reuses them as lifelong memory across task streams. Verified scale and results: 10 environments, 13,719 tasks (12,489 train + 1,230 test — "over 13k" checks out); average success GPT-4o-mini 28.84 → 44.13, DeepSeek-v3.2-exp 41.14 → 48.02 vs memory-free ReAct, beating Synapse/ExpeL/AutoSkill; the only method with positive average backward transfer; positive zero-shot transfer to FEVER and τ-bench-airline where baselines degrade. Build cost ~$1 per 1k trajectories; +~300 prompt tokens vs Synapse.

Mandatory qualifications: pack correction — the "2,000 annotated trajectories" are 2,310 GPT-4.1-generated ReAct trajectories with human QC only (5 annotators judged >90% of steps reasonable; not human demonstrations). No CIs or multi-seed reporting anywhere; experience extraction is GPT-4.1 and clustering GPT-5-mini (heavy single-vendor stack); per-dataset table cells exist but weren't text-verifiable — quote prose figures only; thin failure-mode discussion at scale.

BQAA/ADK application: for trajectory analytics the finding is that clustering choice is a performance variable: workflow-aligned grouping of historical agent runs measurably changes what reuse delivers, and stream order + retrieval interference are first-order effects — the same interference patterns BQAA trace analytics needs to surface. The MySQL "Database" lifelong environment is mild SQL-agent adjacency.

5. NEW rank: Does Video Memory Use What It Retrieves? — identity-free content recovers ~100% of the "memory benefit"; benchmarked benefit ≠ specificity

Does Video Memory Use What It Retrieves? A Causal Audit of Memory Specificity, Aditi Tiwari, Akshit Bhalla, Darshan Prasad, Heng Ji (UIUC + Adobe Research). v1 2026-09-10, Sunday cycle. Full text read. (PDF) (1834 reserve; full-text adjudication completed this slot.)

A causal audit recipe: read-time memory substitution — replace only the consumed memory value at one read, holding query/locations/weights/trajectory fixed — with a substitution ladder (correct / wrong / identity-free / content-free) separating "generic representation support" from "actual episodic content." Verified: on Ego-Exo4D with a frozen DINO-WM world model (revisit-consistency error 0.851 no-memory → 0.456 correct), identity-free barycenter recovers 99%, identity-free sample 97%, wrong-scene 93% of the benefit; a learned constant only 40%; zero/random controls ~0. Dose-response falls 102% → 1% as injected values leave the training-representation neighborhood; direction rotation collapses 101% → 0%. Counter-regimes the card must carry: TUM recovers only 70% (bootstrap CI [59, 94], two evaluation takes — authors say "suggestive"), and SAM 2 is strongly content-dependent (DAVIS J&F 0.926 → 0.182 under wrong memory).

Mandatory qualifications: headline is model-family-dependent (frozen latent world models on a latent consistency metric — not perceptual quality or downstream control; V-JEPA 2 second-host check shows a near-zero memory gap); intervention covers one read (direct effect); requires memory-read-interface access; WorldMem controls change several context factors at once.

BQAA/ADK application: the substitution ladder is a ready-made audit for any BQAA memory/retrieval component: before crediting retrieval for a gain, run the identity-free control — if content-free structure recovers the benefit, "memory works" is an artifact. Same logic applies to RAG-over-traces features: test whether what was retrieved matters, not just that something was.

6. NEW rank: MetroLLM-Bench — serving config alone fakes a 2.7-point generational regression; judge κ collapses to 0.02 under skew while Gwet AC2 holds

Evaluating Language Models as Transit Kiosk Runtimes (MetroLLM-Bench), Remco Hendriks (sole author). v1 2026-09-09, Sunday cycle. Full text read including appendices. (PDF) Pack correction: the paper's title is not "MetroLLM-Bench" — that is the benchmark's name.

The transit-kiosk domain is narrow; the eval engineering is the card. 955 cases / 6 metro systems / 11 categories with a two-tier split: Tier 1 = 14 deterministic components (doubling as PEFT reward), Tier 2 = 8 semantic components, 6 of 8 judged by a pinned Claude Haiku 4.5. Verified:

  • Serving config as measured confound (the quotable finding): an apparent Qwen3.5→3.8 regression of 3.60 Tier-1 points under one uniform greedy/4096-token config shrinks to 0.07–0.88 after raising the output budget (+1.72) and applying vendor-recommended sampling (+1.00). "A leaderboard that imposes one config converts configuration mismatch into apparent capability differences."
  • Judge calibration discipline: judge-vs-author κw = 0.53, judge-vs-second-blind-rater κw = 0.02 (prevalence artifact), human-human 0.25 — while Gwet AC2 stays 0.81–0.89 for all pairings. Report prevalence-robust agreement or the audit is noise.
  • Small-PEFT parity claims are honestly bounded: 4B student 91.32 vs GPT-5.6 90.63/90.00 on held-out Tier 1, but the full-matrix tie is +0.01 [−0.93, +0.94] and the student trails ~1 composite point; every held-out pairwise CI includes zero (n=238 underpowered by the authors' own statement).

Mandatory qualifications: single-author non-peer-reviewed preprint; cases public since June 2026 (contamination caveat for post-cutoff models); best-config leaderboard rows selected on the same held-out cases they are ranked on (disclosed optimism); 2B seed spread 3.96 points; headline "beats GPT-5.6" is 0.7 points — below the paper's own meaningful-difference threshold.

BQAA/ADK application: two standing rules get teeth tonight — (1) serving configuration is first-class telemetry: pin and log decode config on every BQAA eval run, and treat any cross-config comparison as confounded by default; (2) judge audits report Gwet AC2 (or equivalent prevalence-robust agreement) alongside κ, against blind human raters — a 0.53 κ can be 0.02 on a different rater split for purely distributional reasons.

7. NEW rank: Benchmark Radar — a living registry whose own census says only ~10% of scored benchmark records support a clean percentage comparison

Benchmark Radar: A Living Database and Search Engine for AI Benchmarks and Evaluation, Koutian Wu, Junjie Zhou, Ergan Shang, Jiayu Wang, Pengqian Han, Junkai Wang, Wanghan Xu (HDU/CMU/XJTU/Auckland/Tsinghua/SJTU). v1 2026-09-10, Sunday cycle. Full text read. (PDF) Carded explicitly as a resource card, not a methods card.

The registry: 1,283 source records across 4 sources (LLM Stats 687, OpenCompass 461, Artificial Analysis 25, model reports 110), 12,916 numeric score observations on 790 records, daily discovery from 37 sources, web + offline CLI + an agent-consumable "Skill." The value for this catalog is the census: only 82 of 790 scored records (~10%) declare a percentage unit with known score direction and values in 0–100 — catalog-wide headroom/saturation stats are not legitimately computable; 12,594 score observations carry model-announcement dates vs 322 document-publication dates — neither is an evaluation date; of 345 agentic records only 128 are primarily classified "Agentic & Tool Use" (117 hide under Coding).

Mandatory qualifications: no retrieval-quality evaluation (precision/time-saved explicitly unmeasured; the authors admit their own related-work search missed two papers); source records ≠ distinct benchmarks (cross-source duplicates retained); one-snapshot census (cutoff 2026-09-07); content CC BY-NC-SA.

BQAA/ADK application: two uses — (1) a queryable sourcing pool of 345 agentic benchmark records when BQAA shops for new evals; (2) the census numbers are the hygiene bar for any BQAA score-aggregation feature: enforce unit + direction + date-basis metadata at ingest, because ~90% of real-world score records won't have them, and treat "date on a score" as model-announcement date unless proven otherwise.
