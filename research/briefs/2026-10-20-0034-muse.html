  1. A passing trace is not a clean trace (Academy NEW, PDF-verified). MASIF (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25852) annotates "implicit failures" — local errors that are corrected, bypassed, or carried forward without deciding the outcome — across 1,828 multi-agent traces in nine configurations (Table 1). They occur in 54.3% of successful and 75.2% of failed traces (§2.2), and repairing one in counterfactual replay rescues failures by up to +46.2pp over no-op replay (Table 4). For BQAA: outcome labels alone under-report process risk; the trace schema needs first-class local-error and absorption (self-correction, workaround, peer repair, orchestrator intervention, unresolved propagation) fields, or "successful" sessions will keep hiding the failures that predict the next incident.
  2. Move evidence admission, grading, and verdict licensing out of the model and into deterministic harness code (Academy NEW, PDF-verified). AgInt (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23996) has the model propose typed records while the harness admits, grades, persists, and only then licenses an answer. With GLM-5, reject-class F1 rises 0.256 → 0.543 on SWE-bench-Live and wasted inspected lines fall 31.5 → 11.7 on GitBug-Java (Tables 1–2); the matched-template ablation (§4.4) shows the persistent scaffold carries fault localization while the certificate body + gate carry patch-acceptance calibration. For ADK/BQAA: a certificate that outlives the session and can be rechecked without rerunning the model is a stronger analytics artifact than a free-form trajectory.
  3. Audit tool traces per-tool, then prove dependence by redaction — concatenated-trace screening misses the shortcut (Academy NEW, PDF-verified). Auditing Tool Traces (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25540) shows a tool-augmented model at AUROC 0.8136 collapsing to 0.4972 (chance) when one identifier class is redacted, while a control redaction leaves it at 0.8152 (Table 1); full-trace screening stayed near chance (0.4876) while the single carrier tool scored 0.6365 (§3.1, Figure 1). For BQAA evals: never score "the trace" as one blob; keep per-tool provenance so a leaked identifier in one serialized result cannot masquerade as reasoning.

The most material is Inspect AI 0.3.282's breaking per-agent tool-state scopes, filed as Progress. The LiteLLM gateway behavior changes an extra collector admitted are unreleased merges — NOTE at most under the product-level rule, and I decline them as ranks (see Industry).

NEW — MASIF: Implicit Failures for Process Observability in Multi-Agent Systems

PDF extracted in full; core sections read; cited numbers verified against the full text. Huang, Narechania & Sinha study "implicit failures" (IFs): local execution errors that are corrected, bypassed, or carried forward without individually determining the final outcome. Corpus: 1,828 traces across nine MAS–benchmark configurations (Table 1; Magentic-One/GAIA, WebWalker, MrlX, AG2 MathChat, ChatDev, MetaGPT, HyperAgent/SWE-Bench, among others), annotated step-by-step by a Gemini 3.1 Pro judge with human audit (87.7% of jointly reviewed IFs retained by both annotators; §2.1) and cross-judge checks (GLM-5.2, Claude Opus 4.8, DeepSeek V4 Pro). Headline prevalence (§2.2): at least one IF in 140/258 successful traces (54.3%) and 1,181/1,570 failed traces (75.2%) — IF presence does not collapse to the outcome label, and 24.8% of failures contain none. Error-type taxonomy (Table 2): failed tool use 24.5% and wrong action 23.0% dominate, then unnecessary action 16.4% and hallucination 15.3%. Absorption taxonomy (Table 3, 3,044 IFs): trivial issue 36.6%, self-correction 20.3%, unresolved propagation 17.6%, self-workaround 14.3%, orchestrator intervention 9.8%, peer repair 1.4%; among informatively labeled IFs, 27.8% propagate unresolved. Counterfactual replay (Table 4, §2.4): locally repairing a single IF raises failure-rescue over matched no-op replay by +46.2pp (MetaGPT/MMLU), +41.0pp (MathChat/MMLU), +27.8pp (ChatDev/MMLU), +23.0pp (MathChat/OlympiadBench), with 95% bootstrap CIs excluding zero in those four; three configurations show no clear gain (one negative point estimate, MrlX −3.7pp). Downstream (§3): an IF-risk scorer beats three process reward models at best-of-N trace selection in all nine settings (Table 5; e.g. MagenticOne/GAIA 25.0–26.5% vs PRM 18.2–21.2%), and IF-GRPO improves accuracy in six of seven configurations while sometimes increasing per-task IF burden (Table 6) — outcome and process metrics genuinely diverge. Limitations (paper's own): LLM-judge annotation completeness is hard to establish (human audit found 21 missed IFs), replay repairs one IF at a time, and IF-GRPO uses one backbone with a stronger frozen judge. Read: the measurement contribution (prevalence + taxonomies + replay) is the durable part; the training gains are suggestive, not settled. BQAA implication: "trace succeeded" is a weak health signal — schema for local error type, absorbing agent/mechanism, and unresolved propagation, or the majority-of-successes error load stays invisible.

NEW — Agentic Interpretation: Harness-Governed Evidence Construction for Software Agents

PDF extracted in full; method, results tables and threats-to-validity read; cited numbers verified against the full text. He et al. (UC Davis / AWS AI Labs) propose AgInt: the model proposes typed records for a task-specific certificate; a deterministic harness admits or refuses each proposal (Step), grades its evidence, persists admitted records append-only (a "grounding lattice" ordered by inclusion, §3), reports unresolved obligations, and licenses a verdict only when obligations are met (Close). The model's prose can neither grade its own evidence nor authorize its own answer; the sealed certificate can be rechecked by a third party without rerunning the model. Evaluation (four models; execution-free patch acceptance on SWE-bench-Live + DeepSWE, fault localization on Defects4J + GitBug-Java, then execution-licensed bug witnessing): AgInt leads a plain agent and Agentic Code Reasoning on every reported patch-acceptance metric (Table 1) — for GLM-5, reject-class F1 0.256 → 0.543 on SWE-bench-Live, patch accuracy +8 points; for the weakest baseline (Qwen3-Next-80B) reject F1 rises 0.032 → 0.496. Fault localization (Table 2): Defects4J MRR 0.323 → 0.633 (GLM-5), All@1 0.140 → 0.400, GitBug-Java wasted inspected lines 31.5 → 11.7. The matched-template ablation (§4.4, Figure 5) is the honest core: keeping acr's byte-identical certificate body with only AgInt's persistent loop nearly reproduces the fault-localization gain (wasted@5 71.0 → 19.8 vs 17.2 full) but not patch acceptance (F1 0.268 → 0.301 vs 0.383 full) — scaffold carries localization, certificate + gate carry calibration. Bug witnessing (Table 3): harness-reproduced witnesses for 293/475 null-dereference candidates vs 253 for a matched shell agent (resource leaks 62/117 vs 58, within noise), at 15.9 vs 24.5 executions and $3.66 vs $8.83 per candidate; on 142 matched Juliet pairs every faulty site is witnessed and no patched site is (Table 4). Threats (§4.6, paper's own): baselines are single runs (no CIs on between-method differences — read tables as descriptive rankings), DeepSWE's 200 rollouts come from 73 tasks, and the execution-free "cited" grade checks only that a reference resolves, not that it entails the claim. BQAA/ADK implication: admission/grading/licensing as deterministic harness events produce an auditable, replayable evidence record — a concrete schema pattern (proposed → admitted/refused → grade → obligation state → licensed verdict) that free-form trajectory logging cannot reconstruct after the fact.

NEW — Auditing Tool Traces in Agentic LLMs: Source Localization and Counterfactual Dependence Testing

PDF extracted in full; method, case study, Table 1 and limitations read; cited numbers verified against the full text. Liu et al. (UC Irvine / NEC Labs America; NeurIPS 2026 AgenticLS workshop) introduce a two-stage audit for hidden label shortcuts in serialized tool results. Stage 1, Per-Tool TF-IDF Decomposition (PT2D), fits a diagnostic classifier per tool section instead of on the concatenated trace. Stage 2, Token-Class Redaction, removes the localized token class everywhere and re-evaluates the same frozen LLM. Case study: TCR–pMHC binding with a fine-tuned Qwen3-4B over five-tool traces (4,898 peptide-disjoint test examples; §3). The model scores AUROC 0.8136 vs a 0.4928 no-evidence baseline — apparently evidence-grounded generalization. The audit overturns it: full-trace PT2D stays near chance (0.4818–0.4876 across feature sets) while Tool 3 alone reaches 0.6365 with character features (§3.1, Figure 1), localized to PDB-identifier fragments (a two-digit prefix lookup alone: AUROC 0.6967); appending the other tools dilutes the signal monotonically to chance (Figure 2), which is why whole-trace screening misses it. Redaction (Table 1): removing PDB IDs drops the frozen model to 0.4972 (Δ +0.3163) — chance — while redacting peptide sequences as a control leaves 0.8152 (Δ −0.0017). Limitations (Appendix A, paper's own): single benchmark, one 4B backbone, one tool suite; the audit is post-hoc (detects, does not prevent), and redaction can remove correlated signal. The domain is biology; the failure mode is not — any serialized tool result carrying dataset IDs, version strings, file paths, or template artifacts can leak the label. BQAA implication: keep per-tool result boundaries and provenance in the event model, and treat "model improved with tools" as unproven until a per-source localization + counterfactual removal check has run; concatenated-trace evals will certify shortcuts as reasoning.
