1. SkillCFI enforces skill control flow using trusted tool-boundary evidence (arXiv:2611.24185)

Full PDF read: all 21 pages, including methods, results, §7 limitations, references and Appendices A–F (Tables 1–6, Figures 1–10). SkillCFI confines LLM interpretation to an offline propose-and-check compilation step. The resulting executable SkillCFG and the agent's instructions share one interface; at runtime, a deterministic monitor advances workflow state only from correlated tool results and user decisions, refuses unmediated scripts and returns the next legal steps (§§4–5). Its guarantee is for the compiled contract under complete mediation, truthful correlation, trusted helpers, protected state and verified activation (M1–M5), not for semantic content quality.

Across the eight self-written skills' 770-session main cohort, all 240 in-vocabulary violation targets were blocked before dispatch, while both no-gate and allowlist conditions let all 80 corresponding targets execute (§6.3). Next-step feedback raised strict post-refusal recovery from 17/80 (21%) with a bare refusal to 52/80 (65%); normal outcome completion was 78/80 under SkillCFI versus 79/80 without a gate (Figure 6, §6.4). The gate itself took a median 2.1% of task time, but canonical command rendering made sessions 1.32× as long; a compact form reduced this to 1.08× (95% interval 0.97–1.17) with identical replayed decisions (§6.5, Figure 9).

Adoption is the limiting result: none of 53 public skills was accepted as written; seven became enforceable only after scope-restricted adaptation or porting (§6.2, Table 6). The evaluation uses trusted source skills and helpers, serialized single-skill sessions and direct violation requests. Concurrent dispatch, multi-skill composition, richer predicates and harmful content in an otherwise permitted sequence remain out of scope (§7).

2. ARISE gates self-evolving enterprise-agent memory on held-out accuracy (arXiv:2611.23588)

Full PDF read: all 16 pages, including methods, deployment guidance, limitations, references and Appendices A–B (Tables 1–12). ARISE attributes failed training tasks into scoped micro corrections, consolidates them into at most five macro rules and accepts a rule update only when held-out validation accuracy improves. Deployment model weights, tools and workflow logic remain fixed; micro retrieval has its own tolerance gate (§§3.1–3.6).

Across 14 domains and five models, ARISE-Teacher averages 87.4% on SOP-Bench (+10.9 pp over baseline) and 62.3% on τ²-bench (+5.8 pp), exceeding GEPA by 1.4 pp and 4.5 pp respectively (Table 2). Appendix Table 7 reports paired-bootstrap p-values below .01 for those baseline gains and for Teacher versus GEPA on τ²; the SOP Teacher-GEPA comparison is p=.02. Macro rules carry most of the gain: disabling micro retrieval with accepted rules fixed retains 10.2 pp of the 10.9-point SOP improvement (§4.4, Figure 6).

Teacher uses 4.0% fewer model calls and 5.2% fewer output tokens, with mean per-task inference cost down 1.0% (Table 2, §4.4). That cost excludes embeddings, storage and retrieval; one-time adaptation averages $17.46 per model/domain, equivalent to serving about 322 SOP tasks and excluding final testing and embeddings (Table 4, Appendix B.4). The validation gate tests accuracy only, not safety or policy compliance; the micro gate uses different task sets and therefore does not isolate micro retrieval by itself (§§3.4–3.5). The approach requires verifiable pass/fail outcomes, some model/domain cells regress, and production data governance and memory safety are unevaluated (Limitations).

3. Feedback-free skill rewriting deletes outside-prior knowledge; SkillDiff improves same-batch pass rate (arXiv:2611.25534)

Full PDF read: all 23 pages, including methods, results, references, supplementary Appendices A–E (Tables 1–18) and the Appendix E limitations. The study recursively rewrites expert skill documents for 30 rounds without tasks, runs or judgments, then evaluates the same model with the rewritten skill. Every tested agent loses performance on every benchmark: the body's mean changes at round 30 are −14.6 pp on SkillsBench, −14.7 pp on LogicBench and −11.1 pp on MedCalc-Bench (Figure 1, Appendix Table 4; the abstract rounds the SkillsBench loss to 15 points).

Rewriting preferentially removes content absent from ten skills the model writes from the topic alone. Across models and benchmarks, 23.1% of those outside-prior items survive, versus 84.2% of items stated by all ten self-written skills (§3.2). On SkillsBench, restoring outside-prior items recovers 8.8 pp of the 14.6-point loss, versus 4.4 pp from restoring other lost items (Table 2, §3.3). Repeated runs, alternate judges, independent rewrite chains, deletion controls and a τ²-bench replication support the direction of the effect (Appendices A–B).

SkillDiff marks outside-prior items in place and adds a note asking the agent to carry them into its plan. It improves SkillsBench pass rate by 4.8 pp on average, with gains for all six agents in same-batch comparisons, without changing the original skill text (Table 3). This is not a zero-cost or fully general guarantee: generation and judging require 21 calls per skill/model, tags and the note add 129 words on average (Appendix C.4), item labels are LLM-judged, and no significance estimate is reported for the SkillDiff gain. Only preselected skill documents are studied; skill retrieval/composition and joint document/code evolution remain untested (Appendix E).

Six-week EV

  • Evidence-contract shadow (high EV, 2–4 weeks). Compile two high-value skills into explicit workflow contracts and log admitted/refused requests, correlated result ids, digest bindings and next-step guidance. Replay legal traces and direct violations, then report dispatch-block coverage, false refusals, recovery and end-to-end latency; do not claim SkillCFI-like safety until complete mediation and helper identity checks hold.
  • Memory-update acceptance gate (high EV, 2–5 weeks). Shadow micro/macro memory candidates with a frozen held-out set and separate safety/policy tests. Compare macro-only against macro-plus-micro, log accepted/rejected diffs and validation sizes, and include attribution, final evaluation, embedding, storage and retrieval in the cost ledger before promoting a memory update.
  • Skill-drift retention check (medium-high EV, 2–5 weeks). For each self-rewrite, diff item-level provenance against the original and a sampled model prior; flag deletion or weakening of outside-prior procedures, parameters and exceptions. Use same-batch task outcomes and repeated runs, then test retrieval and joint skill/code changes before adopting either rewrite guards or SkillDiff-style annotations.

TL;DRs for BQAA

  • OpenAI perplexity: retain supplier pretraining-loss disclosures as governance metadata, not as an agent-quality KPI; dataset comparability, post-pretraining elicitation and actionable task evals remain separate measurement problems.
  • SkillCFI: trusted result receipts and deterministic pre-dispatch state are more useful than a monitor that trusts model claims; measure false refusals and command-rendering overhead as well as blocked violations.
  • ARISE: accept memory updates through held-out outcomes, keep safety/policy approval distinct, and include adaptation and retrieval infrastructure in cost-per-task accounting.
  • SkillDiff: text similarity is not knowledge retention; protect expert-supplied outliers from self-rewrites and evaluate their downstream use before deployment.
