T1 — NEW rank: your logged trajectory is not the token stream the model sampled

T1: Terminal Agent Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Tasks, Junyao Yang, Yucheng Shi (equal contribution), Zhongzhi Li, Ruhan Wang, Zongxia Li, Haitao Mi, Leowei Liang (Tencent Hy Foundation Model Frontier / NUS / UGA / Indiana / UMD). v1 2026-09-10 03:42Z, announced in the Friday, September 11 arXiv cycle (in-window). Full PDF read including limitations. (PDF)

Post-trains Qwen3.5-122B-A10B (121.4B total, 116.0B expert, 10B active, 48 layers, Top-8-of-256 routing) with PPO inside cloud sandboxes (Daytona via Harbor), rewarded by executing each task's own test verifier. The contribution that earns the rank is diagnostic, not the score: the paper formalizes two fidelity conditions that multi-turn terminal-agent training — and, by extension, any pipeline that trains on logged tool trajectories — silently violates:

  1. Token identity drift (§4.1). The agent harness persists assistant messages as text and re-renders the whole history through a chat template each turn; re-tokenization enc(dec(aᵢ)) is not the identity (decoding is many-to-one), templates prune reasoning before the last user message, and re-serialized tool calls shift whitespace — so the trainer computes losses over token ids the sampler never produced. The fix, TITO (pack correction: the paper expands this as "token-in, token-out," not "Target-Identified Turn Optimization"), exchanges token identifiers across the harness boundary, pins templates append-only, stitches one token stream with a loss mask, and repairs boundaries through a strict-prefix → normalized-repair → retokenized-fallback → split hierarchy. Audited result: 0.0000% drift inside the loss region on a 1,402-sample production run.
  2. MoE routing divergence (§4.3). Inference (SGLang) and training (Megatron) compute router scores with different kernels; with 95.5% of parameters expert-held, a numerically tiny logit difference flips a Top-8 expert and the importance ratio ends up comparing two different sub-networks. The fix, R3 (Rollout Routing Replay), records per-token expert masks at rollout (~1.5 KiB/token, ≈48 MiB per 33k-token trajectory, <3% overhead, cached with the KV cache) and replays them as lookups in the training forward pass while keeping gating computed on live logits so the router stays trainable. Measured effect (Fig. 6, mask-weighted mean |∆log p| over the loss region on the production run): 0.021 without TITO+R3 → 0.013 with both.

Headline benchmark numbers, precisely scoped: Terminal-Bench 2.1 (89 held-out tasks, 3 attempts/task, temp 0.1) base 43.8% → SFT checkpoint 49.4% → T1 64.0% (peak at step 110; +14.6pp from RL, 20.2pp over base — the RL run starts at 49.4%, and the SFT stage is rejection-sampling fine-tuning the paper explicitly places "outside our scope"). Same-harness comparators: Opus 4.7 66.1, Opus 4.6 63.8, GPT-5.4 54.8 — T1 ranks 4th in its own table, not 1st. Long-Horizon Terminal-Bench is an average-reward metric (not resolved rate): T1 27.9 ties Gemini-3.1-Pro and beats GPT-5.4 (27.2) and GLM-5.1 (26.7) — the abstract's two named scalps — but Sonnet 4.6 (37.3), GLM-5.2 (31.6), and DeepSeek-V4-Pro (30.7) are all above T1 on the same metric, and GLM-5.1's number comes from a different harness's leaderboard block. Terminal-Bench Hard: 38.0% vs SFT 28.3%, base 20.0%.

Mandatory qualifications: no standard errors, CIs, or multi-seed runs anywhere; the authors admit a ±3-task (~3.4pp) eval-noise band on 89 tasks and select the best checkpoint by "repeated evaluations" at step 110. No single-axis ablations, by the authors' own §10 statement — reward density, data audit, critic warm-up, and R3 change together across campaign rows; TITO and R3 are not separated even in the 0.021→0.013 figure. Two of three benchmarks (LHTB, TBH) are the author group's own; the harness confound is admitted ("our model was RL-trained for exactly this harness whereas the frontier models were not" — Opus 4.6 swings 63.8→70.1 across harnesses). The verifier runs inside the agent-controlled sandbox ("filtered, not enforced" — no tamper detection). Turn budgets are internally inconsistent (abstract "300+", Figure 2 "up to 500", the eval harness caps at 60, case studies cite 164–473). No GPU count or total compute disclosed; no code, weights, data, or eval workbook released (badges without URLs). Comparator model names and the base-model citation are not independently verifiable.

BQAA/ADK application — editorial proposal: two directly portable audits. First, a token-identity audit: if any BQAA training/preference pipeline consumes logged trajectories as text, measure the enc(dec(a)) ≠ a fraction and template-mutation rate before trusting the logs as the behavior policy's output — T1 shows the default is broken, not the exception. Second, routing-aware telemetry: per-token MoE expert masks are ~1.5 KiB/token of capturable rollout metadata; recording them makes train/inference divergence measurable instead of mysterious (and gives observability a new span attribute class: which sub-network produced this token). The third lesson is about us, not the paper: when BQAA reports eval deltas on ~90-task suites, the honest noise band is ±3.4pp and peak-checkpoint selection needs repeated-evaluation disclosure — T1 states its band; most cards in this catalog do not.
