NEW A1 — Silent Success: A Release Gate That Passed on Checks It Never Ran, and Eight More (arXiv 2609.30307, Dong Hyeon Jeon, independent researcher, cs.SE, v1 23 Sep 2026)

Full PDF read: §1–9, 27 pages.

The gate (§3).

  • A blocking CI gate in front of a detection pipeline returned PASS in 48 seconds. Its false-positive subgate had executed 0 of its 2 checks, and its consistency section 6 of 8 (§3.1).
  • The cause: one datastore wasn't initialized in that environment. Every spec that read it errored and was recorded as "unavailable", which is an honest record.
  • Both deciding keys went wrong from there:
  • The first counted violations over the cases that had produced a result. The failed cases had already been removed, so the count was zero and zero meant pass.
  • The second compared a "no answer" sentinel against False, found them unequal, and read that as "no inconsistency" (§3.2–3.3).
  • Five layers each behaved reasonably, and together they held a false pass for about two weeks of green builds (§3.4).

The repair (§3.5).

  • A third verdict, unable to determine, that fails the build.
  • A coverage key comparing the evaluated population with an expected count derived from the case definitions, not from a constant.
  • The sentinel test changed from "is false" to "is not true".
  • Evaluated-versus-expected counts printed per section.

The build went red on the same 6-of-8 it had been passing, and only turned green again at 8/8 and 7/7. Of 11 regression tests written for the repaired rule, 6 failed against the old code, each asserting that a population of zero had scored a pass.

What stayed unfixed (§3.6). A detector elsewhere was firing with a margin of 0.000177 percentage points, and the repaired gate still can't see that.

Eight more cases (§4). Two are public LLM-infrastructure cases:

  • LiteLLM #35934. default_redis_ttl was configured, parsed and stored on the cache object but never applied to Redis writes. The effective TTL stayed at 60 s regardless of configuration.
  • vLLM #51228. num_free_slots was credited on every decode step for releases that returned nothing. Over six steps it rose 7,500 above the cache's 8,192-slot capacity, and can_allocate admitted an input of cache_size + 1.
  • Both fix PRs were opened by the author in August and are still open as of 2026-09-28 (GitHub API).

The common form and the remedy.

  • All nine cases promote a weaker claim to a stronger one, and none runs the other way (Fig. 1). Examples: unobserved to absent, configured to in effect, released to freed.
  • Seven of nine would have been caught by a single query, command or comparison (Table 2).
  • Rule R8 (§6.1): "A check that counts violations must also count itself." That means:
  • Report the evaluated population beside the verdict.
  • Derive the expected population from the definitions.
  • Add a failing third value.
  • Test with an input in which nothing runs.
  • §6.2 adds: type every zero as unviolated, unreachable or unexecuted, because "an untyped zero is not evidence".
  • §7 applies this to data-quality tools (Schelter et al.): a completeness metric computed over a population that had rows removed before the check still reports complete.

Companion paper 2609.30308 (full PDF read) tests these prescriptions on the same deployment snapshot.

  • The deployment's verification decisions read only values the system had written itself. One decision rests on 24 values, all written by the system (30308 §3.2). In the same deployment, 0 of 124,432 time-series rows came from observation, even though five source labels name measuring instruments (30307 §2.3).
  • Grading rows by provenance raised classified coverage from 36.1% to 98.4% of 194,620 rows (Table 3). A single write ingress refused 3,070 unattributable writes (Table 4).
  • Neither changed either verification decision. The repaired rows and the 32 rows the decisions actually read don't intersect (Fig. 1).
  • One verifier read 0 of the 45 rows it was meant to check. ORDER BY time DESC LIMIT 8 put 69,788 later rows in front of them (§6, K3).

Limitations the author states (§8).

  • The nine cases were fitted after the fact.
  • There is no denominator: the same person found, and upstreamed fixes for, both external cases.
  • The deployment is anonymized and checkable only against the author's own snapshot.
  • The author committed 11 further instances of the pattern while writing, inside tooling built to prevent it.
  • The C1 claim covers the two keys that decided that one run, not every key in the gate.

TL;DR for BQAA:

  • Every evaluator, health-check or eval-gate row should carry evaluated_count and an expected_count derived from definitions, plus a verdict enum whose undetermined value fails closed.
  • A zero-violation result over an empty or filtered population is a coverage gap, not a pass.
  • The companion paper adds a check on reach: confirm which rows a decision actually reads, not just how well the table is labelled.

NEW A2 — Completed Pairs Hide Capped Failures: A ReVerPi Case Study of Selective Context Projection (arXiv 2609.31381, Guangzhe Zhang, independent researcher, cs.AI, v1 25 Sep 2026)

Full PDF read: §1–7 and Appendices A–F.

Setup (§3, Table 1).

  • ReVerPi extends the Pi coding harness (v0.84.2).
  • In outgoing requests it replaces older, large tool observations with a 1,024-byte head/tail excerpt plus a content-addressed handle. An observation qualifies when it is at least 10 KiB, has been sent in two completed requests, and is neither the most recent one nor an error.
  • The model can call search_evidence or recover_evidence, sharing a 3-call allowance.
  • A capture phase seals a common prefix. Full (F) and projected (P) continuations then replay the same tape, each with a 12-request cap.
  • The model label is the proxy-reported deepseek-flash, at low effort.

The stop rule (§3.3).

  • The runner stopped a pair whenever the first arm didn't complete. A wrong final answer counted as completing; hitting the cap didn't.
  • So the probability of observing the companion arm after a first-arm cap is exactly zero (Eq. 2).
  • Adjusting for the first-arm outcome can't recover the missing arms, because the positivity assumption fails (App. A.3).

Key numbers (§5, Fig. 2, Tables 2–3).

  • The campaign: 86 runs, 641 model requests, 8.2M tokens (74.9% of input cached).
  • The 76 paired or capture runs broke down as:
  • 44 finished before any observation qualified.
  • 5 stopped during capture.
  • 27 reached a verified intervention boundary: 15 completed pairs and 12 stopped pairs.
  • Completed pairs tie at 12/15 correct per arm.
  • Of the 12 stopped pairs, 10 hit the first-arm cap with the companion never run. In the other 2, full completed and projected then capped.
  • Counting all 27 boundary runs (Table 3):
  • Full: 14 correct, 6 known failures, 7 never run.
  • Projected: 12 correct, 12 known failures, 3 never run.
  • The projected-minus-full success difference lies in [−9, +1] tasks of 27 (−33.3 to +3.7 points).
  • The first-arm cap rate was 20.0% when full ran first and 58.3% when projected ran first (Table 2).

Cost within the 11 pairs where both arms succeeded (§5.4, Table 5).

  • Projection used 25% fewer logical tokens in aggregate (1,267,036 → 949,774).
  • But the median pair's token ratio was 1.292, and 7 of 11 pairs used more. Dropping one pair turns the aggregate ratio to 1.077.
  • Suffix requests rose from 35 to 55.
  • Cached input fell by 407,680 tokens while uncached input rose by 89,684, so whether projection saves money depends on prices.

The selector (§5.3, Table 4). A size-threshold rule chooses when to project; it was fitted on 4 of the pairs.

  • It ties always-full at 3 failures in 17 runs.
  • That tie rests on a fitting pair. On the 13 runs outside fitting, it adds 1 failure and spends 8.6% more tokens.

One trace (§5.5).

  • In one task, projection retrieved the archived text yet used all 12 requests (237,629 suffix tokens) with no answer. Full answered in 3.
  • Its two quota refusals were logged with isError=false, because Pi marks a tool failure only when execute throws. The mismatch was fixed after the campaign in ReVerPi PR 15.

Limitations (§6, Table 6).

  • The questions were investigator-authored and adaptively built.
  • Each arm had one stochastic continuation, and the model label is proxy-reported.
  • The adapter is read-only rather than the full Pi product.
  • Raw requests and invoices are not distributed.
  • The findings describe this recorded campaign, not population effects.

TL;DR for BQAA:

  • In experiment tables, an arm the runner never executed should be a row with an unknown outcome, not a missing row. A cap is a known failure.
  • Report request counts and cached, uncached and output token categories next to totals.
  • Don't treat a harness's is_error flag as the only tool-failure signal. Here a refusal went out as a success.
