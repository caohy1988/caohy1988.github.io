2. Counterfactual Fairness Audits of Multi-Step Clinical LLM Agents Require a Measured Per-Action Instability Floor (submitted Sep 2, cs.CL, cross-listed cs.LG)

Rohith Reddy Bellibaltu (Florida International), Manpreet Singh (Boston University), Deepak Parashar (Manipal), Rahul Joshi (Symbiosis). Submitted to IEEE; harness released as FairMedAgent 0.1.1.

The clinical framing is a vehicle; the result is about measuring any multi-step agent's actions. The agent runs a fixed six-stage trajectory (triage, ordering, a deterministic environment step that returns fixture results, reassessment, prescribing, documentation), so five model-facing decisions per vignette, each committing a discrete action that constrains the next. Before reporting any demographic contrast, the authors ran the reference condition ten times over sixteen vignettes with nothing varied, in independent contexts, at the provider's default sampling: 45 pairwise comparisons over 800 calls. The pooled flip rate was 0.087 (374 of 4,320 outcome-vignette comparisons; cluster bootstrap interval 0.056 to 0.116), and it was not one number: ICU escalation flipped in 0.022 of comparisons and the controlled-substance caution flag in 0.179, an eightfold spread that survives dividing out prevalence (ratios to a homogeneous-rate null run 0.05 to 0.42). Seventy-five of 96 vignette-action cells were unanimous across all ten draws, so the floor is carried by 21 contested cells, eleven of them with rates between 0.3 and 0.7. A second model gave a pooled floor of 0.067 and ranked the six actions almost identically (Spearman 0.94, exact permutation p = 0.017), with the same action at each extreme, so the level looks like a property of the model and the ordering like a property of the decision. Majority vote over disjoint groups lowered the floor to 0.063 at R = 3 and 0.053 at R = 5, then flattened; a Bernoulli null at the observed per-cell rates reproduces that curve exactly (null mean 0.048, interval 0.025 to 0.076 at R = 5), and a linear continuation would not reach zero before about R = 15. The authors decline to estimate the temperature-zero floor, citing batch-composition nondeterminism the auditor of a hosted model cannot observe. Every demographic contrast in their four-vignette pilot fell inside the range the identical re-render itself spanned; the two cells that looked like signal against the replicated floor were disqualified by a prompt defect in the reference arm (the model declined the first dispatch and it was reissued with added framing), which the harness now prevents by emitting one canonical dispatch per step. The four-vignette pilot's floor of 0.136 with two actions that "never moved" was an artifact of sample size; sixteen vignettes gave a different ranking and no zero. No disparity result is claimed; bands are unadjudicated and the harness refuses to compute the within-range estimand without adjudicators, returning a numeric rate with ground_truth: false rather than NA.

TL;DR for BQAA. This is the action-level twin of Clean Engineering, Unstable Measurement from 1934. Any BQAA metric of the form "how often does action X change between condition A and condition B" (prompt version, model alias, user cohort, tool version) must be shown beside a same-run floor: the same condition replayed R times, reported per action type, not pooled, with the decoding configuration recorded. Three consequences for the schema and the metrics catalogue. The tool-call or decision event needs a stable action-type key so the floor can be computed per action rather than per session. Replicate runs need a draw index and a replicate-group id on the session so disjoint-group aggregation is a query, not a convention. And the vocabulary note from 2034 gets a corollary: a per-action stability rate is diagnostic of the instrument, and the docs should say plainly that it is not a quality score and that a contrast smaller than the floor is noise. Metrics-method note; no feature.

3. Where Reliability Lives: Experimental Localisation of Behavioural Properties in an Agent System (submitted Sep 2, cs.MA)

Timothy Marsden, Matthew Collecutt, James Marsden; Taniwha AI. 31 pages, preregistered designs, sealed artifacts, harness release deferred.

The instrument is a persistent simulated settlement ("Copperhollow") in which an authoritative append-only ledger adjudicates every attempted act against world state: admissible acts are accepted into a single history, inadmissible ones are refused on the wire with a typed, machine-readable reason, and every verdict is scored against conserved world truth the agents cannot alter. Part I holds cognition fixed and intervenes on the institution's epistemic mechanisms. Ablating one provenance rule (sign beliefs remember the band of the reading they replaced, and the tribunal's surprise gate consumes it) moved false attribution from 4 to 44 of 107 verdicts on genuinely sabotaged faces, with the unexplained count invariant at 28. The observability result is section 4.2: graded corruption of the provenance stamp raised false attribution roughly tenfold, while on the answer plane "nothing diagnosed the failure": verdict volume, decisiveness and the behavioural-invariants gate were unchanged at every level, and the one monitored indicator that did move, a verdict-grounds sufficiency check, moved in the reassuring direction. On the mechanism plane the failure was loud: the surprise gate's own trace count tracked the treatment exactly (35, 35, 0, 0, 0 across the five corruption levels). The authors' sentence is the card: "whether the failure was observable at all depended on which plane was instrumented." A 5×2 factorial then shows the belief channel converting evidential darkness into false blame (4 to 60) where the blinded tribunal converts it into honest "unresolved" (27 to 102, zero false attributions in 510 verdicts), and a preregistered falsifier that staged a veridical first-hand witness flipped the channel's marginal value positive (9 of 11 seeds, zero false names). Part II holds enforcement fixed and ablates, kills, replaces (a frozen context-only deepseek-v4-flash panel) and deceives the cognition. Throughput, hygiene and cost moved dramatically; five pre-declared properties did not: singular accepted history, typed refusal of every invalid act, duty recovery from institutional history alone after mid-task process death, zero duplicate accepted work, zero false completions (2,581 completion claims from the panel, none false, so that property is "held" rather than load-tested, and the authors say so). Two findings are directly about event records. One false speculative belief produced 813, 835 and 836 refusals per run (mean 828), all typed already_at_destination, because "no decision path consumes refusal evidence"; the substituted LLM panel met the same contended lease 1, 1 and 3 times and stood down where the native cognition retried 42, 41 and 39 times. And on identical seeds, arms receiving typed, attributable refusals versus generic failure signals retried at the same cadence and recovered at the same instant yet diverged deterministically in accepted acts (16 vs 24), with the less-informed arm producing the better week; the mechanism is unattributed and the authors forbid a "richer feedback is better" reading. Under trusted false testimony, the world refused all 5,455 resulting attempts, typed world_contradiction, and 5,448 of them are fully attributed through a five-link chain from the delivered falsehood to the refusal. Scope is stated bluntly: one designed world, deterministic native minds, n = 3 cells, one comparator at one cadence, no optimising adversary.

TL;DR for BQAA. Three notes. First, a refusal or rejection by a tool, gateway or policy layer is an event with a typed reason code, not an absence or an error string, and the paper's refusal storm (about 830 identical typed refusals a week from one bad belief) is exactly the pattern a per-session count-by-reason-code query catches and a success-rate dashboard does not; ADK's tool-error path and any BQAA guardrail integration should carry reason_type as a first-class column. Second, the mechanism-plane result is an argument for instrumenting gates and validators as their own events (the "surprise gate fired" counter was the only truthful signal) rather than inferring health from answer-side KPIs; this is the same lesson as the tool-timeout result in DNative-Twin from 1934, now with a health indicator that actively lied. Third, the typed-versus-generic refusal divergence is a reminder that the content of an error returned to the agent is an intervention on the trajectory, so the tool-call event should retain the error payload the agent actually saw, not a normalised code alone. Schema notes strengthened; nothing to start.

4. The Illusion of Independent Quorums: Epistemic Fault Domains and Correlated Cognitive Failures in Agentic Quorums (submitted Aug 24, cs.DC, cross-listed cs.MA; listed Sep 4)

Jun He and Deying Yu, OpenKedge.io. Sequel to their "honest quorum problem" preprint (2607.16109).

The setting is a multi-agent approval gate for high-impact mutations (decommission a replica, change a security policy, grant emergency privileges, roll back a deployment). The claim: "replication does not imply epistemic redundancy." Reviewers that query the same stale telemetry cache, retrieve the same corrupted document, invoke the same faulty tool or inherit the same planner premise agree for identical wrong reasons, so three votes can be one epistemic witness. The paper defines an Epistemic Fault Basis (the finite set of modeled upstream fault events for a decision), Epistemic Fault Domains (which reviewers each root structurally reaches), and the Structural Epistemic Cut κ_E, the minimum number of modeled roots whose exposure covers a decisive coalition under the quorum rule. Under a 3-of-5 rule with one telemetry root reaching three reviewers, κ_E = 1 despite five votes. Four results are proved: arbitrarily large quorums can retain κ_E = 1; threshold resilience is exactly characterised by domain coverage; discovering shared ancestry can only lower or preserve credited resilience; and adding voters at a fixed threshold cannot raise the cut, so a weak quorum is repaired by separating evidence paths or changing the rule, never by adding reviewers. Exact κ_E is NP-hard (set cover) with an ILP given. The Dependency-Aware Quorum Controller separates prospective selection over planned ancestry from commit-time admission over realized provenance: after the vote, the controller reconstructs the evidence DAG from provenance logs and attestations, computes the cut, and denies the mutation if the cut is below k_min, with unknown dependencies conservatively classified as shared. Seeded Monte Carlo over 1,000 replayable scenarios reproduces the closed-form expectations within about 1.1 points: under 2-of-3, three copies of one model over shared evidence commit unsafely 97.3% of the time when the shared root is faulted, three different model families over the same evidence 96.8%, and the same single model over three separately rooted evidence packages 6.7%; under unanimity, path separation takes the failure rate to 0.1%. Path separation also raised inter-reviewer disagreement from 25.8% to 90.8%, which the authors flag as a runtime signal of upstream faultiness. A frozen 120-task benchmark (five categories, three individually sufficient evidence packages per task, one faulted on unsafe tasks, CEL safety predicates, manifest hashed) is specified for external model runs but not yet run; the paper is explicit that the cut is a structural property of a declared model and "not a calibrated probability of semantic truth."

TL;DR for BQAA. This is the fifth paper this week to need a record that cites the exact records it derived from, and the first to say what the citation buys at the multi-agent level: if each agent's vote or recommendation event carries the ids of the evidence it consumed (tool-call ids, retrieved-document ids, telemetry snapshot ids), then "how many distinct upstream roots stand behind this approval" is a graph query over the event table, and the disagreement-rate signal (a quorum whose members suddenly agree at 26% instead of 91%) is a derived metric. Same nullable repeated source-event-ids field as the 1001 note, applied to inter-agent votes rather than to plans; the reconstruction step in DAQC is what BQAA's store would be the input to. Schema note strengthened; the controller itself is application logic, not BQAA.

5. Legibility is Not Interpretability: Comparing Judged and Actual Importance in Chain-Of-Thought Reasoning (submitted Sep 3, cs.CL, cross-listed cs.LG; COLM 2026)

Kevin Du, Alexander Hoyle (ETH Zürich), Laura Ruis (MIT), Acyr Locatelli (Cohere). Code, rollouts and a viewer released.

The paper operationalises a reasoning step's importance as its advantage: the change in the probability of the final answer from including that step, estimated by Monte Carlo rollouts (N = 50 per prefix) from the same model, with a changepoint procedure (PELT with an exact binomial cost, calibrated to a 2% per-series false-positive rate) to label a step consequential when its jump exceeds 0.1 with 95% posterior probability. On 1,800 responses per model across six math benchmarks, consequential steps are rare (1.8% of steps in-distribution for Qwen3-1.7B non-thinking, lower with thinking on), and gains from scale and thinking mode come mostly from responses whose value is high from the first step (24% to 61% with thinking) rather than from mid-trajectory recovery. The result that matters here is section 6: out-of-the-box LLM judges asked to rate a step's advantage improve with scale over a prevalence baseline but the best (Qwen3.6-27B) sits 9× below the in-distribution noise ceiling; fine-tuned critics reach PR-AUC 0.28 to 0.30 on incorrect responses (about half the conservative ceiling of roughly 0.6) but only 0.065 to 0.10 on correct ones (10 to 20% of ceiling), and critic scaling is flat. At a 0.5% inspection budget critics hit precision 0.55 to 0.72 on wrong answers, matching the ceiling, and 0.08 to 0.29 on right ones. The authors' reading: step importance is "only partially recoverable from the text of the reasoning trace," the asymmetry may be partly the easy proxy of a wrong final-answer emission step, and the finding bears on "judge- or human-based evaluation of reasoning steps, process reward models, and critics that assume step-level text comprehension." Math-only, Qwen family, one judge prompt family; the labels are themselves estimates, hence the split-half ceilings.

TL;DR for BQAA. This bounds the failure-step pointer reserve from 1001 and the step-citation verdicts from DRACO (2034) and MLflow's judges. A judge that reads a trace and names the step that mattered is well calibrated for "where did this go wrong" and poorly calibrated for "which step made this succeed," so a step-level verdict row should record which of the two it is, and any BQAA docs example that aggregates "critical step" citations across successful sessions should carry this caveat. Column note for eval events: step citations from a judge need an outcome stratum (session failed or succeeded) before they are counted. No feature.

Six-week EV

Ranking unchanged from 1001, 1934, 2034 and 2115. What moved:

  • Per-action instability floor beside any flip-rate metric (new metrics-method note, from item 2). Any BQAA metric or example that compares an agent's actions across conditions must be reported with a same-run identical-replay floor, per action type, with decoding configuration recorded. Needs an action-type key on decision or tool-call events and a replicate-group id on sessions.
