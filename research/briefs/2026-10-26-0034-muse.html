NEW 1 — TraceTrial-Bench: score the harness on its own sheet, or success will hide its failures

arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32285 (v1, submitted 23 Oct 2026; announced in the Mon Oct 26 batch). Full PDF read — every part read, including methods (§§3–4), results (§5, Tables 6–10), limitations (§6) and Appendices A–F (taxonomy, adapters, Trace IR schema Tables 26–29, verdict rules, heatmaps, five case studies). Cao et al. (TraceLite AI; CAS / Zhongguancun Academy / Tsinghua / BIT).

What it is. A 95-task, mechanism-oriented benchmark that treats the native harness as the system under test across 4 harnesses (Claude Code, Codex, DeepSeek Harness, OpenClaw) × 9 models = 3,420 selected runs (§4.1). Reliability is organized into seven components — Context, Execution, Governance, Lifecycle, Observability, Tools, Verification (Figure 4; Observability has only 7 tasks, Verification 1 — coverage is uneven). Native traces are normalized into a provenance-preserving Trace IR (common envelope + event families in Table 2; full schema Tables 26–28), evidence is selected by declared per-criterion selectors (MEA, §3.3.2), and Agent vs. Harness criteria are scored on separate sheets (50/50 combined score, §3.4).

Numbers to quote (with their tables). Only 286/3,420 runs (8.4%) pass every criterion in both layers, while 1,351 (39.5%) pass all Agent criteria (§5.1). 1,065 runs (31.1%) achieve task success despite ≥1 failed Harness criterion (Table 10) — the paper's central warning against outcome-only evaluation. Harness Macro spans 59.8% (Codex) to 72.0% (OpenClaw); Claude Code leads task success (44.0%) but not Harness satisfaction (Table 6), so the two rankings genuinely diverge. By dimension, Execution is weakest pooled (64.6% Harness Micro) and Observability strongest (84.3%; Table 9) — read that as “the 7 observability tasks chosen were mostly passed,” not as field-wide observability maturity. Candidate failure-attribution links activate in 842/1,700 joint-failure runs (49.5%; 1,633 endpoint pairs, §5.5).

Honesty the brief must keep. The authors are unusually disciplined, and Collection should preserve their distinctions: an activated candidate link is not validated propagation — validation requires manual endpoint reconciliation, an observable intermediate distortion and competing-explanation review (§3.5, Table 34), and the paper reports no corpus-level estimate of link precision, selector recall or judge agreement (§3.6). Limitations (§6.1): one selected execution per cell (no within-cell repeatability), uneven mechanism coverage, varying judge budgets (§4.2: single judge model, mixed token caps), and state-2 harness verdicts that depend on the Agent outcome (Table 4) — so the two layers are distinct but not statistically independent. Treat harness scores as operational criterion satisfaction, in the authors' own phrase, not confirmed-defect rates.

BQAA read. This is directly importable schema guidance: (a) store agent_verdict and harness_verdict as separate columns — collapsing them loses the 31.1%; (b) record evidence boundary per claim (external state vs. model-facing result vs. provider declaration, Table 25) — a tool result has several non-equivalent representations and BigQuery should not silently substitute one for another; (c) keep unmatched tool calls/results explicit (tool_result = null ≠ empty success, §C.3) and never heuristically pair ambiguous records (§3.3.1); (d) if BQAA ever exposes “attribution,” copy the claim ladder (verdict → confirmed defect → candidate link → validated propagation) instead of a single blame label. Released tasks/traces/verdicts (repo linked on the paper's title page) make this checkable rather than rhetorical.

NEW 2 — MCP-Injector: calibrated tool faults collapse quality by exposure, and ungrounded answers hide it

arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32555 (v1, submitted 23 Oct 2026; announced Mon Oct 26). Full PDF read — every part read, including fault calibration (§4, Table 2), protocol (§5), results (§6, Tables 4–5), limitations (§7) and Appendices A–I plus the NeurIPS checklist. Iannillo et al. (Univ. Luxembourg / Tilburg / GSSI / Naples).

What it is. A drop-in, protocol-aware fault-injection proxy on the MCP tool-call boundary: six operators (minor mistake, incorrect output, invalid output, explicit error, delay, silent drop; Table 1) × three persistence regimes (permanent / transient / intermittent), with selection weights calibrated from 57,240 classified issue/PR threads across 1,774 public MCP repos (human audit κ = 0.93 mode / 0.80 persistence on 117 threads, §4). The mined mix is permanent-heavy (91.3% permanent; explicit-error/permanent alone 65.6% of injectable mass, Table 3) — the authors explicitly call it a stress profile, not an operational failure rate (§4). Experiment: 1,350 cells (2 gpt-oss backbones × 3 stacks — MCP-Bench plan-and-execute, OpenAI Agents SDK, LangChain — × 3 proxy conditions × 25 MCP-Bench tasks × 3 seeds, §5).

Numbers to quote. Under faults, pooled task-completion score falls 60% (8.08 → 3.23/10; paired Δ −4.85, all nine metrics BH-corrected p < 0.001, Table 5), and the drop persists at 57% among runs that complete under both conditions — i.e. mostly quality loss, not just non-completion (§6.1, Appendix D). Partial degradation is the modal outcome (56% of 450 fault cells, Figure 3). Crucially, Appendix C finds an exposure effect, not dose-response: cells with ≥1 fired fault score 2.72 vs. 6.93 for zero-dose cells, while the per-fault dose coefficient is ~0 — the first fault does most of the damage, and dose is confounded with tool-call volume. Scale does not buy resilience for these two backbones under this setup: resilience-ratio model coefficient −0.009 (p = 0.96, wide CI), pooled completion 91.1% (120B) vs. 95.6% (20B) reported as descriptive only (§6.3). Stack differences are suggestive, not established (one interaction p = 0.048, one p = 0.074, no multiplicity correction, unequal fault exposure across stacks, §6.2) — do not card “LangChain is most resilient” as a finding. The sleeper result for analytics: tool bypass (plausible answers with no tool evidence) exists without faults and, under faults, runs below the grounding threshold supply 16% of all fulfillment credit vs. 5% fault-free, so grounded fulfillment falls 63% rather than 58% (§6.1).

Limitations (authors' §7, endorsed here). One model family, one serving setup, 16K context; MCP-Bench is easy fault-free (95–100% completion), limiting transfer to harder workloads; transient/intermittent regimes are effectively untested (9 of 1,905 fired events); content operators omit schema drift/truncation; LLM-judge noise remains despite 5× median scoring. Quote the 60% as “under this permanent-heavy stress profile on 25 single-server tasks,” never as a production failure rate.

BQAA read. Tool-error telemetry deserves first-class columns: operator-equivalent mode (explicit error vs. wrong-but-valid vs. schema violation vs. delay vs. drop), persistence guess (same tool failing every call vs. once), and post-failure action (retry / switch / fallback — Appendix G: switch 57%, fallback 22%, retry 21% pooled, stack-dependent). Without the mode split, silent content faults (19.1% of the profile) look like model reasoning errors in aggregate. And every “success” metric on a warehouse should have a grounded twin (success with tool evidence) — otherwise bypass behavior, which needs no fault to occur, inflates exactly the number leadership watches during an incident.

TL;DRs for BQAA

  • TraceTrial-Bench: store harness verdicts separately from task verdicts — 31.1% of runs succeed while the harness fails, and outcome-only tables cannot see it.
  • TraceTrial evidence discipline: a tool result's external, model-facing and normalized forms are different claims; keep boundary + provenance per row, and leave ambiguous call/result pairs unlinked.
  • MCP-Injector: first tool fault does most of the damage (exposure, not dose) — alert on first-fault-per-run and mode × persistence, not raw error counts alone.
  • Grounded success: always pair fulfillment/success with a tool-evidence-gated twin; ungrounded bypass supplied 16% of fulfillment credit under faults.
  • Industry: verified quiet — 20/20 registry clocks checked, zero in-window uploads; spend no schema effort chasing this window.
