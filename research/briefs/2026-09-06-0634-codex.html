Academy

1. HarnessDev: evaluate the selected harness on unseen tasks — arXiv:2609.01437

Paper: HarnessDev: Can LLMs Create and Evolve Their Own Agent Harness? Yuhao Wu et al.; September 1 v1. The PDF's internal September 2 dateline differs from its arXiv submission date. Full 41-page PDF, including appendices, read; Tables 6–7 and Figure 7 visually checked.

The benchmark separates harness creation, execution, and evaluation. Its evolution study reports nine creator/runtime lineages. A disjoint 630-task SWE-Pro evaluation occurs after development; those scores cannot guide editing or final selection. All five self-runtime declarations improve on held-out tasks, but three of four non-Gemini creators using the fixed Gemini executor regress. The GPT-5.5/fixed-Gemini lineage gains 2.4 points on its development benchmark pair while losing 10.32 points on held-out SWE-Pro. These are different evaluation aggregates, not a paired estimate of one effect. Across 64 version transitions, development and held-out scores move in the same direction only 34 times. PDF, §§3.2, 4.2, pp. 12–13.

Limits: self and fixed-executor evolution use separate lineages, with one trajectory per cell; they are not matched swaps of a final artifact. Reported executor-token efficiency excludes creator cost. PDF, Figure 7 and Appendix B.4.

Why this clears dedupe: the existing same-model-harness card establishes that changing a harness changes performance. This adds evidence about development-time selection and generalization, beyond recording a harness version.

BQAA TL;DR — inference: distinguish feedback, probe, and held-out evaluations; freeze the declared harness before revealing final scores. Retain creator, executor, artifact revision, and task-set identity. A bounded check should ask whether the chosen improvement survives unseen tasks and an alternate executor, with development and execution costs reported separately. This is an acceptance test for existing work, not permission to build a self-improving harness.

2. MaP-SQL: measure useful selection separately from order stability — arXiv:2609.00834

Paper: Replacing Training with Memory: Listwise Selection for Text-to-SQL. Yeonseok Jeong, Soyoung Yoon, Seongjun Lee, and Seung-won Hwang; September 1 v1. Full 19-page PDF, including appendices, read; Tables 2–3 and 5–6 visually checked.

MaP-SQL retrieves selection criteria distilled from labeled training examples and aggregates rankings of fixed SQL candidate pools. Its implementation fixes execution-result group order and shuffles within groups. On BIRD-dev with 32 Agentar candidates, execution accuracy rises from 71.97% to 73.08%, while reported selector input tokens fall from 376,863 to 98,234 per query. The comparator is the authors' R³-SQL reproduction without fine-tuning, using shared generation/selection models; it is not the unavailable original trained selector. The abstract's average 2.92× token reduction covers input-token accounting, not total workflow cost or elapsed time. PDF, §§4–5, Table 2.

Order sensitivity remains substantial: in the eight-candidate test, same-candidate agreement across two initial orderings rises from 18.98% to 33.17%; agreement with a correct answer reaches 22.63%. The paper assumes upstream schema linking for very large schemas. PDF, §6.3, Table 6, §7.

Why this clears dedupe: it supplies a SQL-specific controlled selection experiment and distinguishes candidate agreement from execution correctness. It adds an actionable check to existing memory-use and judge evaluation, rather than another generic claim that memory helps.

BQAA TL;DR — inference: freeze the candidate pool and retrieved criteria, then change presentation order. Record candidate identity, execution-result identity, and independently checked correctness separately: choosing different equivalent queries need not mean a changed answer. Test whether the criteria improve decisions within a complete budget, including generation, execution, retrieval, and selection. “No selector fine-tuning” does not mean no labeled data or no preparation cost.
