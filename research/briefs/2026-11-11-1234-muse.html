On certified candidates its verifiers beat seven plain coding-agent harnesses on all four execution-scored benchmarks (Cohen's κ* 0.787 / 0.720 / 0.465 / 0.548), but the transferable findings are the failure accounting: with the model fixed the harness alone swings κ* from 0.16 to 0.73, on heavy environments plain harnesses lose mainly by never delivering a verifier at all, delivered generated verifiers mainly reject correct solutions (Veritas itself accepts only 45% of Terminal-Bench references), and the certification audit finds official verifiers erring the other way (57 of 146 τ²-bench tasks lack full power; 24 pass a no-op outright). Shared requirement across both ranks: autonomy and auto-graded improvement loops are only as trustworthy as the stored evidence and the checker judging them — govern the ontology, trace every action with cost, and certify the verifier before its verdicts gate anything.

NEW 1 — Databricks Genie becomes an autonomous AI coworker, with Ontology, Search, and the Unity Gateway control plane

Primary: Databricks Blog, "The new Genie: autonomous AI for complex enterprise work" (2026-11-11), https://www.databricks.com/blog/new-genie-autonomous-ai-complex-enterprise-work — counted as one item; the surrounding "Week of Genie" posts (Genie One expansion, Genie Ontology GA, Genie Agents data sources, plugins into third-party assistants) are one launch train, not separate ranks.

What shipped, per the primary. Genie is now positioned as an autonomous coworker that "stays on the job continuously, tracking progress, following up on missing inputs, and taking action as new information arrives," reachable via Genie One, email, Slack, and Teams. Jobs run on a schedule or on triggers (the post's examples: a metric dipping, or more than 10 customers asking for the same feature); Genie is proactive about noticing repeated work and offering to take it on; many jobs run at once under user-set guardrails with a control panel for active jobs and outputs, and the user decides what runs autonomously vs what needs sign-off. Example jobs are analytics-native: monitoring insurance claims for fraud-pattern and loss-ratio drift and routing assembled case files to underwriters, and managing marketing campaigns with underperformance investigations and weekly budget changes proposed for approval. Two context layers support that autonomy. Genie Ontology is described as an agentic ontology "built by agents and governed by people": agents build most of it automatically from dashboards, queries, and notebooks (plus external BI-tool logic and Workday org directories), authoritative definitions live in Unity Catalog (Domains, Pages, Ontology Snippets), an OntoRank algorithm gives governed definitions precedence, and Ontology Manager adds context-health monitoring and proactive gap alerts for frequently-asked concepts lacking governed definitions. Genie Search, introduced today, is a hybrid enterprise search inside Databricks: an index over core business apps (Drive, Jira, Confluence, Workday, SharePoint) plus live MCP search for recent/long-tail sources, composed per task with SQL into one cited answer, with each source's access controls mapped and enforced before results return; search evidence also feeds OntoRank's source-trust weighting. Genie Agents (team-scoped specialists) extend across unstructured files via content search in Unity Catalog Volumes "without a separate vector index," act through connectors (Slack, Atlassian, GitHub, Gmail, Drive, Microsoft 365), and can be shared externally via OpenSharing or Clean Rooms with conversation/output limits. The control plane is Unity Gateway: Genie acts with the user's identity under Unity Catalog and source-system permissions; admins control connector/MCP-tool access and approval requirements; and — the sentence BQAA should quote back in competitive reviews — "Every action Genie takes is traced and auditable," with workspace and per-user budgets, spending caps, and alerts, and the ability to ask Genie to investigate its own usage and agent traces for wasted tokens and costly tool calls. Databricks also claims, in a new benchmark built with Turing in the same workspace, Genie with Ontology answered correctly in 91% of task runs (1.6× the stronger of two compared frontier agents) at less than half the tokens; treat that as a vendor-run comparison on knowledge-connecting tasks, not an independent result, and do not card the ratio without the setup.

NEW 1 — Veritas: A Verifier Generation Harness for Agent Tasks — https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13336

Reserve promotion from the Nov 11 batch (abstract-screened at the 0034 slot; no new arXiv batch is live this slot). Full PDF read (pdftotext, entire paper: Sections 1–5, Tables 1–5, Figure 1, Limitations/Ethics, and Appendices A–E including the certification gates, official-verifier audit Tables 6–9, four case studies, and the full prompts/contracts).

Zhang et al. (ByteDance/UIC/NYU) study the missing piece in every self-improvement loop: tasks collected from real use arrive without a verifier, so they cannot enter the loop until someone hand-writes one, and a flawed verifier makes the loop optimize the flaw. Veritas is a coding agent (OpenCode) plus three mechanisms, each aimed at a named structural failure (Section 3.3, prompts in Appendix E). Evidence header (E): state up front exactly what the verifier will observe at judgment time (Table 1: e.g. τ²-bench messages + database before/after; terminal benchmarks final-state filesystem only), require every check to rest on listed evidence, give state comparison precedence over trajectory/dialogue when both bear on a requirement (the trajectory is "the evidence a candidate can most easily fake" — an agent can say the booking is canceled without canceling it), and declare unwitnessable requirements unchecked rather than inventing a criterion. Domain rule library (R): a mounted filesystem of 19,603 sourced rules (six domains; PRBench finance/legal splits plus τ² airline assertions and banking policy titles from tasks disjoint from evaluation) that the agent browses selectively before writing trajectory assertions, instantiating only applicable rules with their ids — a prior for obligations the task text never states (confirm before refund, report after). Authoring principles (H): nine benchmark-invariant rules standing in for the tests a verifier cannot yet run — judge observable outcomes not implementation details, claim-only/no-op candidates must fail, correct-but-different candidates must pass under the weakest comparison that still separates, and always deliver a loadable artifact. Two of the nine principles (weakest comparison, output self-check) were kept by a 32-round self-improvement loop over fit/validation splits with a mechanical anti-fitting lint; the terminal benchmarks were added after the mechanisms froze and are the held-out rows (Sections 3.4/4.1, Appendix A.3).

The measurement design is the reason to take the ranking seriously. Because official verifiers are themselves known-flawed, verdicts are scored only on certified tasks/candidates: per-task suites built from reference solutions or oracle final states (reference, no-op, near-misses, equivalents, cut/corrupted outputs) must pass gates G1–G5 (reference accepted, both labels present, single-check deletion exposed, every mismatch hand-adjudicated, three repeats stable), leaving 58 τ² tasks (166 candidates), 138 SWE-bench Verified tasks (435), 33 Terminal-Bench tasks (189), and 39 TB-Science tasks (219) (Section 4.1, Appendix B). The headline metric κ* is Cohen's κ against official labels with every missing verdict charged as an error — so a harness that fails to deliver is penalized like one that judges wrong. With the model fixed (GLM-5.2) and only the harness varied, Veritas leads seven plain harnesses (Codex CLI, OpenCode, pi, Terminus 2, Hermes, Claude Code, Gemini CLI) on all four execution-scored benchmarks (Table 2: τ²-bench 0.787 vs best-plain 0.726; SWE-bench 0.720 vs 0.693; Terminal-Bench 0.465 vs 0.387; TB-Science 0.548 vs 0.474), ties the best on JudgeBench (0.980 two-order-consistent accuracy) and trails by one pair on LLMBar (0.893 vs 0.920), at no measurable token premium on τ²-bench (97.0M prompt tokens vs 104.5M for plain OpenCode, Appendix C.2). Three supporting results matter more than the margins. First, the harness alone is a first-order variable: same-model plain harnesses span κ* 0.16–0.73 on τ²-bench and −0.04–0.39 on Terminal-Bench, and no plain harness is best everywhere (Section 4.2). Second, the mechanisms transfer unchanged and help the weakest most: added to Claude Code they lift Terminal-Bench 0.238 → 0.586 (+0.348) and TB-Science 0.144 → 0.465 (+0.321), improving 9 of 12 harness–benchmark pairs across three harnesses with no drop worse than 0.050 (Table 3); against vendors' own harnesses Veritas wins on two of three other model families and loses clearly to Codex CLI on the third, where Veritas also delivered 0 of 47 banking verifiers and τ² is unreportable (Table 5) — card the loss, not just the wins. Third, the ablations warn against naive adoption: on τ²-bench removing H collapses κ* 0.787 → 0.250, below plain OpenCode (0.563), because evidence + rules without authoring discipline produce more assertions that reject correct candidates; removing R costs 0.472, removing E mostly costs delivery on SWE-bench (verdictless candidates 15 → 46, judged-subset κ nearly flat) (Table 4, Section 4.3). The error analysis (Section 4.5, Appendix C.1/Table 9) should be quoted on the card: on Terminal-Bench plain harnesses lose mainly by non-delivery (pi judges best among delivered verifiers at 0.671 yet leaves 67/189 candidates verdictless, κ* 0.048; Veritas leaves 7), and even Veritas accepts only 45% of reference solutions — generated verifiers err by false rejection, while the Appendix B.3 audit finds official verifiers erring mainly by leniency (Table 8: τ²-bench 57/146 tasks lack full power including 24 where a no-op scores full marks; DeepSWE 36/106 give full marks to a hunk-deleted gold patch; a shared model judge changes verdicts on identical input in 72/500 tasks and rejects 47 correct paraphrases; Agents' Last Exam 27/82 confirmed defective by execution). The four case studies (Appendix D) make each mechanism concrete: state precedence rejecting a dialogue-only identity check the database never recorded; a synthetic-damage check rejecting a center-returning placeholder that interface checks pass; a library rule (AIR-0021) correcting a baggage-allowance verifier from 0 to 1 free bag; and vendor-harness verifiers guessing an exact answer and rejecting the only correct candidate where Veritas checks only the verifiable subset.

Why it matters for BQAA/ADK, and how it differs from cards already held. Existing judge cards score how judges behave (trace-vs-text inputs in 2611.14493, calibration in 2611.09291, confirmation bias in 2611.05662) and 2611.09895 mutates workflows to test checks; Veritas is the complementary authoring side — how to generate an executable checker for a customer task that has none, and how to certify it before trusting it. The importable pattern for any BQAA-generated-check or ADK-eval-authoring feature is explicit: declare the judgment-time evidence contract (which trace/state fields the checker may read, with final-state precedence over transcript claims), supply a browsable domain-rule prior instead of stuffing rules into the prompt, enforce weakest-comparison + delivery disciplines, and evaluate checkers on certified known-correct/known-wrong candidates while reporting delivery rate, false-rejection vs false-acceptance separately — never a single agreement number. Limits to carry on the card (Limitations, Section 4.1): each configuration run once (two-run κ* wobble 0.040–0.083 on τ²-bench); 33–138 tasks per execution benchmark; most candidates constructed (reference/no-op/corrupted) rather than real agent attempts, which "may be harder to judge"; certified official verdicts treated as truth, so a correctly-stricter generated verifier is scored wrong where officials are lenient; τ²/SWE rows are fit+validation tasks the mechanisms were designed on (test splits held back; only the terminal rows are held-out); the harness sees no reference solution but the model may have memorized public benchmarks; and the rule library's airline/banking entries come from non-evaluation tasks of the same benchmarks — disjoint, but the same distribution, so transfer to BQAA SQL/analytics tasks is unproven. Card the E/R/H recipe, the harness-spread and delivery/false-rejection accounting, and the certification-gate + official-audit method; do not transfer the absolute κ* values to analytics-agent checking.
