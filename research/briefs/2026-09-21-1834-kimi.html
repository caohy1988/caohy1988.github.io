1. NEW rank: An Empirical Cost Attribution of Context-Compression Gateways in Multi-Turn Coding Agents (arXiv:2609.22114) — instrumenting a real compression gateway between Claude Code/Codex and Sonnet/GPT-5 decomposes the token bill into three levers that save at fundamentally different rates: tool-schema filtering dominates linearly (~21K tokens/turn), content compression compounds quadratically (~22.2% cumulative by turn 15), and history summarization carries a bounded recall re-send trap

v1 2026-08-19 (single version; 10pp). Luzhuo Chen, Jiayu Shi — affiliation printed as "Paritok" (the gateway vendor evaluated; flag: vendor paper about its own product, though its findings deflate its own compression lever). Abstract numbers claim-checked against Tables 1–4.

  • The decomposition (verified): tool-schema filtering removes a fixed block every turn (~29K→8K on a typical turn) and dominates absolutely — 392K tokens saved on a 12-request session and 1.22M on a 34-request session vs content compression's 18K/28K (Table 1). Content compression is small per turn (−1.6K/−2.1K on turns 6/8) but its cumulative savings fit ≈3,350·N² (per-N² ratios 3.35–3.53×10³ across five turns; 83,710/377,099 = 22.2% by turn 15), crossing the linear lever at N≈6.3 — the "compression helps long sessions more" claim is real but window-capped (~200K context, turns 12–15).
  • The recall trap: non-destructive recall re-sends one compressed segment per recall — bounded, not multiplicative; deleting recall entirely costs 46 turns / $1.85 (3.2×) on the tested repair task.
  • Corrected/qualified numbers: the rich-session lever ratio is 43.6:1 (1,220,000/28,000), not the claimed "∼33:1"; the intro's "∼25% single-turn / ∼39% by turn five" end-to-end figures are not traceable to any table (Table 3 shows 4.6% / 31.4% per-turn); the abstract's "∼2% of prefix per turn" understates Table 2 (4.5–5.5%); Table 4's "quality retained" is normalized to the uncompressed arm, not an absolute resolve rate.

TL;DR for BQAA: the cleanest empirical cost-attribution decomposition of production-shaped coding-agent sessions to date — cache-tier-priced, per-lever, with provider-reported usage. Directly reusable methodology: decompose savings linear-vs-quadratic by lever, treat MCP tool-block size (40 vs 90 tools) and proxy tax (~17K tokens/turn on non-official base_url) as measurement confounds. Vendor provenance and a five-turn quadratic fit are the caveats to carry.

NEW rank: Total Cost of Agency: Exact Attribution of Memory Injection Cost in Multi-Agent LLM Workflows (arXiv:2609.23790) — a five-term decomposition (base prompt / inference / memory injection / miss penalty / context accumulation) with a two-pass non-billable tokenizer count shows injected memory tokens are 13.6% of optimizer-controllable cost and rise 8.4%→27.6% as workflow depth goes 2→6 — a cost line existing observability tools do not break out

v1 2026-09-20 (single version, one day old; 8pp). Vivek Kumar Singh, Preeti Priyam, Gautam Bhowmick — independent researchers (McKinney TX / Chicago IL; no institutional affiliation). Claim-checked against Tables I–V.

  • Verified: on a self-built 200-task enterprise benchmark (5 categories × depths 2–6, balanced 8/cell — Table I arithmetic checks) against Claude Haiku/Sonnet: injection $0.003220/task vs inference $0.020408 → 13.6% of optimizer-controllable cost (Table III); full-bill share 11.8%. Injection share scales 8.4% (depth 2) → 27.6% (depth 6); linear fit R²=0.9974 at 150 tokens/depth; the quadratic coefficient is concave (−4.57) — no runaway growth. Capacity lever K=32→2 cuts injected tokens 1,074→766 (28.7%), cost −6.7%, accuracy 0.600→0.570 (within the single-seed band 0.575–0.645).

3. NEW rank: Agreement Overstates Evidence: Error Dependence in LLM Judge Consensus (arXiv:2609.22512) — LLM judges fail together: mean pairwise error correlation 0.21 across a ten-judge bank makes ten judges worth only ~3.5 independent ones, and a frontier panel (GPT-5.6-sol, Claude Opus 5, Grok 4.5) at 93.3% majority accuracy is worth ~1.4 — cross-provider diversity does not rescue you (0.42 vs 0.40 within-provider)

v1 2026-09-18 (single version). Elias Hossain, Niloofar Yousefi, Ser-Nam Lim (University of Central Florida). Claim-checked against Tables 1, 7–9, Figure 2; preregistered hypotheses including a failed non-inferiority test (22/24) are reported — unusually honest.

  • Verified: 1,133 listwise-complete RewardBench v2 pairs, 10-judge open-weight bank: ρ̄ = 0.206 → n_eff = 3.51 (eigen-rank 6.74). Frontier 3-judge bank (400 pairs): 93.3% majority accuracy yet ρ̄ = 0.56 [0.42, 0.68], n_eff = 1.41; conditional co-failure at 7.7× the independence rate. Frontier–open pairs correlate only 0.07 — mixing capability tiers buys more independent evidence than adding same-tier judges.
  • Statistical consequence (verified): on resampled 100-pair panels, a pooled-vote significance test is significant in 79% of panels vs 52% for item-level inference — pooling judge votes as independent observations manufactures significance. The "up to 28% verdict-flip" headline is the Focus N=100 maximum; Factuality is 22%, Math/Precise-IF ≤2%.
  • Corrected: the contribution bullet claims the mixed 11-judge bank has the highest majority accuracy, but the paper's own Table 1 shows GPT+Claude+Grok alone at 0.933 vs mixed 0.915 on RewardBench (mixed wins only on PKU-SafeRLHF).

TL;DR for BQAA: immediately operational for any multi-judge eval pipeline: report ρ̄ and n_eff alongside judge accuracy, never pool votes as independent observations, and prefer item-level inference. This is the statistical-discipline citation for BQAA's eval-evidence grading — and it lands the same day Codex ships the attribution fields that make per-judge provenance trackable.
