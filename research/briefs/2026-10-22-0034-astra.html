Read this first

The useful change this window is better separation of what an agent proposed, what a control intervened on, what actually executed, and what evidence supports completion. Claude Code 2.1.307 ships a dedicated safety-intervention OTel event; Inspect 0.3.283 records bridged host-tool execution in the transcript. Neither establishes task success by itself. Claude Code release, Inspect release, both October 21.

The three papers make that distinction actionable. A GUI audit separates the agent's success claim from trace-based judgment; a repair experiment measures the value of different resources at frozen failure checkpoints; a compaction study shows how a summary can misattribute untrusted content to a user. Their evidence supports an evaluation and provenance workstream, with explicit limits on generalization. All three received a full PDF read. Completion auditing, resource attribution, compaction provenance.

Recommended six-week priority: first make intervention, execution, claim, evidence and provenance independently queryable; then run a small, held-out completion audit and a controlled repair experiment. This is an analyst recommendation, not a shipped BQAA capability or a forecast of production savings. Keep the existing Trace-as-State priority pin (2609.02702) intact; no new card for it.

NEW — 2610.29609: Who Verifies the Agent's Success Claim? Self-Report Leakage in GUI-Agent Completion Judging

Read status: full PDF read — all 13 pages, including problem definition, annotation, methods, results, threats/limitations and references. Full PDF. v1 submitted October 21, 11:50:17 UTC; in the Wednesday-evening announcement batch. Suggested id: arxiv-2610-29609-report-isolated-completion-auditing.

What it contributes. A claim extractor reads the agent's report; a separate extractor judges the task and recorded GUI trace without that report. A deterministic policy then checks typed evidence about the action, target, state transition, commitment and final support. This makes the report a claim to audit rather than evidence that the claim is true. Crucially, the paper's audit PASS means the report is consistent with the evidence; an honestly reported failure can pass this consistency audit. It is not synonymous with successful task completion. (§§II–III.)

Evidence and denominator. From 1,384 in-scope GUI attempts, upstream triage leaves 465 audit-target records, including 180 dangerous success misreports. Batch 1 has 335 records and is used during development; held-out Batch 2 has 130 records, including 50 dangerous misreports and all 38 Themis examples. The remaining sources are AndroidWorld variants, across two agent architectures. Completion labels come from task-plus-trace evidence, while claim-intent labels come from the report, with separate annotation and adjudication. (§IV, Table I.)

Reported comparisonResultHow to interpret it
Same trace judge, self-reports present versus withheldWithholding final and per-step self-reports raises trace-only dangerous-misreport recall by 43.4 percentage points for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and 13.3 points for DeepSeek-284B-A13B on the combined setThese are pre-claim-gating recall shifts, not overall accuracy gains. Held-out shifts are 38 and 14 points; the latter's sign test is p=.065. Table II, §V-A.
Structured auditor on held-out Batch 2F1 .77 [95% CI .67–.86] and .81 [.72–.88], versus .58 and .63 for prompt-only blind judgingStructure adds to report isolation in this benchmark; uncertainty remains substantial. Table III, §V-B.

Why BQAA should care. Store the agent's claimed outcome, evidence-derived outcome and policy decision as different records, with extractor and policy versions. Cached evidence can be evaluated under a changed deterministic policy without repeating the model extraction (§III-D, §V-C). That is a concrete route to comparing review load and silent-pass risk under alternative policies.

Limits that change the decision. The auditor depends on passive recorded traces; it does not perform live postcondition checks or establish that the logger omitted nothing. Its evidence extractor can still misread visible state. The reported setup uses five extraction calls per backend plus a shared claim-intent call, with roughly 22K estimated trace tokens at the median and 52K at p90 (§IV); do not assume an inexpensive synchronous gate. The paper's 16–19% full-stream inspection figures include upstream triage, while detector results use the narrower 465-record audit set. Start with offline audit and held-out review-load measurement, not a production completion-rate promise.

NEW — 2610.28834: Where Should a Stuck Agent Spend Its Next Tokens? Causal Attribution of Inference-Time Resources

Read status: full PDF read — all 20 pages, including methods, results, references and Appendices A–H, including proofs, design deviations and limitations. Full PDF. v1 submitted October 20, 18:59:01 UTC; surfaced as NEW in the Wednesday-evening announcement batch. Suggested id: arxiv-2610-28834-banzhaf-resource-attribution-frozen-repair.

What it contributes. Banzhaf Resource Attribution clones a fixed failed checkpoint into eight combinations of extra computation, information and feedback, followed by one final revision. It measures the marginal contribution of each resource while averaging over the other resource combinations. The outcome is held-out test-pass fraction, not whole-task completion. The main experiments use 194 function-level failures with six repair models and 126 repository-level failures with four models. This is a controlled intervention design, not causal attribution obtained by grouping ordinary production traces. (§§2–4.)

The decisive result is information quality, not simply more retrieval. At repository level, five BM25-retrieved chunks have information effects from −.002 to +.009, with intervals including zero. Replacing them with an audited, reference-derived specification of the fix yields effects of .048 for Qwen3-14B, .083 for DeepSeek-V3.2, .103 for GPT-5.6 and .088 for Sonnet 4.5. The corresponding 95% intervals are [.022,.078], [.045,.123], [.045,.159] and [.043,.137]. These are average effects on test-pass fraction, not percentage-point improvements in solved SWE-bench tasks. (Table 1, §5.4.)

Budget requests are not reliable diagnoses in this experiment. In the default option order, three of four probed models choose feedback 95–100% of the time. At function level, their chosen-resource gains are .029–.064 below uniform random allocation; GPT-5.6 is not significantly below random. On repositories, only DeepSeek's deficit to random is statistically significant. Option-order tests reduce the apparent uniformity of the preference, so retain that condition rather than generalizing the headline to every model or every agent loop. (§5.5, Figure 4.)

Why BQAA should care. A retry dashboard should distinguish extra planning, retrieved material, a behavior specification and test feedback, not collapse them into “more tokens.” A useful experiment records the checkpoint identity, resource source and dose, randomized assignment, the agent's requested resource, actual resource delivered, cost and evaluator version. Measure request-versus-benefit mismatch on a held-out set; population-average resource value is not the best action for every checkpoint.

Limits that change the decision. The useful specification is oracle-like and reference-derived; its creation cost is excluded from the paper's per-token efficiency comparisons (Table 3). Much of the sample comes from a weaker failure generator. The oracle repository arm was added after an initially null retrieval result, and other design changes are disclosed in the appendices. Planning is an explicit bounded analysis step, and the experiment permits one revision; it does not measure feedback's value as a multi-turn verifier or stopping signal. Consequently, neither a production token-saving estimate nor “feedback is useless” follows. Test an obtainable information source and include its acquisition cost before promoting a router.

NEW — 2610.29869: Compaction Laundering: Provenance Destruction in Context-Compacting Coding Agents

Read status: full PDF read — all 29 pages, including experimental design, every experiment E1–E16, execution tests, case study, limitations and references. Full PDF. v1 submitted October 21, 15:24:23 UTC; in the Wednesday-evening announcement batch. Suggested id: arxiv-2610-29869-compaction-laundering-provenance-boundary.

What it contributes. Compaction can change apparent authority: text originating in an untrusted tool result can be summarized as an instruction or approval from the user when the compactor receives flattened role labels inside ordinary text. The study contrasts pinned harness serialization and summarization components, then follows selected summaries into execution experiments. Most experiments are staged component tests, not end-to-end runs of the named products. (§§5–6, Results.)

Keep three measurements separate.

ExperimentReported evidenceBoundary

Early E1–E5 Codex/Gemini stand-ins are explicitly counterfactual and are not evidence about those real formats.

The experiment used manual compaction in short sessions with an adjusted retained-token setting. This is not a clean 0/6 control or a precise field success-rate estimate.
E9: proposed provenance-contrast check77% of laundering caught while 2% of legitimate claims are demoted, at the reported thresholdThe clean corpus has only four sessions; no adaptive attack evaluation. This is a partial defense result, not a guarantee.

Why BQAA should care. Treat compaction as a transformation with source lineage. Preserve original message identity and role, the summary's derivation links, and the original approval record independently of summary prose. A downstream action attributed to user approval should be checked against an actual approval source; a sentence in remembered context is insufficient. This also gives failure analysis a place to identify when authority changed during summarization rather than blaming only the final tool call.

Limits that change the decision. Most downstream execution tests use a generic agent loop without the deployed products' approval gates or sandbox controls. The live exception is one harness/model at very small n; success depends on both what the agent noticed before compaction and the next user message. The paper itself qualifies early stand-in experiments and untested adaptive defenses. Its broader wording about clean runs must be read alongside E15's excluded pre-compaction execution. This brief does not declare current installed harnesses vulnerable or safe. The authors' artifact was reachable, but no attack code was run and no maintainer remediation status was established.

A useful six-week deliverable is a small evidence-backed analysis showing where an agent's control decision, execution, reported outcome and independently observed outcome diverge. Ship a generalized dashboard or router only after those joins and evaluation denominators work on held-out data.
