NEW rank: Generalized Agent Iteration (GAI) — one formal framework placing iterative policy improvement and recursive self-improvement on a single two-dial map (is the improving mechanism part of the agent × is the evaluation standard grounded outside it); anchored / goal-drift / fully self-referential polarity; four-item defect catalogue tying each RSI failure mode to a lapsed GPI guarantee

Generalized Agent Iteration: One Formal Framework for Iterative Policy Improvement and Recursive Self-Improvement, Hongyao Tang, Yi Ma, Pengyi Li, Yifu Yuan (TJU RL Lab, Tianjin University — per first-author homepage; affiliation not printed on the paper). v1 2026-09-11; no v2; cs.AI/cs.LG; ~10-page position/framework paper (39 KB PDF). Full text read: Sections 1–6, Tables 1–4, Figures 1–2, Equations 1–4, notation appendix, full references. (PDF)

The framework (verified against §3): a system is χ = (π, V, m, U, ρ) — policy, action critic, modifier (proposes system changes), modification critic (scores proposed changes), and evaluation base (the standard critics measure against); the agent is Ag ⊆ {π,V,m,U,ρ}, the modifiable subset. Generalized Agent Iteration alternates agent evaluation (critics score against ρ) and agent improvement (ξ ~ m(·|χ)). The two dials:

  • Dial 1 — is m ∈ Ag? If the modifier is external and fixed, the cycle is exactly GPI (Eq. 2); if m is itself modifiable, the proposal carries the next modifier and critic — "recursion closes at m with no external meta-layer" (Eq. 3). This dial alone draws the GPI/RSI boundary.
  • Dial 2 — is the evaluation standard grounded outside the agent? Anchored (external content, ρ ∉ Ag — Gödel machine, STOP, Darwin Gödel Machine, SICA, Polaris, Hyperagents), Goal Drift (external content but ρ ∈ Ag — the agent may rewrite the measuring instrument; Red Queen Gödel Machine), or Fully Self-Referential (ρ empty or dependent on U itself; critic equation reduces to internal self-consistency). Remark 2 concedes: a learned reward model counts as anchored wherever it sits outside the agent — "the dial asks where the standard sits, not how faithful it is to the goal."
  • The defect catalogue (§5, Table 4) — the analytical payload: (1) search over candidate selves (m ∈ Ag; improvement no longer cheap/monotone), (2) self-evaluation (U ∈ Ag; the evaluator is no longer outside what it evaluates), (3) ungrounded base (objective no longer evaluated from outside; linked to the delusion box), (4) goal drift (ρ ∈ Ag; linked to utility self-modification literature). Each defect is tied to a specific GPI condition that lapses.

Mandatory qualifications: pure position/framework paper — zero experiments, zero theorems (self-acknowledged: "the reduction from RSI to GPI is prose rather than a proposition with explicit hypotheses"; Eq. 4's self-consistency conditions are "characterization conditions, not enforced updates"). Do not attribute the 58.33% / 78.26% non-monotonicity numbers to this paper — they are cited from RSIBench-Data (Meng et al. 2026, 2607.25886). The four "defects" are structural/definitional claims, not measured failures. Contribution is a taxonomy with notation, close to distinctions in the 2026 RSI surveys it cites; arXiv-only, unreviewed, single-lab author group; affiliation from author homepage, not the paper.

BQAA/ADK application: the value is vocabulary for eval-grounding analysis, landing the same week the industry section keeps re-discovering its defects: ImpossibleRubrics' verifier-swap swing (0634) is a self-evaluation/base-grounding problem; Promptfoo's vacuous threshold and grader-failure inversions (this slot) are ungrounded-base failures in production form. Concrete uses: (1) for any BQAA self-improvement or harness-tuning loop, ask the two dial questions in design review — is the modifier inside the agent, and where does the standard physically live? (2) treat "can the agent edit its own yardstick?" (ρ ∈ Ag) as a named audit item — goal drift is the formal name for rubric/metric mutation by the system being measured; (3) the defect catalogue gives a checklist shape for agent-loop design reviews (candidate-self search cost, self-evaluation, base grounding, drift) — adopt as review headings, not as measured results.
