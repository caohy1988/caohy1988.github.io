1. Arize Alyx connects Signal trace investigation, Managed Agents, Decision Judges and dashboards

2026-11-20T17:34:00Z (official Arize RSS), product update. Alyx can now inspect issues that Signal finds in production traces, open the underlying traces and recommend follow-up investigations. Users can also create, update, pause and resume Managed Agents; create and edit Decision Judges, including evaluation questions, classifications and variable mappings; and build or explain dashboards for latency, cost and evaluation results. This is a workflow expansion inside Arize AX, not a measured claim that the agent's recommendations are correct.

BQAA relevance: the important join is recurring trace issue → evidence inspection → managed investigation or judge → dashboard. Keep the agent's suggested configuration separate from the reviewed, active version and measured outcome.

2. Standard Bank automates evidence gathering and policy checks for generative-AI risk reviews

2026-11-20T20:37:31Z (official AWS ML RSS), production customer story. A pre-assessment agent maps repository and documentation evidence to about 40 intake questions; the pipeline also tests live application behavior and uses narrow LLM-as-a-judge checks against more than 300 controls derived from standards and internal policy. Each judgment retains its evidence, inconsistent repeated findings are flagged, reviewers can amend answers with a business justification, and results feed reports, mitigation work and a CloudWatch dashboard.

AWS and Standard Bank report that a manual two-week review became an approximately two-hour comprehensive report across six assessments, with a 14% improvement in use-case risk scores after remediation. These are six operational assessments, not a controlled judge-accuracy study; the post explicitly says the assessment does not certify regulatory compliance and that the risk committee retains approval.

BQAA relevance: store control and policy-clause IDs, evidence spans, live-test inputs and responses, judge repetitions, consistency flags, reviewer overrides and remediation outcomes as one auditable record.

3. OpenAI reports adaptive evaluation of reasoning-trace extraction defenses

2026-11-20T10:00:00Z (official OpenAI RSS), safety research and deployed safeguards. The post covers request-level behavior training, input and output classifiers, API restrictions and campaign-level investigation. Output classifiers inspect assistant messages and tool-call arguments, while API controls restrict reuse of protected reasoning across conversations, users, workspaces, organizations and model families.

In the article's adaptive black-box evaluation table, aggregated across six attacker models, average attack success for extracting protected reasoning from an earlier request fell from 54.0% with the prior Astra safeguards to 0.2% with the updated Astra safeguards; extraction from the current request fell from 22.9% to 0.9%. These are internal automated red-team results for reasoning extraction, not proof that all distillation or cyber-capability transfer is prevented.

BQAA relevance: safety telemetry should cover tool arguments as well as visible responses, separate static replay from adaptive attacks, and track detection-to-enforcement time without retaining protected reasoning content in analytics.

4. Snowflake Decision enters public preview through Cortex AI_COMPLETE

2026-11-20 (dated release note; date-only), public preview. Snowflake Decision supports classification, ordered scoring and probability-style truth assessments over unstructured data. One AI_COMPLETE request can evaluate multiple independent criteria and return structured answers with probabilities and confidence values for routing, human review or fallback processing, including agent-routing workflows expressed in SQL.

The documentation advertises approximately 1.58 ms processing per row and $0.042 per million tokens; these are vendor figures, not an independent accuracy or calibration benchmark. The product opportunity is the confidence-bearing decision record, not the headline latency alone.

Academy

NEW

1. arXiv:2611.25583 — DriftGuard: Online Memory-Drift Detection via Causal Attribution for LLM Agents

Full PDF read: every section and appendix, including methodology, all result tables, limitations, proofs and reproduction details (24 pages). DriftGuard constructs three paired counterfactual replays per candidate memory from a shared pre-action state. Component-wise votes produce a four-part memory drift score: action change, attack removal, violation removal and task recovery. A learned online risk model estimates those components from pre-action inputs only, avoiding online counterfactual agent or tool rollouts.

  • Detection (Table 1): on MPBench with the GPT-5.6 Luna victim, DriftGuard reaches 0.2415 AUPRC versus 0.1297 for the strongest few-shot LLM-ranker baseline, an 86.2% relative improvement reported by the authors. This is still a low absolute AUPRC and should not be described as near-perfect detection.
  • Defense (Table 5): on Mem2ActBench under Sleeper Memory, attack success falls from 77.41% to 3.25% while task success rises from 11.50% to 56.13%. Under Direct Override, attack success falls from 46.68% to 5.28% and task success rises from 47.71% to 77.28%.
  • Serving cost (Table 6): mean additional latency is 1.52±0.21 seconds and 986±68 defense tokens per task, versus 6.19±0.53 seconds and 2,603±195 tokens for A-MemGuard; DriftGuard uses no extra victim-agent rollout online.
  • Limits (Section 6): offline supervision still requires agent, tool and evaluator replays; single-memory interventions miss interactions among multiple memories; and neutralization is not guaranteed safe under domain shift or unseen attacks. Start with offline replay labels and shadow scoring, not automatic blocking.

2. arXiv:2611.26525 — Backtracking Agent Applications Security Incidents (Origin)

Full PDF read: every section and appendix, including query semantics, implementation, case studies, tool-failure analysis and labeling evaluation (33 pages). Origin's cross-layer dependency graph joins a function plane, labeled with Perceiving, Planning & Acting, Recalling & Reflecting, Prompt Kitchen or Unknown regions, to a variable-level taint plane. Analysts issue region-level queries followed by variable-level queries to locate a compromise entry point (CEP) and its propagation path.

  • Case studies (Table 3): across five open-source agent applications and eight IPI or memory/context-poisoning incidents, the analyst found every CEP with one candidate per incident. Five incidents required two queries and three multi-phase incidents required four.
  • Inspection scope (Table 5): region queries retained 6.49%–35.76% of the full variable plane, a 64.24%–93.51% reduction in nodes requiring inspection. Region inference precision and recall were imperfect (Table 6), so this is analyst guidance rather than autonomous root-cause proof.
  • Cost and labels (Tables 7–8): per-incident query evaluation averaged under 90 ms and peak memory stayed below 7 GB; graph construction dominated offline time. Developers labeled only 2.4%–8.9% of application functions.
  • Limits (Sections 4.1 and 11): the work targets predefined C1 workflows, not LLM-driven C2 agent loops; framework-induced indirect calls can remove paths; and human validation remains necessary. The labeling-speed/accuracy study has only four participants and should not be generalized broadly.

3. arXiv:2611.26077 — Same Judgment, Different Scores: Numerical Scoring Topology in LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluation

Full PDF read: every section and appendix, including all 13 scoring topologies, multilingual and judge-generalization experiments, mitigation prompts, limitations and additional result tables. The main design holds responses, criteria, prompts and judge fixed while changing scale width, granularity, offset, symmetry and boundaries.

  • Task sensitivity (Table 3): judgment-intensive benchmarks average 19.9% of matched instances differing by more than 10 normalized points and 7.9% by more than 20, versus 11.8% and 3.6% for answer-verifiable tasks. Normalization therefore does not make scoring schemes interchangeable.
  • Boundary mechanism (Tables 4–5): unstable non-round maxima can pull judges toward salient values below the declared maximum; for the 6.2–7.2 range, 7.0 accounts for 58.5% of judgments and normalizes to 80, not 100.
  • Mitigation (Table 10): explicitly assigning the maximum to a fully correct answer reduces MATH-500 disagreement for 6.2–7.2 from 64.8% to 3.2%, and LiveCodeBench disagreement from 93.7% to 28.6%. Other benchmarks retain substantial disagreement, so explicit-max prompting is a mitigation, not a universal repair.
  • Ranking nuance (Tables 16–17): all eight response generators retain the same ordering on MATH-500 and Arena-Hard (Kendall's τ=1.00) even when absolute scores shift materially. Preserve raw score, scale bounds, normalization formula, judge model/prompt/language and both absolute-score and ranking-stability metrics. The study covers pointwise numerical scoring, not pairwise or fully rubric-guided protocols.

Six-week EV

  • Evidence-linked assessment ledger (high EV, 2–4 weeks). Join control/policy-clause ID, artifact and evidence span, live-test input/output, judge question/model/version, repeated-run consistency, reviewer override, mitigation and recheck outcome. Report judge agreement and remediation separately from regulatory status.
  • Termination and approval semantics (high EV, 1–2 weeks). Preserve raw and normalized finish reasons, preliminary/final tool-result state, approval request/decision/signature, denial source, replay rejection and producer SDK version. Add replay tests for denied calls and cross-provider dashboards for tool_call, refusal, context exhaustion and errors.
  • Memory-drift shadow attribution (high EV, 3–6 weeks). Record pre-action snapshot, retrieved memory IDs and ranks, candidate context, structured next action, offline counterfactual branch outcomes, risk-model version, threshold, rewrite and final task/security outcome. Measure AUPRC, ASR/TSR, latency and defense tokens before considering online intervention.
  • Cross-layer incident provenance (high EV, 4–6 weeks). Add trace-to-source-function mappings and information-source labels so an incident query can move from agent region to function and variable path. Track CEP candidate count, graph nodes inspected, query latency and unresolved framework dispatch rather than claiming automated root cause.
  • Judge scale and confidence registry (high EV, 1–3 weeks). Store raw score, minimum/maximum, normalization formula, explicit-max prompt version, judge language/model and confidence calibration. Run topology-sensitivity checks before comparing scores, and publish ranking stability separately from absolute-score movement.
  • Reasoning-leak safeguards without reasoning retention (medium-high EV, 2–4 weeks). Instrument classifier decisions for assistant and tool-argument channels, cross-context reasoning-access denials, static/adaptive attack type and detection-to-enforcement latency. Keep protected reasoning itself out of analytics payloads.

TL;DRs for BQAA

  • Arize Alyx: link recurring trace issue, evidence inspection, managed investigation, judge configuration and dashboard outcome; suggestion and active configuration are different states.
  • Standard Bank: an auditable control-to-evidence-to-remediation record is the useful primitive; two hours is report generation across six assessments, not proven judge accuracy or compliance certification.
  • OpenAI: reasoning-trace safety needs tool-argument coverage and adaptive attack evaluation, not only final-answer classifiers.
  • Snowflake Decision: SQL-native probabilities and confidence values can drive review/fallback routing, but calibration still needs measurement on the customer's data.
  • LangChain.js Core: raw provider reasons and normalized finish reasons must coexist with SDK-versioned semantics.
  • Vercel AI SDK: rotate the approval secret for the denied-call signature fix and regression-test replay before treating approval telemetry as proof of enforcement.
  • DriftGuard: counterfactual memory attribution belongs offline first; online risk scoring needs its own latency, token, false-positive and task-utility measurements.
  • Origin: connect agent traces to code-level provenance for incident backtracking, while retaining unresolved-path and dynamic-agent limitations.
  • Scoring topology: never compare normalized judge scores without the scale, boundaries, prompt and ranking-stability check.
