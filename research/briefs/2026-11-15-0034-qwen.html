Academy

NEW ranks (reserve promotion)

arXiv:2611.14980 — CoLFA: Collaboration-Guided Lightweight Failure Attribution for LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems (reserve promotion)

Yufei Luo, Yuchen Ling, Chunrong Fang (Nanjing University); v1 [cs.CL], submitted 11 Nov 2026; code announced for release at github.com/luoyufeihaha/CoLFA.

Full PDF read — all 23 pages, every part: abstract, §1 Introduction, §2 Related Work (2.1–2.2), §3 Method (3.1 task definition/overview, 3.2 Handoff-Guided Step Expansion, 3.3 Cross-Referenced Content Organization), §4 Experiments (4.1 setup, 4.2 main results, 4.3 ablation, 4.4 analysis of attribution inputs), §5 Conclusion, Limitations, References, Appendix A (A.1 execution-link rules, A.2 expansion scope; Tables 5–6), Appendix B (B.1 record organization, B.2 judgment prompts; Algorithm 1), Appendix C (C.1 coverage/statistics Table 7, C.2 annotations and input protocols), Appendix D baselines, Appendix E (E.1 token breakdown Table 8, E.2 complete ablation Table 9, E.3 paired bootstrap Table 10), Appendix F (F.1 coverage Table 11, F.2 threshold sensitivity Table 12, F.3 textual basis Table 13, F.4 input size Table 14, F.5 step selection Table 15), Appendix G (Prompts 1–2). Not truncated.

The problem. Failure attribution = given a failed multi-agent trace, name the decisive error step (earliest uncorrected error decisive for the final failure) and the responsible agent. Two properties of collaborative traces fight you (§1): Interleaved Steps — the steps that produce, hand off and process a result belong to different agents at different times, so chronological order does not reveal the connections; Cluttered Content — the evidence is buried in reasoning, actions and environment feedback, and is repeated, paraphrased or modified as it passes between steps. Prior methods split into single-pass trace analysis (cheap, weak localization) and candidate-search/multi-round investigation (stronger, expensive). §4.2 frames the question the paper actually answers: can you beat the accuracy/cost trade-off rather than move along it?

The method (§3). CoLFA's insight is that the same collaboration that creates interleaving also leaves the evidence for un-interleaving: handoff records (assignments, agent mentions, speaker changes) and content correspondence (recurring numbers, identifiers, quoted text with inverse-document-frequency specificity ≥ θ=1.4, i.e. occurring in ≲¼ of a trace's records). Two components:

  • Handoff-Guided Step Expansion (§3.2) starts from the final result and walks backward, splitting steps into output steps (all decision steps of the agent authoring the final decision), production steps (upstream steps linked by handoff/content correspondence, expanded recursively until stable), and supply steps (directly linked steps by the remaining agents, where expansion stops). S = So ∪ Sh ∪ Sp are the "formation steps" — the only candidates the model may answer from.
  • Cross-Referenced Content Organization (§3.3) keeps a separate record per execution (so each decision is judged in its own context), replaces repeated ≥12-word spans with pointers to their primary source (content references), lists referenced earlier step indices and authors inline (step references), and adds a chronological trace overview of the whole execution so an error's role can be judged against what happened after it. Actions are never truncated; reasoning/tool output are truncated (1,500/6,000 chars Who&When, 500/1,500 LongRCA — Table 5) with omissions marked. Attribution is a single model call.

Results. Five settings across three backbones (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash for the four main settings; Claude Sonnet 5.5 for a fifth on Who&When) against ten rerun baselines (All-at-Once, A2P, CORRECT, Step-by-Step, Binary Search, ECHO, RAFFLES, DeepDebug, DCFA, CHIEF).

  • Who&When (Table 1): CoLFA Overall Step 44.57% (DeepSeek) / 46.74% (GLM) at 6.9K / 7.1K tokens, CoP 16 / 15; best baseline CORRECT 41.30% / 44.57% at 22.7K / 20.6K tokens, CoP 55 / 46. CoP cut 27–30% vs the lowest baseline CoP.
  • LongRCA (Table 2): Root Exact 21.84% / 23.60%, Role 51.58% / 52.28%, ±5 32.81% / 36.23%, MAE 35.54 / 35.42 steps, at 22.4K / 22.0K tokens (CoP 103K / 93K) vs CORRECT 18.86% at 114.5K and RAFFLES 21.75% at 212.3K. CoP cut 71–74% vs the lowest baseline CoP.
  • Claude Sonnet 5.5 (Table 3): Overall Step 46.20%, Agent 69.57%, 10.7K tokens, CoP 23 vs A2P 43.48% / CoP 31 — the organization transfers to a frontier backbone.
  • Headline (§1, §4.2): beats each setting's most accurate baseline by 1.84–3.26% with 66–90% fewer tokens.
  • Ablation (Tables 4, 9): removing either component lowers accuracy and raises cost; w/o Both on LongRCA costs 2.32× tokens and 2.83× CoP and drops Root Exact 21.84→17.89 (DeepSeek). Both levels of organization contribute independently.
  • Length scaling (Figure 3, §4.2): CoP is lowest in every trace-length group; in the longest (>64K tokens) CoP is 75–83% lower than the cheapest baseline, with the highest Root Exact there. Table 14: attribution input is 40% of complete-trace tokens in aggregate — 42% smaller in the shortest quintile, 69% smaller in the longest; log-log slopes 0.57–0.68 (all < 1), so cost grows sublinearly with trace length.
  • Cost structure (Table 8, §E.1): input tokens are >86% of total consumption for every method, and reduced input accounts for 90.5–99.4% of CoLFA's savings. Content references appear in 99.9% of multi-agent traces and replace 24.0% of pre-truncation characters (Table 13).

The caveats, which are the most transferable part.

  • The accuracy win is significant only on long traces. Table 10 (trace-level paired bootstrap, 10,000 replicates, 95% percentile CIs vs the most accurate baseline in each setting): LongRCA [0.44, 5.53]% (DeepSeek, +2.98) and [0.28, 3.51]% (GLM, +1.84) — above zero; Who&When [−1.72, 9.78]% (+3.26) and [−2.80, 8.15]% (+2.17) — include zero. §E.3 states this plainly ("the confidence intervals are wider and include zero", n=184 vs n=1,140), but §4.2 in the main text quotes only the two LongRCA intervals. Read the cost result as robust and the accuracy result as robust on long traces only.
  • A hard structural recall ceiling. §A.2/Table 6: CoLFA's scope reaches 78.9% coverage at 49.3% mean step share, while retaining all decision steps of the output and upstream agents reaches 87.4% at 71.9%. Of the 20,126 upstream steps CoLFA excludes, 76 are annotated decisive error steps — those are unreachable by construction, no matter how good the judgment prompt is. Pruning by lineage trades recall for cost, explicitly.
  • Coverage, not reasoning, is the binding constraint. Table 15 (matched step counts, 1,032 multi-agent LongRCA traces): CoLFA covers 78.9% of decisive errors vs 61.5% (most-recent) and 57.1% (random); for errors authored by agents other than the output agent — 65.3% of decisive errors at decision steps (Table 11) — coverage is 72.4% vs 44.8% and 37.7%, and Exact tracks it monotonically (21.12/22.97 vs 17.93/18.12 vs 17.15/16.18). §4.4: "Coverage is a prerequisite for correct localization."
  • Recency is a bad prior. Table 11/F.1: formation steps beat most-recent selection by 26.1–27.1 points across θ ∈ [1.0, 1.8] (Table 12), with the widest gaps on SWE-bench Pro (+52.3) and Terminal-Bench 2 (+28.6), where errors occur earlier in the trace.
  • Stated limitations: expansion scopes are defined only for delegation/handoff and broadcast-group-chat configurations; applicability to other collaboration configurations and record formats is uninvestigated. And no repair validation — "Whether attribution results support effective repair and improve task completion remains to be directly validated by correcting the relevant steps and examining the outcomes of subsequent reruns."
  • Internal inconsistency, disclosed: the abstract claims "improves accuracy by up to 37.50% and reduces token consumption by up to 97.6%", while §4.2 reports largest per-split step-localization gains of 47.62% (Alg.) and 25.86% (Hand.) against individual (weakest) baselines. The abstract also says "five settings … three backbone models" where §4.1–4.2 describe four main settings on two backbones; the fifth setting and third backbone are the Table 3 Claude Sonnet 5.5 comparison. The defensible headline is the +1.84–3.26% / 66–90%-fewer-tokens figure measured against the most accurate baseline.

Benchmarks (Table 7): Who&When Alg. 126 traces (8.7 steps mean, CaptainAgent, broadcast group chat) and Hand. 58 traces (51.6 steps, Magentic-One, delegation); LongRCA 1,140 traces (156.0 steps mean) from TravelPlanner 685, WebArena Verified 177, SWE-bench Pro 128, VitaBench 108, Terminal-Bench 2 42. Who&When runs in the benchmark's With-Ground-Truth setting (reference answers supplied); LongRCA does not release them and no method receives them.

Six-week EV

Inspect 0.3.290 (Progress) — moderate, near-term. Within six weeks this is directly usable: if BQAA ingests Inspect eval logs, stable message ids plus tool→proposing-message links remove heuristic join logic, and judge-model usage landing in the eval's own accounting closes a cost-attribution gap that otherwise shows up as unexplained spend. The denominator fix (#6476) matters for any pass-rate metric computed over errored/cancelled evals — without it, totals silently shrink and rates inflate. EV is realization of a known direction rather than a new capability, so it will not change your roadmap; it will remove a class of join and accounting bugs. Risk: low. Watch whether the message-id contract survives the next bridge refactor, since 0.3.280→0.3.283→0.3.290 have all touched the same code path.

CoLFA (reserve promotion) — high, and mostly methodological. The transferable result is not the 44–47% number; it is the shape of the finding: for agent-trace attribution, input tokens are >86% of cost, coverage of the candidate set is the binding constraint on accuracy, and organizing evidence along handoff/lineage edges dominates both reading-everything and reading-recency. That maps almost one-to-one onto a warehouse-native design: BigQuery can materialize handoff and content-correspondence edges as a joinable graph, which lets you build the "formation step" candidate set in SQL and send a model 40% of the trace tokens instead of all of them — with a measurable coverage figure (78.9%) telling you in advance what fraction of decisive errors you have made unreachable. Over six weeks, expect this to argue for lineage-first trace schemas (record the handoff edge and the proposing-message pointer at write time) rather than post-hoc model reasoning over flat logs; the catalog's agenteval card ("attribution works when the schema records the edges") is the same lesson from the other direction, and CoLFA quantifies it. Two honest discounts: the accuracy gain is only significant on long traces (Who&When CIs straddle zero), and the 76-excluded-decisive-errors figure is a recall ceiling you inherit the moment you prune. Also unproven end-to-end — no repair/rerun validation, so "better attribution" is not yet "better outcomes".

TL;DRs for BQAA

  • Inspect AI 0.3.290 (stable, in-window): one message id end-to-end through the agent bridge and the log, tool events record the proposing message, active_model_event_id() exposes the in-flight attempt, judge-model usage folds into the eval, and errored/cancelled evals keep their total sample count — trace joinability plus cost and denominator integrity.
  • CoLFA (arXiv:2611.14980, reserve promotion): organize attribution input along handoff and content-correspondence edges and you localize the decisive error step from ~40% of trace tokens — best accuracy at lowest cost in all five settings, but the accuracy gain is statistically significant only on the 1,140-trace LongRCA (Who&When bootstrap CIs include zero).
  • CoLFA's structural ceiling: of 20,126 upstream steps it prunes, 76 are annotated decisive errors — lineage-based pruning has a recall bound you should measure before adopting it.
  • Coverage beats recency: formation steps cover 72.4% of decisive errors authored by non-output agents vs 44.8% for most-recent and 37.7% for random at matched step counts — "look at the last few steps" is a demonstrably bad attribution prior, worst exactly where errors happen early (SWE-bench Pro, Terminal-Bench 2).
  • Input tokens dominate: >86% of attribution cost is input for every method tested, and 90.5–99.4% of CoLFA's savings come from the input side — cost optimization for trace analytics is a context-selection problem, not a decoding problem.

    • arxiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14980
    • card text: Reserve promotion, full PDF read (all 23 pages incl. Appendices A–G, Tables 1–15). CoLFA reframes multi-agent failure attribution as an input-organization problem: Handoff-Guided Step Expansion walks backward from the final result along handoff records and content-correspondence edges to build a "formation step" candidate set, and Cross-Referenced Content Organization keeps a separate record per execution with content/step references plus a whole-trace overview, so a single model call judges each decision in its own context. Across five settings and three backbones on Who&When and LongRCA it posts the best exact step-localization accuracy at the lowest token cost — beating each setting's most accurate baseline by 1.84–3.26% with 66–90% fewer tokens (Table 1: 44.57%/46.74% Overall Step at 6.9K/7.1K tokens; Table 2: 21.84%/23.60% Root Exact at 22.4K/22.0K; Table 3: 46.20% on Claude Sonnet 5.5), with attribution input at 40% of complete-trace tokens and 69% smaller on the longest quintile (Table 14). Two caveats are the transferable part: the accuracy gain is significant only on long traces (Table 10 paired bootstrap — LongRCA CIs [0.44, 5.53]% and [0.28, 3.51]% exclude zero, Who&When CIs [−1.72, 9.78]% and [−2.80, 8.15]% include zero, and §4.2 quotes only the LongRCA intervals), and pruning by lineage carries a hard recall ceiling — of 20,126 excluded upstream steps, 76 are annotated decisive error steps (§A.2/Table 6; coverage 78.9% at 49.3% mean step share). Coverage, not reasoning, binds accuracy (Table 15: 72.4% vs 44.8% recent vs 37.7% random for errors authored by non-output agents), and input tokens are >86% of attribution cost for every method with 90.5–99.4% of the savings on the input side (Table 8). Not validated for repair/rerun outcomes; scopes defined only for delegation/handoff and broadcast-group-chat configurations.
