NEW rank: Coding Agents Have Converged — a frozen-leaderboard audit shows SWE-bench Verified's top 30 has zero statistically separable adjacent pairs (0/29 McNemar, smallest p=0.545); the same test separates 14/23 on the Test split; ships comparison-set-relative n_eff and a five-step audit protocol agent-eval teams can run tomorrow

Coding Agents Have Converged: Why the SWE-bench Leaderboard Can No Longer Order Its Top Entries, and What to Measure Instead, Fengshuo Liu, Ying Liu, Ruize Sun, Lie Luo, Siyuan Guo. ADMA 2026 (Special Session on Responsible Data Intelligence), camera-ready; v1 2026-09-15; no v2. Full text read (15 pp) including protocol, robustness checks, limitations. (PDF)

No models are run — this is a pure audit of the 254 publicly posted SWE-bench leaderboard submissions (134 Verified) with published per-instance harness verdicts, frozen 2026-07-30. Verified findings:

  • The frontier is a tie, exactly (§1, Table 2): the leading two Verified entries both resolve 396/500; six more sit within 14. The top 10 share 285 all-solved + 51 none-solved instances — only 164 of 500 discriminate among them. Effective size n_eff(S) (instances solved by neither all nor none of the comparison set): 0.33 at K=10, 0.07 (36 instances) at K=2, vs 0.94 across all 134 submissions.
  • Ranks do not separate (§5): exact paired McNemar on adjacent pairs — 0 of 29 Verified top-30 pairs separable at α=0.05 (median gap 1 instance, median discordance 61; smallest adjacent p = 0.545); same result on Lite. Counter-test: on the harder Test split (top-24 scores 0.2%–52.6%), 14 of 23 adjacent pairs separate — the audit method itself discriminates where ability spans a range; the null is the benchmark's, not the statistic's.
  • Solutions nest rather than complement (Table 3): median frontier nesting 0.935 vs a score-implied random-allocation baseline of 0.774 — top systems solve largely the same instances, so "ensemble the leaders" buys less than raw scores suggest (union of top-2 = 414 vs 396 best single).
  • Scaffold is a large confound (§4): within-model scaffold range median 78 instances (15.6pp); claude-3-5-sonnet across 9 scaffolds spans 168→317 (29.8pp) — wider than the entire top-30 spread (8.8pp) and ~2.9× the repeat-submission replication floor. Model×scaffold interactions Holm-significant in 6 of 9 tests with two sign reversals. And the metadata to even study this barely exists: only 61/134 (46%) Verified submissions carry a single usable model tag.
  • Budget arithmetic (§5–6): separating leading adjacent pairs at observed discordance needs a median 52× ≈ 26,000 same-character instances — but ~900 targeted instances suffice at 0.2 imbalance; tier-reporting instead of ranks is recommended, with explicit non-equivalence/non-transitivity caveats. Supporting psychometrics: 2PL-IRT information peaks below median system ability (2.3× more information about median than frontier systems); KR-20 collapses 0.994 → 0.475 at the top 10. Data/code released (github.com/Adkid-Zephyr/resolution-audit, frozen inputs + hashes).

Mandatory qualifications: the title overreaches — by the authors' own framing this is one comparison set on one leaderboard snapshot, not "coding agents" generally; the model×scaffold factorial is sparse and hand-normalized (55 occupied cells of 30×34; 54% of submissions unplaceable), so scaffold effects are observational, not causal; one run per submission means run-to-run variance is unseparated (McNemar conditions on observed submissions, resamples instances only); the tier groupings are rule-dependent (3 tiers uncorrected vs 19/11 groups under Holm — reported honestly as sensitivity); the headline "0 of 29" is partly structural (McNemar ignores concordant instances), which the authors disclose.

BQAA/ADK application: the most directly reusable methodology paper since Continual Search. (1) n_eff(S) is a dashboard metric BQAA can compute today over any internal eval verdict matrix: the fraction of items that are all-pass or all-fail relative to the candidate set under decision — when it collapses, the suite has stopped resolving the comparisons you actually make, regardless of what aggregate scores say. (2) Paired testing is free when you own the harness — every candidate graded on identical instances licenses exact McNemar; adjacent-pair separability and discordant-pair budgets should be standard output of any internal model/harness leaderboard. (3) Scaffold provenance as a schema requirement: scaffold swings (29.8pp) exceeding the entire top-30 spread, plus 54% unplaceable metadata, is a concrete argument for recording structured (model, scaffold, version, attempts, harness-hash) per run as first-class telemetry. (4) "Tiers, not ranks" plus cost/latency per tier is a ready-made reporting pattern for model-selection surfaces; the instance-budget inversion (~900 targeted vs ~26k same-character) is a measurable curation SLA for new eval tasks. (5) The §6.2 checklist for internal benchmarks (evaluate the deployed pair, pre-register missing-verdict handling, grade from artifacts not agent self-reports, re-mine saturated items) reads like a requirements doc for an agent-eval platform.
