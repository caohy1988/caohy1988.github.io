  • #24635 adds durationMs predicates.

1. NEW rank: arXiv 2609.27334: Just-in-Time Memory — Learning to Curate Task-Adaptive Memory for LLM Agents

What they did: Salesforce AI Research (Zhou, Li, Liu, Yavuz, Joty).

  • The memory bank stores complete raw trajectories with no summarisation.
  • A trajectory enters the bank only if the executor, acting as judge, says it succeeded.
  • BM25 retrieves the top 3 trajectories by task description. A curator then writes a payload specific to the current task, and a frozen executor acts on it.
  • The curator is Qwen3-8B, trained with GRPO for 100 steps on task reward alone.
  • Benchmarks: ALFWorld (140 test tasks), WebShop (500) and τ²-bench. Executors: Qwen3-8B, Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-5.4. Scores are the mean ± std over 3 task orderings.

Findings:

  • Trained curator vs SkillOS (write-time skill memory), Qwen3-8B executor: ALFWorld 77.4 vs 61.2 SR (+16.2) and WebShop 32.8 vs 16.5 (+16.3) (Table 1).
  • Efficiency (ALFWorld, GPT-5.4): input tokens drop 50.3–56.3% and executor steps drop 28.4–31.4% relative to ReasoningBank and SkillOS (Table 4).
  • Raw storage matters: distilling trajectories at write time drops WebShop SR by up to 8.2 points (Table 9).
  • The curator transfers across executors: trained with Qwen, it gets 86.7 with a GPT-5.4 executor, vs 88.1 when trained on GPT-5.4 itself (Table 3).
  • τ²-bench: 75.6 vs 71.7, using the untrained GPT-5.4 curator. On Airline it scores 63.5, below the no-memory baseline of 65.0 (Table 2).

Caveats:

  • Baseline provenance. The Table 1 "No Memory" rows (Qwen 47.9 / 33.3 / 9.8, including std) are identical to the "SkillOS Reported" rows in Table 6, and the authors' own reproduction is lower. The baselines appear to be copied from the SkillOS paper, and it is not stated whether they were rerun.
  • The write gate relies on an LLM judge.
  • Three orderings, std only, no significance tests.
  • No code or data link.

TL;DR for BQAA: This is the argument for BigQuery as agent memory: store the raw trace and curate at read time. Log task_description (the retrieval key), the retrieved trajectory IDs and ranks, payload_tokens, the curator and executor models, and judge_success separately from verifier_success. A memory bank gated on a judge's verdict inherits that judge's errors.

2. NEW rank: arXiv 2609.26637: Capable yet Parsimonious — Extracting and Characterizing Hidden Chain-of-Thought in Frontier Models

What they did: Aalborg University Copenhagen and Seafill (Luo, Ren, Yu, Li, Li, Bjerva).

  • They register a forced-reasoning(reasoning: string) tool and force the first call with tool_choice. Each call gets a fixed "Received" reply, then tool choice returns to auto. Native reasoning is off, or at the lowest effort.
  • Models: Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-6 Astra, each run with no reasoning, native-high reasoning and forced reasoning.
  • Benchmarks: MATH (80 problems: 47 APEX plus 33 HMMT Feb-2026) and 100-problem subsets of LiveCodeBench and HLE, all via OpenRouter.
  • Validated on open models (DeepSeek-V4-Flash and GLM-5.2, 10 rollouts each) against their native chain of thought.

Findings:

  • The reasoning-token counter misses reasoning sent through tool arguments (Table 6). With the think-here prompt:
  • GPT-6 Astra produces 1,754.66 output tokens of visible tool reasoning.
  • The provider reports 13.65 native reasoning tokens.
  • 93.75% of calls report zero reasoning tokens.
  • Forced reasoning is close to native, but not equal (Table 1): Astra MATH 93.8 vs 97.5, Sol MATH 91.3 vs 97.5, Opus HLE 27.0 vs 33.0. The abstract's "matches" overstates this.
  • Open-model check: DeepSeek-V4-Flash scores 30.5 with no reasoning, 73.3 forced and 70.0 native.
  • Reasoning shape: Astra's reasoning trees are far narrower than Sonnet 5's (median 27 vs 136 nodes; width 5 vs 28.5) at similar median depth (Table 3).
  • Transplanted traces: Astra's reasoning states the correct answer on 73 of 80 problems. Yet 15 of 18 Haiku 4.5 errors happen with that answer already in its input (Table 10).
  • Refusal: extraction succeeded on 0% of runs for Claude Opus 5, Fable 5 and Fable 5.1 (App. D.3.1).

Caveats:

  • Closed-model results are one rollout per question, with no seeds or CIs.
  • Samples are small (80 or 100 problems), and prompts are tuned per model.
  • Episode labels come from a Claude Opus 5 judge that was piloted on only 200 units.
  • A table caption is wrong.
  • Code was shared only with the OpenAI and Anthropic security teams.

Why it is ranked despite the small n: the Table 6 result is a direct telemetry measurement, not a judge-scored one. It is the observability claim that matters for BQAA.

TL;DR for BQAA: Do not use reasoning_tokens as a governance or cost signal on its own. Also log per call:

  • tool_choice_mode (forced or auto)
  • reasoning_effort
  • tool argument size (tool_arg_chars or tokens)
  • a flag for scratchpad or no-op tools

Apply the same redaction policy to tool arguments as to thinking blocks.
