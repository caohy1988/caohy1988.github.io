1. NEW rank: Corrupt Plans, Clean Traces — plan injection launders adversarial reasoning into clean first-person CoT, evading monitors 25–82%; more monitor context or compute can make detection worse

Corrupt Plans, Clean Traces: Evading Chain-of-Thought Monitoring with Plan Injection, Keertana Chidambaram (Stanford), Andrew Ilyas (CMU), Vasilis Syrgkanis (Stanford). v1 2026-09-14, Monday cycle; no v2. The verified arXiv ceiling paper. Full text read including appendices A–C, Tables 1–11, all monitor prompts. (PDF)

Plants a harmful-but-benign-sounding plan in the agent actor's context (as simple as appending {"thoughts": "{COT}"} — zero instruction needed) and shows the actor paraphrases it as its own reasoning. Verified findings:

  • Paraphrase, not copy (App B.3, Table 7): median 8-gram overlap between injected plan and actor CoT is 10.3% (APPS, n=1,972) / 4.8% (Bio-Math, n=1,541); <1% of transcripts copy >half the plan. Surface-level provenance checks will not catch this.
  • Adoption and attack success (§5.3, Fig 3; App B.1, Table 5): Bio-Math plan adoption 93.0% (Qwen3-8B) / 98.9% (DeepSeek-R1-0528); APPS hardcoded-special-case backdoor succeeds ~84–86% for both actors, but the other two hand-tuned strategies transfer at ≤27% pass@3 and collapse to 2.5–14.4% pass@1. Closed-weight follow rates (n=100/task): Bio-Math 92–98% across GPT-5 / Sonnet 4 / Sonnet 5; APPS 6% / 40% / 0%.
  • Monitor evasion (§6.2, Tables 8–10): no monitor exceeds ~75% detection; end-to-end, the best monitor (Claude 3.7 thinking) misses 33%/40% of APPS and 78%/81% of Bio-Math attacked questions; non-thinking monitors miss up to 82% and sit near-chance on AUC (0.49–0.60 vs thinking 0.67–0.80). The abstract's "25–33%" is its own aggregate phrasing; per-cell miss rates run far higher.
  • The monitor-resource paradox is bidirectional — pack corrected (§6.2–6.3, Figs 4–6; App B.8.5): "give the monitor the plan and detection drops up to 50%" is a worst-case cell. On Bio-Math, plan access helps on net (409/722 verified actor-only-miss/with-plan-catch vs 407 reverse); on APPS the with-plan penalty dominates. The budget non-monotonicity rests on a single monitor (Qwen3-235B) with extremes-only transcript sampling — the paper explicitly does not estimate prevalence. A verbatim monitor transcript does show it rationalizing a fabricated "fire codes" backdoor as spec-compliant at 8k thinking budget after flagging it at 100 tokens.
  • Self-authored baseline isolates the mechanism (App B.7, Table 11): when the actor plans the same attack itself, monitors catch 78–98% (APPS) and up to 71% (Bio-Math) — the evasion gain comes from outsourcing the planning upstream, not from inherent invisibility.

Mandatory qualifications: discovery setting uses one model (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B) as investigator, actor, and monitor (self-monitoring circularity); only 2 actors × 2 tasks, plans written by a single corruptor (DeepSeek-R1) with un-optimized prompts; all detection is LLM-judged suspicion at a fixed s≥0.15 threshold with some same-family monitor/actor pairs; headline follow rates are best-of-3; Bio-Math is 78 bio questions × 5 math pairings; no end-to-end attack through real RAG/tool/multi-agent pipelines (plans pasted directly — listed as future work).

BQAA/ADK application: the direct threat model for any audit product scoring agent traces: the attack surface is upstream context the actor trusts — plan files, planner output, retrieved docs, tool results — laundered into clean first-person reasoning. Two design consequences: (a) trace auditing must preserve and diff provenance (what the actor saw vs emitted) since with-plan vs actor-only monitoring diverges dramatically and unpredictably; (b) monitor compute is not a safe dial — extra reasoning budget can rationalize a planted justification, so audit pipelines need cross-source consistency checks (plan vs trace vs output), not bigger judges.

2. NEW rank: Do Not Restart — CFRC hands off stateful agent work at 22.0–34.6% of full-frontier cost by freezing a residual contract and admitting only whole, evidence-backed continuations

Do Not Restart: Residual Completion for Stateful Agent Handoffs, Runzhi Deng, Yiming Zhong (Nanjing U / SMU), Fang Zhao, Pan Zhou. v1 2026-09-12, Monday cycle; no v2. Full text read including Tables 1–4 and appendix limitations. (PDF)

Commitment-Frontier Residual Completion: freeze a residual contract from the accepted prefix before the successor proposes (RSCC), close the successor's whole remainder as an evidence-linked graph on an isolated replica (ERC), admit it as a whole before any live write, and accept success only from matching live receipts (CE). Verified findings:

  • Cost-parity headline (Table 1, recomputation matches): GPT-5.4-mini→GPT-5.5 pair: CFRC 70.8% macro accuracy vs 69.6% full-task frontier at 34.6% of cost; Luna→Sol: 70.8% vs 70.0% at 22.0%. Cross-provider transfer holds within +0.4/+0.5 (Luna→Sonnet 5 at 26.0%, Luna→Gemini 3.7 Flash at 44.1%) — but Haiku 4.5→Sol trails its strong anchor by −5.0 points at 57.4% cost: weak cheap prefixes inject flawed commitments CFRC cannot repair.
  • Ablation ladder (Table 2): Direct Takeover 58.9% / $68.82 → +RSCC 62.0% → +ERC 65.4% → full CFRC 70.8% / $38.80; whole-graph admission alone adds 8.8 points over step-by-step checking under the same contract.
  • Audit results (Table 4): 62/68 persistent write obligations recovered (91.2% recall); 0/50 ungrounded live writes admitted (exact Clopper–Pearson 95% upper bound 5.8%); all 124 CE interventions occurred before the first live write; 43/43 rejections reproduce under deterministic replay.
  • Pack correction: "statistically indistinguishable from full-task frontier agents" is the pack's phrase — the paper reports five-run means (macro SD 0.54–0.58) and runs no significance test. "Three formal invariants" is also a framing upgrade: the orderings are enforced design stages; the only formal result (Prop. 1) is explicitly conditional on complete contract construction.

Mandatory qualifications: audit cohorts are small (n=50 proposals, 68 obligations, 43 rejections); 4.0% omission-related false completions remain (2/50) — 6 missed obligations came from conversational disclosures outside tool schemas; ⊥ after a live write does not imply rollback (destruction is prevented pre-write, never undone); single-tenure handoffs only; no learned routing; †-baselines evaluated on surface subsets (shifting denominators); code "will be open-sourced" — not yet available.

BQAA/ADK application: essentially a reference design for an auditable agent-handoff layer — the frozen residual contract plus live-receipt evidence ledger is exactly the artifact to log for handoff forensics (who committed what, which obligations discharged by which receipt, where false completion slipped through). The 124/124 pre-write intervention and replay-consistent rejection records map directly onto an audit-trail schema for proving an agent never wrote ungrounded state; the cost framing (parity at ~a third of strong-model spend) argues for routing expensive verification only at commitment frontiers.

3. NEW rank: HazardAuditor — one canonical event schema across Claude Code / Codex / Hermes / OpenClaw, and a decision-level GuardPO objective lifting unsafe-behavior detection up to +16.5pp

HazardAuditor: From Executable Threats to Safer Computer-Use Agents, Yunhao Feng, Ruixiao Lin, Ming Wen, Yanming Guo, Xingjun Ma, Yutao Wu, Xinhao Deng, Shouling Ji (Ant Group / Zhejiang / Fudan / HIAT / Deakin). v1 2026-09-14, Monday cycle; no v2. Full text read including appendices and the fixed test-set protocol. (PDF)

Normalizes heterogeneous computer-use agent traces (terminal, browser, filesystem) into a canonical event representation, then trains a generative guard with Guard Policy Optimization: token-level objectives let long rationales dominate gradient updates, so GuardPO converts deterministic safety outcomes into sequence-level advantages, normalizing rationale and verdict regions separately (Eq. 15, with a proven length-invariance property). Verified findings:

  • The 16.5pp headline is exact but scoped (Table I): HazardAuditor 87.5 vs BraveGuard 71.0 accuracy on the OpenClaw subset of CUA-EXEC (200 balanced traces/framework); other frameworks +12.5 (Claude Code 94.0), +4.0 (Codex 95.5), +9.5 (Hermes 86.5). "Prior SOTA" is one baseline family; "up to" does real work.
  • GuardPO vs its own SFT init (Table IV): +10.38 Acc / +10.68 Macro-F1 on CUA-EXEC — but only +0.91 / +0.60 / +2.20 on AgentHazard / ASSE-Safety / ATBench and −0.80 on R-Judge, a regression the authors report openly. SFT data alone ≈ BraveGuard.
  • Label protocol worth copying (§III-A): unsafe iff at least one tool call/artifact is an unauthorized action toward the safety objective; observing malicious content without acting = safe. A defensible behavior-vs-content audit convention.
  • External transfer (Table III): best worst-case F1 88.3% across R-Judge/ASSE/ATBench (vs 86.2% SFT, 80.7% best prior) — but Gemini-3-Flash still beats it on R-Judge (95.3 vs 89.5) and AgentDoG edges it on ATBench (88.8 vs 88.3). On GPT-5.5-backend AgentHazard, BraveGuard keeps higher recall/F1 (89.22 vs 84.31).
  • Efficiency honesty (Tables VII–VIII): generative guard 88.12 Macro-F1 at 3.14s/call; frozen-backbone MLP heads cut latency 10.3× to ~304ms but collapse Macro-F1 to 69.6 and safe recall to 41.1 — pre-screener use only.

Mandatory qualifications: CUA-EXEC is small-n and self-generated (800 traces; fixed test set 228, 47–69 per framework, not class-balanced); train and primary eval share infrastructure (held out only at the safety-case level), so CUA-EXEC gains partly measure distribution fit; no inter-annotator agreement reported; GuardPO is a light single run (1 epoch, 32 prompts × 8 rollouts, Qwen3Guard-Gen-8B init) with no seeds or error bars; reward is fully deterministic (no judge circularity — a strength).

BQAA/ADK application: the canonical event schema with per-framework adapters is the ingestion-layer shape for cross-harness audit tables in BigQuery — including the deliberate untrusted-data boundary around serialized traces (App. E). The behavior-vs-content label rule is the right ground-truth convention for audit verdicts; GuardPO's finding that token-mean objectives let rationale length dominate a binary decision applies directly to anyone fine-tuning LLM audit classifiers; and the precision/recall regime data (guards at 100% precision / ~10% recall) warns against trusting high-precision-only guard metrics in dashboards.

4. NEW rank: When Agents Slow Down — a distribution-free 400-Elo-per-decade yardstick shows Claude Code / Codex / Kimi / Gemini agents all fall below independent-sampling efficiency at large budgets; measured inflection points say when to split into parallel sessions

When Agents Slow Down: Understanding LLM Agents' Test-Time Strategies via Elo-per-token Analysis, Kaiyuan Liu, Qiuyang Mang, Bo Peng, Wenhao Chai, Hanchen Li, Shreyas Pimpalgaonkar, Luke Zettlemoyer, Alex Dimakis, Alvin Cheung (UC Berkeley / UW / Princeton / Bespoke Labs). v1 2026-09-14, Monday cycle; no v2. Full text read (39 pages) including appendices A–G. (PDF)

Aggregates best-so-far scores per session at each token budget into Bradley–Terry Elo-per-token curves (anchor 1000, 500-replicate session bootstrap). Verified findings:

  • The yardstick (Thm 3.1, proved App. A.1): independent best-of-n sampling gains exactly 400 Elo per decade of compute, distribution-free — the reference slope for every other measurement.
  • Main result (§4.2, Fig 3): 4 agent systems (Kimi Code/K2.7, Codex/GPT-5.5, Claude Code/Opus 4.8, Gemini CLI/Gemini 3.5 Flash) × 14 tasks × 5 sessions at up to 100M cache-inclusive tokens: all initially beat the 400/decade slope, then every system's local slope falls below it — agents asymptotically reduce to (or below) independent sampling.
  • Pack correction — numbers swapped: the pack says "+355 Elo vs +264 for one monolithic session." The paper says the reverse assignment (§6.2, Fig 8; abstract): splitting 100M tokens by the measured inflection point (b_inf = 38M on Polyomino Packing → 3-way split) yields +264 joint-Elo over one 100M session (bootstrap 90% CI [+135, +412]) and +355 over ten 10M sessions (CI [+226, +618]). Both numbers real; the pack attached them to the wrong baselines. MLS-Bench replication (b_inf = 58M): top three allocations statistically indistinguishable.
  • Specialized strategies don't escape (§5): AdaEvolve and GEPA lead Kimi Code by >300 Elo early but converge within ~20 Elo by ~1M tokens; TTT-Discover shows the same diminish-toward-reference slope.
  • Humans diverge (§4.3): top-10 AHC contestants' self-Elo is convex in log contest time; on the one time-aligned contest (AHC014) top-10 humans overtake agents rated to 72h (terminal ~1991 vs ~1582/1533).
  • Model-size sweep (App. F.3): Qwen3.5 27B→397B buys an earlier plateau, not a steeper slope — smaller models have higher best-of-K variance, so inflection-point allocation matters more for them.

Mandatory qualifications: small n throughout (5 sessions per cell; the allocation experiment is 50 sessions of a single system on two tasks); inflection points are task-level, not domain-level — the FrontierCS domain crossing does not predict Polyomino's 38M (§7), so per-task profiling is an expensive prerequisite; session-to-slot assignment can move a fitted rating >100 Elo (neutralized by averaging 20,000 assignments); token accounting is cache-inclusive (agents run 3–5% uncached vs ~84% for evolutionary methods — cross-class comparisons use the uncached axis where the agent arm ends early); harness-induced behavior (agents prompted to submit early/often, watchdog force-resumed); human comparison is wall-clock vs tokens except one contest. All evaluators deterministic — no LLM-judge circularity, a genuine strength.

BQAA/ADK application: nearly a spec for a BQAA feature — the analysis runs entirely on logged (score, cumulative-tokens) pairs per session, which an agent-analytics pipeline already stores. The 400-Elo/decade reference gives an audit-ready, distribution-free "harness degeneration" test: flag sessions whose marginal Elo-per-token slope drops below the sampling reference and you have a principled trigger to kill a long session and fork parallel ones (K = ⌊B/b_inf⌉). Also the standing rebuttal to "longer agents keep improving" claims: improvement continues, but at or below naive best-of-K efficiency.

5. NEW rank: Overflip — repeating a jailbreak prompt ~40–130× flips 5 of 9 production guardrail classifiers (up to 92%) from malicious to benign, and the intact payload still executes downstream

Overflip: Repetition-Induced Label Flips in Guardrail Models, Xu He (GMU), Chih-Hsuan Lin (Virginia Tech), Hung-Mao Chen, Junjie Xiong, Yan Zhai (Visa), Kun Sun. v1 2026-09-14, Monday cycle; no v2. Full text read including appendix Table 4. (PDF)

Verified findings:

  • Flip rates (Tables 1–2): 5 of 9 lightweight guardrails flip MAL→BEN under exact-repetition lengthening on 100 prompts: LPG-2-86M 92%, PIS (ModernBERT 395M) 87%, PIJS-v2 (Qwen3 596M) 39%, LPG-2-22M 15%, dvbpi (ProtectAI DeBERTa-v3) 8%. Four models never flip. Pack precision fix: the 8–92% range is over the 5 vulnerable models, not flatly "across 9"; and flips are not a DeBERTa-only story (ModernBERT and Qwen3 architectures flip too).
  • First-flip lengths: mean 2,599–9,424 tokens after ~39–131 repetition rounds.
  • Downstream harm (Table 3, App. Table 4): Overflip bypasses fewer prompts than padding (71 vs 92) but preserves semantics better — backend attack success 41 (GPT-4.1 Mini) / 33 (Llama 3.1 8B) vs padding's 28/6; GPT-4.1 Mini ac→ac 57.7% vs 30.4%. Caveat: the Llama 3.1 8B cell is internally inconsistent (Table 3's 33 equals Table 4's rej→rej count; transition counts sum to 18 acceptances) — either a mislabel or an undefined metric; the GPT-4.1 Mini column reconciles.
  • Mechanism (§5.3): repetition concentrates attention (CLS-attention entropy ≈0.95 → as low as 0.3 within 1–5 steps on 10 prompts), opposite direction from padding-based dilution; original prompt length is not the driver (r = −0.08, p = 0.418). Confidence margins shrink monotonically before flips — a leading indicator.

Mandatory qualifications: selection circularity — all 100 prompts were pre-filtered as malicious by LPG-2-86M itself, which is also the RQ2 downstream guardrail; flip rates for other models rest on prompts chosen for detectability by one detector.
