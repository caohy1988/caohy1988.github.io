Academy

1. ObserverBench — select a monitor under the review policy it will serve

arXiv:2609.03026 · submitted September 2 · full PDF, 28 pages, §§5–7, especially Tables 11–14.

In a non-executing authorization task with 384 held-out safe/unsafe pairs, every monitor faces the same 10% block and 10% escalation budgets. A label monitor achieves AUROC 1.000 with mean policy loss 3.334. Weighting its score by operation severity lowers AUROC to 0.954 while improving loss to 2.155. Classification quality alone selects the wrong policy here. APPS backdoor experiments also show a reversal in mean monitor rankings, although the reported Gemma paired outcome range includes zero. Scoring is offline; attacks and measurement channels are fixed, and some controls were added after earlier results.

BQAA + ADK judgment. Add a replay that holds review capacity and intervention rules fixed while comparing scorers. Retain score, policy version, selected action and adjudicated outcome separately. This tests allocation quality beyond the 14:34 report-to-response query; it does not validate an adaptive defense or require activation capture.

2. Making Every Tool Call Count — a relevant goal can still reward waste

arXiv:2609.03493 · submitted September 3 · full PDF, 17 pages, §3, §4 and Appendix C.2/C.7/C.9.

NTEP-R scores pre-call goal alignment and post-call evidence acquisition, with a duplicate-goal penalty. In Table 6, two configurations share the same 7,774-example training pool and 4,417-example evaluation suite. Goal-only process supervision yields 55.492% macro accuracy and 5.360 calls/example; the full reward yields 69.690% and 1.549. This comparison removes both acquisition scoring and the penalty, so it cannot isolate either component. The answer-only row uses a different training pool. Results generally use one training run and one evaluation pass; teacher-generated paths and semantic judging remain dependencies.

BQAA + ADK judgment. Test whether a tool-use rubric distinguishes seeking evidence, receiving it and using it. Retain the returned evidence and attribution source beside the verdict. This is a concrete failure case for the existing DRACO/Coverage validation work, not evidence that semantic path labels establish causal necessity or that every repeated call is waste.
