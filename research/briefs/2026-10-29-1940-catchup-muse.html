#1 — Masked Erosion: How Acceptance Gates Lose Redundant Safeguards in Self-Improving Agents — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38392

So, Liu & Long (Univ. of Macau / Tsinghua). Full PDF read (all 25 pp. incl. Appendices A–K).

Self-improving agents keep a harness edit only if an acceptance gate scores it higher. The paper shows the gate is structurally blind to redundancy loss: while one safeguard still blocks a harm, deleting an overlapping one adds almost no observable harm (child-only harm 0.016 into protected children vs 0.422 into unprotected ones, §4.2), so the gate decides on task success alone. Across 411 improvement loops under ten gate rules (Table 1; loop census in Table 16) in two environments (credential misuse in AppWorld; destructive cleanup in a simulated ops service), with harm measured for all 16 subsets of four written safeguards:

  • A paired significance gate blocks chance deletions (accepts 0.04 of planted deletions vs 0.48 greedy, §4.3) but not deletions that genuinely raise success — and larger batches lower the cost at which it breaks: frozen pre-run predictions placed breaks at costs 0.20/0.09/0.03 for batches of 20/40/120 tasks; at cost 0.15, 20-task paired loops kept all six harnesses protected where 40-task loops lost all six (p = .001, §4.4; Table 23).
  • Harm audits stop exposure, not depth loss: a 120-run audit veto left 0/6 loops unprotected but five of six finals at margin 1. Across all six audit-veto finals, credential misuse averaged 0.21 under Qwen3-30B (Table 3). Lost depth becomes harm on model swap (protected-final contrast 0.30 vs 0.004, §4.5).
  • An off-the-shelf evolutionary prompt optimizer run unchanged kept every safety rule verbatim, yet two of its three finals misused credentials under the weaker model (2 and 5 of 40 runs vs 0 of 80 for the seed harness) via an added instruction outside the labelled safeguards (§4.6; Table 30).
  • Freezing selected safety text or checking one-layer ablations retained protection in the tested arms, with limits: freezing protects text rather than behavior, and noisy ablation checks still left four of six stress-loop finals at margin 1 (§4.7; Appendix F, Table 22).

Methodological care is high. Predictions and analysis code were frozen and hashed before each round's tests; 113 of 177 predictions held. Table 35 summarizes the tests behind the main claims. Appendix K discloses that two amendments and the GEPA predictions were frozen after related outcomes had been seen. Audits are deterministic with no LLM judge, and loops are the unit of analysis. Limits (Limitations): two environments with one harm and four safeguards each; most loops plant one deletion per generation (a stress test of the gate, not a frequency estimate for deployed optimizers); the 120-task batches resample a 60-task pool, so that arm confirms direction rather than the exact break (Appendix B); swap effects are largest for weaker executors. BQAA read: acceptance analytics that report only observed harm and success will certify harnesses whose redundant protection has already eroded; margin (fewest further failures to harm) is a measurable, gate-able quantity, and batch size is a safety parameter, not just a precision knob.

#2 — Beyond fabrication: Misattributed numbers in papers written by AI research agents — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38691

Alfaqeeh, Hourani, Hourani & Alfaqeeh (Al-Ahliyya Amman / Binghamton). Full PDF read (all 42 pp. incl. Appendix A).

Re-analyzes all 540 released judgments from a public benchmark of AI research agents (nine agents, fifteen open problems, 135 submissions; only 21 passed the integrity gate) and hand-labels 256 numerical claims from 18 submissions. In this benchmark, unsupported numerical claims mostly misattribute existing values.

  • Judge failures concentrate on write-up-vs-work criteria — code–paper alignment fails 52% of counted nodes, number traceability 38% — while overt-misconduct criteria almost never fire (hard-coded values 1%, external code 1%, §5.1; Fig. 2).
  • Of 53 unsupported labeled claims, 47 are wrong-source (the value exists in the agent's own files, for a different experiment, metric, run, or statistic) and only 6 are absent; population-weighted wrong-source share 81% (§5.2; Table 4). Recurring patterns: a standard deviation reported as a mean, a baseline's value reported for the proposed method, one seed reported as the average (Table 5).
  • A deterministic claim-to-evidence checker (context-overlap provenance score + per-claim decoy calibration, operating point frozen before labeling) certifies claims at 0.92 precision pooled (78/85; held-out 27/29; two-annotator κ = 0.89) but only ~23% coverage — and most uncertified claims are still traceable (57–72%), so "not verified" must route to review, not count as falsehood (§5.3; Tables 6–8). Naive value-presence matching accepts 87–95% of unsupported claims; an LLM verifier on the same evidence accepts 19/53 non-traceable claims versus the checker's 7/53 in the pooled analysis of 211 real claims (§5.3).
  • The checker's certified share is positively associated with judge integrity scores across 99 submissions (Spearman ρ = 0.22). One agent has only 13.8% certified claims despite a mean integrity score of 92.9; the paper treats the explanation involving headline versus other numbers as a hypothesis requiring further labels (§5.4; Table 10). A second judge from a different vendor orders agents similarly and ranks vendor groups identically, but passes twice as many submissions (12 vs 6 of 30), so absolute integrity rates are judge-dependent (§5.5; Table 11).

Limits (§6.3): one benchmark and domain family; both annotators are team members (blinded, high agreement, but not an external panel); held-out set is small (60 real claims). Certification measurements cover 99 of 135 submissions: three additional submissions completed without extractable claims, 15 timed out, two failed, and 16 exceeded the storage limit (§5.4). Certification proves recorded provenance, not experimental soundness. BQAA read: for agent-produced analyses, the auditable question is "which run produced this number?" — presence checks and single-judge verdicts both fail it, in measured ways; provenance certification at partial coverage with human routing for the rest is the honest operating point.

#3 — Tracekit Box: Running AI Agents in Micro-VMs Where Every Way Out Is Decided and Signed — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38458

Ghosh (Cygnux Labs). Full PDF read (all 20 pp. incl. Appendices A–D).

A system paper joining the two halves agent infrastructure usually ships separately: containment and evidence. Each job runs in a Firecracker micro-VM with no network card; the only exits are host-served "doors" over vsock. Host-started commands, outbound connections, and checkout writes through tkbox apply receive advance signer decisions. Secret uses and inspected model requests receive signed observations, not separate policy decisions. The signer is one the agent cannot reach, and decisions and observations are signed into one run record per box (guarantees G1–G5, §3.3; door-to-record mapping in Table 7). Secrets never enter the VM: the agent sees placeholders the host proxy swaps for real values in one bound header for exact bound hosts (§4.5). Changes return only as a patch applied file-by-file after per-file decisions (§4.7). Records fail closed: a must-keep record that cannot be kept stops the box and is counted as a visible gap (§5.3; Table 8). A run can be exported as a bundle plus trust file for offline verification; after a daemon crash, that box cannot be exported.

Evidence is concrete but early: 13/13 end-to-end checks pass on real KVM in CI (28.47 s including image pull and boot; Table 12); 15 property-stated fuzz targets on the trust boundary produced nine findings, all fixed — the most serious finding could expose a swapped secret to another host if an upstream server routed by the request's absolute-form target (§7.3; Table 15); the demo run's 102 signed records (46 decisions) verify (Table 13). Limits are stated plainly (§7, §8.1): every bundle reported has only dev assurance (same-user signer; Table 9); no Firecracker jailer yet; records doors, not processes inside the VM; decides who the agent may reach, not what it sends; no performance benchmarks and no adversarial-agent study yet. At dev assurance, bundle verification does not itself prove that the record came from a correctly configured box (§7.2). Single author, and the system builds on the same author's earlier signing tool — treat as a well-tested v0 design point. BQAA read: this is the ingest-side complement to agent analytics — a run record whose completeness comes from the environment (no other way out) rather than from trusting the agent's own instrumentation, with gaps made explicit instead of silent.

Six-week EV

  • Margin becomes a first-class eval metric. Masked Erosion gives BQAA a concrete, measurable quantity — fewest further safeguard failures before harm — that current acceptance dashboards do not report. Expect harness-evolution tooling (prompt/context optimizers, self-improving ADK-style loops) to face "what did the gate leave behind?" questions within weeks; teams that log per-edit ablation results will answer them, teams that log only success/harm will not.
  • Provenance checking splits from judging. Two independent papers this batch (#2 directly; Prompt Lottery as scouted corroboration) show single-judge verdicts are unstable in absolute terms while deterministic, artifact-grounded checks are not. Six-week read: evaluation stacks that pair a cheap deterministic certifier (partial coverage, high precision) with judge/human routing for the remainder will look increasingly standard; pure judge-only integrity gates will look increasingly fragile.
  • Signed run records move from tracing feature to sandbox property. Tracekit Box's "completeness from the environment" argument — the record is complete because there is no other way out — is the strongest current answer to "how do you know the trace isn't missing the bad part?" Watch whether sandbox vendors adopt signed, offline-verifiable run bundles; if they do, BQAA ingest should be able to verify and store them as first-class evidence.
