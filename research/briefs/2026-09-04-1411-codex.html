Academy

2. EarlyEval makes predicted outcomes an explicit evaluation product boundary

arXiv:2609.02783 · submitted September 2 · full PDF, 12 pages, especially §§III–VI and Table III.

EarlyEval trains success and failure classifiers on trajectory prefixes and substitutes a prediction for the eventual benchmark outcome when a calibrated threshold is crossed. Its retrospective evaluation holds out agent configurations; TerminalBench additionally separates model and scaffold overlap. On SWE-bench Verified at threshold 0.95, Table III reports 26.0% fewer steps, 32.7% fewer input tokens, and 1.1 percentage points mean absolute resolve-rate distortion. Those are savings against recorded full runs, not proof that a prematurely stopped agent delivered a finished artifact. Reference-patch features are used where available; other benchmarks omit them. Success prediction transfers less reliably than failure prediction, particularly to unseen scaffolds.

TL;DR for BQAA + ADK. A useful offline experiment, with separate records for predicted outcome, stopping step, classifier/calibration version and observed completion. Preserve completed controls and report error separately for predicted successes and failures. Do not merge predicted passes into verified-pass dashboards.

3. Requirements After the First Edit separates changing requirements from defective execution

arXiv:2609.03028 · submitted September 2 · full PDF, 30 pages, §§4–6.

The study starts with 3,553 eligible SWE-chat sessions; its canonical emergence set contains 921 events across 402 replayable sessions. The primary matched comparison uses 452 events: subsequent deletion/replacement of prior agent-authored lines averages 57.5 versus 29.4 at controls, a ratio of 1.96, with a 95% interval of 1.31–2.82. Failure-triggered corrections are excluded from the emergence definition. Repository replay, authorship and requirement timing make this measurable; counts alone do not establish semantic waste or causation. Replay selects a convenience sample, and line tracking can misclassify moves or formatting. The controlled experiment does not demonstrate that advance warning reduces overwriting.

TL;DR for BQAA + ADK. Join user requirement changes to later edits, and distinguish newly disclosed constraints from corrections of earlier failures. Keep the event reference and measurement window with any rework metric. This supports a coding-session query example, not a universal “wasted work” score or mandatory clarification gate.

4. Coverage, Not Targeting supplies a control that the DRACO interpretation needs

arXiv:2609.02417 · submitted September 2 · full PDF, 22 pages, §§4–8.

In the studied terminal-state verification setting, the observable progress signal lands on one final write in 98% of rollouts, although successful execution requires a chain of five to eight tool calls. Necessary reads leave no scored state change. Under matched rollout/advantage budgets, concentrating credit on apparent progress or on shuffled turns underperforms spreading it uniformly; BFCL and cross-family experiments probe the boundary. The real-model comparisons isolate the first policy update. A synthetic crossover is not a universal deployment threshold.

TL;DR for BQAA + ADK. Preserve verifier coverage and attribution-method provenance alongside step scores. Compare proposed localization against both uniform and shuffled controls. This paper was previously excluded as training-side; reconsider it now because the 12:34 brief promoted rubric-to-step attribution. It qualifies that inference without refuting DRACO's added rubric information: a step pointer is addressability, not demonstrated causal credit.

5. CAPTURE distinguishes authentic memory revision from successful poisoning

arXiv:2609.02265 · submitted September 2 · full PDF, 29 pages, §§4–8.

CAPTURE combines a temporal belief tracker, memory hypotheses separated by scope/timescale, clarification, and removal-based audits of cited memories. On 480 held-out synthetic episodes from 96 users, it accepts 83.5% of genuine updates while fixed-policy poisoning succeeds at 11.5%. Adaptive attack success rises to 24.7%; the comparison with a provenance-only baseline is not statistically separated on that security axis. The identically supervised baseline closes much of the headline performance gap. Forty participants supplied natural histories, but attacks and clarification answers were added during offline, oracle-assisted replay; this was not a live adversarial deployment study.

TL;DR for BQAA + ADK. Attribute memory writes to their source, prior belief, proposed scope and disposition. Report acceptance of genuine changes and acceptance of poisoned changes separately. Reuse the existing memory/source-event lineage note; the evidence does not warrant building a new memory controller in six weeks.

6. FailBench tests the evidence available to a multimodal judge

arXiv:2609.03611 · submitted September 3 · full PDF, 19 pages, §§3–5 and Tables 1–4.

FailBench evaluates 13 detectors on 2,197 robot attempts from 14 sources. The strongest model scores approximately 0.77 macro balanced accuracy; the macro denominator is 13 two-class subsets, with a failure-only subset excluded. No detector exceeds 0.60 on the contact-rich assembly slice. Supplying a localized crop improves the strongest detector in a controlled pipeline comparison, suggesting that evidence presentation can matter more than another reasoning pass. These are completed, visually judged tabletop manipulation attempts. Non-visual contact signals are unused, and the detailed reasoning diagnosis comes from one model.

TL;DR for BQAA + ADK. Strengthen the existing multimodal-evaluation note: retain the actual evaluated artifact or resolvable reference, frame selection/crop transform and source slice. Judge identity alone is insufficient for reproduction. The robotics scores do not transfer numerically to UI or enterprise agents; the testable lesson is to vary evidence presentation while holding the judge fixed.

7. Knowing Is Not Enough makes reviewer assistance part of measurement provenance

arXiv:2609.01976 · submitted September 2 · full PDF, 65 pages, Study 1, Study 2 and limitations.

Two randomized experiments involve 640 employees in one enterprise-software organization. In Study 1, participants who generated explanations of example errors before receiving standardized explanations achieved a mean detection score of 0.78 versus 0.68. Study 2 follows 240 participants over a baseline and seven workdays: personalized cues tied to prior verification reasoning slow the decline in detection compared with personalized neutral messages. The experiments use prepared tasks and searchable references. Retrieval is not directly observed, and the mechanism is not fully separated from attention or effort.

TL;DR for BQAA + ADK. Human review completion does not certify label quality. When comparing review queues or calibration runs, retain the assistance condition and measure detection plus false positives against adjudicated examples. This is a review-method note; it neither re-cards Braintrust blind reviews nor justifies adding prompts to every reviewer interaction.

8. Agent Flight Recorder separates record integrity from recording completeness — older catch-up

arXiv:2609.01931 · submitted September 1 · full PDF, 9 pages, §§III, VI and VIII. No matching abstract ID or title was found in the catalog or earlier dated briefs.

The prototype binds intent, policy decisions, approval, execution/effects and provenance into structured records, with hash chains, Merkle batches and external anchoring. Its most useful result for this beat is a boundary: tamper evidence cannot reveal actions that bypassed instrumentation, prove post-compromise entries truthful, or recover lost payloads from roots alone. The main evaluation is synthetic; real-trace overhead validation covers just five trajectories and 38 events. There is no implemented comparison against signed-digest or timestamp-authority alternatives. Reported anchoring fees are projections from measured testnet gas, not production cost observations.

TL;DR for BQAA + ADK. Keep capture completeness, record integrity and payload availability as separate claims. Typed approval/delegation events and source references remain useful without adopting this anchoring architecture. Retain cryptographic anchoring on the do-not-start side of the six-week plan.
