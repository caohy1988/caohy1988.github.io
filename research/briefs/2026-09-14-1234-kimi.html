1. NEW rank: Studying Without a Syllabus — an agent that studies an environment before knowing the tasks cuts test-time sampling 2–8×; bigger study budgets don't reliably help

Studying Without a Syllabus: Task-Agnostic Environment Preprocessing, Vinay Samuel, Varun Ursekar, Vijay S. Kalmath, Apaar Shanker, Veronica Chatrath, Yuan Xue (Scale AI; Samuel also UMD College Park — pack correction: the "University of Waterloo / Vector Institute" affiliation is fabricated, it appears nowhere in the paper). v1 2026-09-09, Sunday cycle. Full text read including appendices. (PDF) Code: github.com/scaleapi/meta-study.

Formalizes task-agnostic environment preprocessing: a studying system explores an unfamiliar sandboxed environment (Docker/Harbor containers) under a dollar budget with no access to downstream tasks, traces, labels, or verifiers, and writes persistent file artifacts (indices, scripts, playbooks, cheatsheets) for a frozen solver (Claude Code harness + Claude Haiku 4.5). Verified findings:

  • A Meta-Agent variant tops 5 of 6 benchmarks on Avg@3 (Table 1; 36 environments across BCP-Grep, OfficeQA, Harvey LAB, DABStep, Apex Agents, AppWorld) — e.g. OfficeQA 0.633±0.014, AppWorld 0.688±0.057; the fixed Corpus2Skill pipeline takes BCP-Grep.
  • The honest negative result (RQ3): $1–$50 study budgets show flat/non-monotonic downstream reward on OfficeQA and Apex; only Harvey LAB scales. More study spend ≠ more reward.
  • The cost-shift result (RQ4): the no-study baseline needs 2–8 oracle-selected rollouts at 1.6–5.5× inference cost to match one studied Best@1 rollout (Fig 3, Table 4) — study artifacts convert test-time sampling into pre-task preparation. Study costs themselves are separately metered and can be large (Apex $584–682, Table 5), so amortization is benchmark-dependent.
  • Statistics: N=3 study iterations × 3 eval rollouts (small-n SDs), task-bootstrap 95% intervals on scaling figures, no significance tests. The task-blind protocol explicitly bars verifier/instruction leakage — eval-integrity hygiene worth copying.

Mandatory qualifications: single-vendor stack (all-Claude models + Claude Code harness; no cross-solver transfer evidence); PREPING baseline run at 5 cycles vs its published 10 (a weakened baseline — its AppWorld budget scaling does not reproduce here); the RQ4 comparison excludes study cost and gives NoStudy oracle Best@K; interpretability sections lean on three LLM judges with descriptive-only claims; dollar cost is a provider-dependent proxy.

BQAA/ADK application: this is the controlled version of a question BQAA answers informally — is offline environment discovery (schema indexing, catalog caching, playbook writing) worth its metered cost against runtime exploration? The paper's discipline is the takeaway: meter study and inference dollars separately, freeze the solver, keep the study phase task-blind, and report the negative scaling result beside the win. Expect the Apex-style outcome — preparation pays on some environment families and not others.

2. NEW rank: Beyond Top-k Skill Retrieval — a query-residual DPP kernel beats top-k selection on multi-skill queries (with an honest n=75)

Beyond Top-k Skill Retrieval: Diversity-Aware Skill Routing for LLM Agents, Wang Wei, Tiankai Yang, Samyadeep Basu, Hongjie Chen, Yue Zhao, Zhengzhong Tu, Xiyang Hu, Franck Dernoncourt, Ryan A. Rossi, Hoda Eldardiry (Virginia Tech lead, with USC, Adobe Research, Dolby, Texas A&M, ASU). v1 2026-09-05, Sunday cycle. Full text read. (PDF)

Pointwise skill routers score each candidate independently and return top-k, so over large (~80K-skill) redundant registries they return near-duplicates and miss complementary skills. DSR keeps the retrieve-and-rerank pipeline and replaces only final selection with a DPP whose novelty is a query-residual diversity kernel: each skill embedding has its query-aligned component removed before the similarity penalty (blended λ=0.85), so "diverse" means diverse beyond shared query relevance. Verified results (Table 1; n=75 expert-verified queries — 24 single-skill, 51 multi-skill; EASY 78,361 skills, HARD +780 LLM-generated distractors; tier-averaged):

  • Multi-skill: Recall@20 0.704→0.739, FC@20 0.458→0.492, FC@50 0.458→0.551 vs the SkillRouter baseline. All-query FC@50 0.560→0.633.
  • Ablations (Table 2) isolate the kernel: query-residual vs cosine lifts multi-skill FC@10 0.254→0.441; reranker-quality vs embedding-quality as the DPP quality term lifts R@10 0.618→0.711.
  • Two caveats a collector would miss: SkillRouter's @50 equals its @20 because its released output is truncated at 20 — the @50 "gains" are partly an asymmetric-list artifact; and MRR@10 slightly drops (0.788→0.784) in the appendix. Single-skill queries tie (0.875).

Mandatory qualifications: no seeds, no variance, no CIs, no significance tests — a 1–3.5-point delta on 51–75 queries is ~1–3 queries; single benchmark, single retriever/reranker pair; no MMR baseline (the natural comparator for DPP); no λ/candidate-size sensitivity analysis; retrieval metrics only — no end-to-end execution success.

BQAA/ADK application: the kernel is the import — when BQAA (or any skill/tool registry at scale) selects k items for a query, penalize redundancy in the query-orthogonal subspace, not raw cosine; shared relevance to the query is not duplication. But hold the paper's own evidence bar against any internal version: n=75 with no significance testing is a demo, not a result — an internal DSR-style selector needs per-query paired deltas and CIs before the gain is claimed.

3. NEW rank: Online Learning with LLM Experts from Limited Feedback — bandit routing that learns from a handful of judge calls (and an honest small-budget reversal)

Online Learning with LLM Experts from Limited Feedback, Wang Wei, Soumyabrata Pal, Koyel Mukherjee, Franck Dernoncourt, Ryan A. Rossi, Branislav Kveton, Hoda Eldardiry (Virginia Tech + Adobe Research). v1 2026-09-05, Sunday cycle. Full text read. (PDF)

Formulates prompt routing to K LLM experts as a contextual linear bandit where judge feedback costs money: total feedback budget m ≪ T, and the algorithm adaptively decides when and for which past round to query the judge (G-optimal-design-style selection over covariance determinant). Verified: bandit-setting LimBanFeed achieves Õ(dT√(K/m)) regret (Thm 4.1; the pack's headline bound is the bandit bound — accurate) with near-matching lower bounds Ω(T√(d/m)), Ω(TK/√m). Empirically (Table 1; Nectar, K=6, d=40, T=60k, 5 runs ±std): LimBanFeed beats the naive NoLookBack baseline at every budget (m=1,000: 3,583±68 vs 3,790±30).

  • The counter-detail the prose buries: in the full-information setting, LimFullFeed is worse than NoLookBack at m=1,000 (672.9±37.4 vs 631.6±22.1) and at m=2,000, reaching parity only from m≥5,000 — the paper's "consistently outperforms" claim does not survive its own Table 1. The label-efficiency win is real in the bandit setting only.
  • Runtime ~linear in T and K for the bandit variant (Table 2); cost-aware per-expert evaluation pricing is theory-only, never tested.

Mandatory qualifications: weak baseline suite — only the authors' own NoLookBack and an AllFeedback oracle; no RouteLLM/FrugalGPT/cascade comparison despite citing them; ground truth is GPT-4 judge labels with the linear heads OLS-fit to those labels (evaluation circularity risk); single embedding stack (bge-small → PCA d=40 retaining ~50% variance); linear-reward assumption; static benchmarks.

BQAA/ADK application: the formulation is the value — "how few judge calls suffice to learn a router" is exactly BQAA's eval-budget question, and retrospective G-optimal feedback selection (judge the past round whose label reduces model uncertainty most) is a concrete, implementable policy. Carry the reversal as the design lesson: at very small feedback budgets, even a principled method can lose to doing nothing naive — publish the small-m regime, not just the asymptote.
