Academy

Two Thursday Sep 3 batch papers that no earlier brief today opened. Both read in full.

CivBench: A Long-Horizon Benchmark for Tool-Mediated Agents in Civilization VI (submitted Sep 2)

Andrews and Wilkinson (joint first authors, Oxford and the Tony Blair Institute), with Heagerty (Google DeepMind), Coppock (UK AI Security Institute), Foerster and Ponte Costa (Oxford). Submitted to the NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets track.

The environment is a Civilization VI server exposed through 76 MCP tools, with a 29-function narration layer that turns game state into structured text. The design rule that matters: the agent sees nothing it does not explicitly query. Victory progress, rival strength and diplomacy exist in the game but produce no observation unless a tool is called. That separates "information unavailable" from "information available but never retrieved," and the authors use it to measure attention allocation from the trace rather than infer it from outcomes. A full game is 300+ turns and thousands of tool calls, 5 to 15 per turn.

They ran 23 admissible full games across Claude Opus 4.6 (8 runs), GPT-5.4 (8), Gemini 3.1 Pro (6) and one exploratory Kimi K2.5 run, each costing $31 to $229 in API fees and 2 to 8 hours. They are explicit that this is a pilot, not a ranking. Win counts are sparse (3 of 23), Fisher's exact test gives p = 0.488, and an intraclass-correlation sweep over 13 outcome metrics finds only exploration at turn 100 discriminates between models. Aggregate score compresses the behavioral variation that separates agents.

So they define two metrics computed from the tool-call log under a shared playbook:

  • Proactive Monitoring Rate (PMR). Strategic-monitoring tool calls divided by all non-infrastructure tool calls. Across models it sits between 0.96% and 2.13%. Victory monitoring specifically is 0.05% to 0.29% of calls, or 3.7 to 10.0 calls per game, even though the playbook says to check every 20 turns. Agents actually check every 30 to 75 turns. In 7 of 20 defeats where a rival's win was detectable at least 20 turns out, the agent never queried victory progress in that window. PMR does not rise toward the endgame, so this is a persistent allocation choice, not a context-limit artifact.
  • RAG@K, the Reflection–Action Gap. The playbook requires a five-field diary each turn (tactical, strategic, tooling, planning, hypothesis). Commitments are extracted from the planning field by Claude Haiku 4.5 under a fixed rubric, labelled executed, partial or not executed over the next K = 10 turns, and scored as (Y + 0.5P) / total. A 50-item human-labelled sample gives 92% agreement, Cohen's kappa 0.879. RAG@10 is 48.2% for Claude Opus 4.6, 65.8% for Gemini 3.1 Pro and 63.2% for GPT-5.4, with overlapping bootstrap intervals. Example unexecuted commitments from the traces: "Found second city," "Check victory progress."

Stated limits: 23 runs is powered only for large effects, the playbook is a confound (a playbook-free baseline was not viable, only 21% of pre-harness runs finished), there is no random or scripted baseline, and the game is commercially licensed. They release environment, scenarios, full MCP transcripts, metrics and the analysis pipeline, and the scripts recompute PMR, RAG@K and tool-use profiles from raw logs. The harness is Inspect from the UK AI Security Institute.

TL;DR for BQAA. Both headline metrics are group-by queries over a tool-call table: PMR is a ratio of calls by tool category per session, and the missed-warning table is "was tool X called in the last N turns before terminal event." RAG@K needs one more thing, a typed "stated commitment" event with a target and a resolution label, which is a judge output BQAA could store as a row. This is the cleanest public example yet of evaluation being done as analytics on the trace instead of on the outcome.

Beyond Outcome Gaps: Process-Aware Fairness Diagnosis for LLM-based Multi-Agent Decision Systems (submitted Sep 2)

Zhao, Zhou, Fang, Chen, Wu, Liu and Xu (Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Shandong University, Zhejiang University).

SCOPED-Hiring builds a two-stage hiring committee on AutoGen: a screening committee (Tech Lead, Peer Dev, Recruiter), then for Stage 1 passes an executive committee (VP Engineering, Hiring Manager, HR Director). Each agent gets a per-case archetype (gatekeeper, advocate, pragmatist); agents evaluate independently and debate only when votes diverge; all agents in a run share one model. Candidate cases come from real CV seeds in the Djinni corpus, expanded into controlled variants that inject one signal while holding merit fixed: gender-implied name and age, country and identity group across 12 national contexts, university tier, city tier, hobbies as a socioeconomic proxy, and typed career gaps.

The part that matters for BQAA is the record. Every case-condition trajectory is logged as structured fields: candidate attributes, seed and variant id, model, condition, stage, phase, role, archetype, plus per-agent private assessment, public argument, scores, investigation requests and votes. That is 311,494 records across GPT-5.4-mini, Gemini-3.1-flash-lite and Qwen-3.5-flash. Six lenses then read different slices of that table: Statistical outcome (hire-rate and score gaps), Counterfactual (matched-seed flip rates), Operational process (a lexical Bias Revelation Index on private assessments, skeptical-investigation rate), Pathway (Stage 1 pass gap, Stage 2 conversion gap), Evolutionary dynamics (score drift and vote flips between independent and debate phases), and Design effects (single-agent versus committee, topology, memory, pressure variants). Each lens yields direction-aware disadvantage scores that roll up into a signal-family by lens salience matrix.

The finding is that outcome-only audits under-read the risk. Across the three models, process-aware lens salience exceeds outcome-lens salience by 2.74x to 4.66x. Raw examples: career-gap candidates are privately flagged as risky in 94% of GPT assessments versus 32% for no-gap candidates, with only a small hire-rate gap; Tier 1 universities pick up 0.14 to 0.32 composite-score points over Tier 3 with hire-rate gaps of 1 to 2 points. The most pointed result is a design-effect one: under a single-agent condition, GPT's investigation rates for UK White and UK Black candidates are near-identical (both above 0.87), but under the committee they diverge to 0.305 versus 0.357. Same model, different structure, and the structure created an intermediate location where unequal treatment became observable.

A diagnosis-guided repair (three targeted prompt "skills") cuts total layered burden by 72.3% with a 1.86-point hire-rate shift, while a generic "be fair" reminder and an evidence-ledger baseline do not improve total burden. Stated limits: hiring only, one CV corpus, two flagship occupations; private assessments and investigation requests are elicited audit fields, not recovered chain-of-thought or executed tool calls; the landscape depends on normalization choices; and the 72.3% is a suite-level result that cannot be attributed to any single skill.

TL;DR for BQAA. This is a fairness audit run as a query over typed columns for role, stage, phase and per-agent artifact, and its single strongest result (investigation rates diverging only under committee structure) is a two-way group-by. It is the second paper in 24 hours arguing that multi-agent trace fields need to be first-class columns, not a JSON blob, and the first to show a compliance-grade reason: outcome-rate audits of the kind NYC's automated employment decision tool rule asks for can miss what the trajectory shows.

Six-week EV only

Ranking unchanged from 0746. Nothing in industry moved. Both papers reinforce the "named loop and eval schema" item rather than adding a new line. Two notes, alongside the failure-step pointer and escalation flag from 1934:

  • Tool-category dimension. CivBench's PMR only works because tools are bucketed (monitoring, action, query, infrastructure). A tool-category attribute on tool-call events, set by the developer or derived from tool name, turns "how much of the budget goes to monitoring" into a one-line query and makes the missed-warning table possible.
  • Role and stage on multi-agent events. SCOPED-Hiring's design-effect result requires agent role, pipeline stage and deliberation phase as columns on every agent output. ADK multi-agent traces already carry agent name; stage and phase are the gap.
