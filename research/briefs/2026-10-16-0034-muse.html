Stronger Judges Do Not See More: Deployment-Integrity Blindness in LLM Evaluation

  • Full PDF read. v1 [cs.CR] 15 Oct 2026. Su, Xiang, Zhang, Zhang (University of Connecticut).
  • The question: evaluation reports behavioral scores for what is now a deployed stack — model, tokenizer, system prompt, tool specs, retrieval corpus, runtime, postprocessors. If one component is swapped or tampered with after evaluation and outputs on the suite still look fine, does any output-only evaluator notice? And does a stronger evaluator notice more?
  • Method: DIB-Bench applies six controlled single-component tampers across 7,560 scored examples (three model pairs, three seeds, nine runs) and scores each with an evaluator ladder — rule-based scorers (Standard/Strict/Forensic/Canary), a local Qwen3-8B judge, and GPT-5-nano — alongside a manifest-hash identity channel that compares declared vs observed stack components. Anchors on BFCL-simple and GSM8K (n=200×3 seeds each) and AgentDojo banking trajectories (n=16) test whether the pattern survives public benchmarks (Table 4).
  • Results: blindness increases with evaluator strength — mean tamper acceptance is 88.6% (rule-based), 89.3% (Qwen3-8B), 94.1% (GPT-5-nano), while the identity channel separates every tamper at 0% (Table 2). Suite-level thresholds don't save the output channel: at τ=0.80 the strict/canary settings that reject tampers also reject clean deployments (Table 3). A trigger-conditional tamper is 100% invisible on the suite yet produces harmful outputs off-distribution at rates rising with capability — 48% (Llama-3.1-8B), 81% (Qwen2.5-7B), 100% (Qwen2.5-14B). Behavioral fingerprinting identifies the model component at 76.7% but covers one of six components with a 37.5% collision floor. A PAC-style argument closes the escape hatch: tampers with zero output-distribution shift are undetectable from outputs at any sample size, while manifest hashes decide that class with zero samples. The honest counter-result is included: a stealth tamper hiding in an undeclared component evades both channels (Table 5) — identity detection is a function of manifest coverage, and the paper's graded deployment-identity descriptor (Table 6) exists to make that coverage reportable.
  • Limits (stated by the authors): synthetic single-component tampers in the main package; no multi-component attacks or manifest forgery; models span 1.5B–20B plus the GPT-5-nano judge, not frontier-scale deployed systems; the manifest is software-level evidence without hardware-rooted attestation; the PAC dichotomy covers passive i.i.d. sampling, with adaptive-probe bounds left as future work.
  • Why it matters for BQAA: this is the measurement case for recording deployment identity next to every behavioral score. A warehouse full of scores without stack manifests cannot distinguish "the agent got worse" from "the thing being scored changed" — and this paper shows upgrading the judge actively widens that blind spot. The reportable unit going forward is score + manifest coverage: which components were declared and hashed, and which were never measured at all.

Prune the Summary, Not the History: Source-Judged Compaction for Long-Horizon Agents

  • Full PDF read. v1 [cs.CL] 14 Oct 2026. Min & Chen (University of Virginia). Code linked in the paper (not audited by me).
  • The question: context compaction replaces history with a compressor-written summary, and compaction prompts explicitly ask for decisions and next steps. After compaction the agent cannot distinguish facts the environment established from reasoning the compressor invented. How much of a typical summary is unsourced, what does acting on it cost, and can entries be filtered by provenance at compaction time?
  • Method: the authors decompose 667 OpenClaw structured summaries from AppWorld training runs into 26,881 entries and judge each for whether an environment output or the previous summary establishes it (the judge is Jev, a non-generative decision model returning a calibrated probability; judge quality is itself validated in Table 12). They then trace 35 analyzable failed runs back to the action that caused failure. The proposed fix, Source-Judged Compaction (SJC), is a training-free post-processor: split the summary into entries, score each for source support, delete unsupported entries whole, at every boundary — one extra judge request per compaction, agent and compressor untouched. Evaluation on AppWorld test-normal (168 tasks) and OfficeBench with two agent models (GPT-5.6 Luna, MiniMax-M3), three runs each, reporting accuracy and Pass3 (solved in all three runs).
  • Results: observation sections are almost fully supported, but 67.7% of Open TODOs and 86.1% of pending user asks have no source; 38.7% of reasoning entries (Decisions + Open TODOs) are unsupported, rising to 54.9% at the first boundary (Table 2) — and unsupported reasoning is regenerated each boundary (~4 entries per summary regardless of position; only 20.5% persist to the next summary), so one-time cleanup cannot work. In 29 of 35 failed runs the failing action carries out a specific summary entry (two annotation rounds agree on every run). SJC achieves the highest Pass3 among compressed methods in all four benchmark–model settings, improving over the underlying Prompting compressor by 2.1%–8.9% with the largest gains on harder tasks and no increase in agent token usage (Table 3; OfficeBench in Table 4); judge overhead is under $0.002 per task (Table 5). Replacing Jev with the compressor judging itself is less reliable (Table 6).
  • Limits (stated by the authors): SJC filters by provenance, not correctness — supported and unsupported entries are correct at similar rates (92.2% vs 90.6%), so sourced-but-wrong entries survive (4 of the 29 acted-on failure entries were supported) and correct helpful entries get pruned (the judge recovers 64.2% of supported entries at their threshold; one documented prune flips a task for MiniMax-M3). Two benchmarks, two agent models, one host compressor, small windows forcing per-task compaction; single-instruction tasks that stay visible after compaction (multi-turn user messages as sources are unevaluated); the external judge is API-accessed and not fully deterministic; failure attribution rests on LLM annotators under a written protocol.
  • Why it matters for BQAA: paired with yesterday's Haystack offload card, compaction now has both halves priced: where removed content goes, and whether what stays was ever established. For trace analytics the actionable schema point is per-entry provenance at compaction boundaries — source span or compressor invention — because post-hoc failure analysis otherwise treats laundered reasoning as observed fact. The regeneration finding also kills a tempting shortcut: provenance must be judged at every boundary, not inherited.

AgentEval: Evidence-Bounded Assessment of Stateful Agents

  • Full PDF read. v1 [cs.AI] 15 Oct 2026. Gao (Alibaba Cloud, Alibaba Group) — single author; note the name collides with an earlier, unrelated AgentEval (Arabzadeh et al. 2024), which the paper itself disambiguates.
  • The question: for stateful agents, task completion doesn't establish that obligations were satisfied (money moved exactly once, only the authorized record changed), and a recorded judgment doesn't guarantee its evidence still supports it. Can one contract-bound assessment record be carried through execution, evidence capture, report qualification, and case maintenance so that what happened, whether it satisfied the contract, and whether the conclusion is usable now stay separate, inspectable claims?
  • Method: a trusted host runs permission, transaction, and clarification tool-worlds with evaluator-only evidence capture (privileged state/events never enter the agent's observation interface); task contracts declare goal and process obligations as predicates yielding PASS/FAIL/UNKNOWN; assessments are content-addressed and bound to specification, seed, registration, instrument, and scope. Report inspection rechecks present-use qualification without rewriting history. A separately implemented checker (reading specs, state facts, events, provider receipts — never product verdicts) validates labels; goal-only and hash-only checks serve as ablations. Studies: 32 allocated tool-world administrations (E1/E2), 48 controlled evidence-view interventions on six anchors (E3), case-lifecycle admission (E4), 12 two-turn Scenario Studio reports, and a frozen τ-bench-derived retail study (96 calibration + 192 held-out executions).
  • Results: 30 of 32 administrations complete (two end in idempotency conflicts when an agent reuses a tool-budget key with different parameters — retained, not replaced). Native and independent full-contract labels agree on all 30: 23 PASS, 2 FAIL, 5 UNKNOWN (Table 2); goal-only checking gets the same 23 passes but collapses every UNKNOWN into FAIL — in the absent-trusted-reply condition, five episodes where the agent asked for clarification and received no bound answer are misread as failures. The evidence interventions are the sharp result: hash-only checks accept 36 of 48 edited views whose bytes stay consistent while specification/seed/instrument/scope bindings no longer match; full-binding checks reject all 48 (6 unavailable, 42 invalid), and instrument-binding edits leave historical judgments readable while current reports are withdrawn (Figure 3) — preservation and present use genuinely diverge. In the retail holdout, independent subgoal checks agree on all 192 executions (187 satisfactions, 5 violations) while native full-task grading reports 177 successes: ten completed return subgoals sit inside broader task failures, visible only because both objective scopes are retained (Table 9 / Figure 5). Case admission correctly refuses both full-contract failures as sources — which also means the forward effect of activation/use/withdrawal is implemented and software-tested but unmeasured for lack of an eligible source.
  • Limits (from the paper's own reporting): small, single-system studies — 32 administrations across three authored tool-worlds, six Studio programs, one retail benchmark slice; no cross-agent comparison is supported under the registered-runtime interface; target stochasticity prevents attributing the trusted-reply contrast solely to reply availability; the case-lifecycle's downstream value is unmeasured (above). I found no standalone Limitations section; these bounds are stated inline where the results are reported.
  • Why it matters for BQAA: this is a schema paper wearing a systems paper's clothes. Its three-way split — execution history (fixed), contract verdict (three-valued, UNKNOWN is a real state), present-use qualification (rechecked against bindings) — maps directly onto warehouse practice, where eval records are routinely reused after harnesses, judges, and even task specs have moved. The 36-of-48 hash-only result is the quotable warning: byte integrity is not binding integrity. If BQAA retains scores, it must retain what they were bound to, and be able to withdraw present use without deleting history.
