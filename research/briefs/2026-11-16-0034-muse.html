arXiv:2611.18471 — Do Verifier Judgments Survive Abstraction? Measuring Verification Transport for LLM Agent Evaluation

Disha Sheshanarayana and Sai Linisha Reddy Goli (Manipal University Jaipur); arXiv v1 submitted Nov 13, 2026; NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper. Catalog searches for the exact id and title found no existing item.

Full PDF read: all 29 pages, extracted with pdftotext -layout and read completely in chunks: abstract; §§1–7; references; Appendices A–G including construction algorithm, metric definitions, full prompt templates, failure decomposition, diagnostics, bootstrap analysis and limitations; Tables 1–5 and Figures 1–6 captions. Full paper.

What it contributes. Verification Transport asks a narrow, well-controlled question: if a verifier is correct on the native view of a frozen execution, does it stay correct when only the verifier-visible representation changes? Task, trajectory, outcome, ground truth, criterion and verifier are held fixed (§3). Three boundaries are tested — macro abstraction (composed operations behind one boundary), delegation (a block behind an agent boundary, constructed without rerunning any worker), and history compaction (earlier evidence compacted, recent pair retained) — each with a paired restoration that reintroduces the exact stored provenance (§3.3, Appendix B, Table 3). Four protocols span direct judgment (V-LLM), evidence-sufficiency gating (V-EVIDENCE), provenance requesting (V-AGENTIC), and a second-stage audit of a frozen first judgment (V-DELIBERATIVE) (§3.4). Scale: 250 AgentProcessBench + 758 ATBench + 441 PlanRewardBench + 754 AgentDojo eligible instances, seven verifier backbones, temperature 0 (§4).

Results worth acting on:

  1. Transport failure is large and boundary-dependent. Denominator-weighted VTFR (share of native-correct judgments lost) is 31.0% macro, 30.9% delegation, 20.3% compaction (§5.1, Tables 1–2). Per-benchmark macro/delegation ranges are 25.5–32.2% / 26.1–32.9% versus 16.2–24.7% for compaction. Native-view accuracy alone therefore overstates deployed verifier reliability whenever traces cross composition or delegation boundaries — the normal BQAA case.
  2. Lower VTFR can mean more confident error, not more reliability. Under macro abstraction, V-LLM fails as 15.8% wrong verdict + 0.7% no verdict; V-EVIDENCE as 10.6% + 24.0%; V-DELIBERATIVE as 9.4% + 39.9% (Appendix E.1, Table 4). Aggregate VTFR by protocol (macro/delegation/compaction): V-LLM 16.4/17.3/14.1%, V-AGENTIC 25.0/23.6/18.2%, V-EVIDENCE 34.5/34.0/18.7%, V-DELIBERATIVE 49.3/50.2/30.7% (§5.1). On binary benchmarks V-LLM also has the highest false-certification rate, 41.3% (Table 5). Report wrong-verdict and coverage separately; a forced-choice judge that never abstains will look "robust" while certifying bad executions.
  3. Provenance fidelity, not re-asking, drives recovery. Exact restoration recovers 85.5% of transport failures overall while degrading 5.2% of survivors; macro RRR 87.9%, delegation 86.9%, compaction 79.8% (§5.1). The fidelity ablation is monotone Requery < Lossy < Exact in all four model–benchmark cells — e.g. APB/Gemini 22.5% → 42.8% → 80.0% (§5.2, Figure 4). Lossy restoration there means ordered tool identities without arguments, observations or text. For BQAA: keep boundary-linked, selectively restorable provenance (arguments + observations), not just span names.
  4. Deliberation without new evidence hurts. The V-DELIBERATIVE audit repairs 8.7% of initially wrong/non-operational judgments but degrades 28.5% of initially correct ones under abstraction (Appendix E.2, Table 5); coverage falls 94.6% → 67.3%. A second look at the same impoverished view is not a substitute for fetching the missing child trace. V-AGENTIC requests provenance in 37.4% of abstracted cases (43.3% macro, 44.5% delegation, 24.3% compaction) and retains 93.3% coverage.

Limits. Transformations are synthetic views of frozen trajectories, not live system abstractions; restoration assumes original provenance is retained and reintroducible (Appendix G). VTFR conditions on native-correct subsets, which differ by protocol, so cross-protocol VTFR gaps mix robustness with subset composition (§5.1). Severity ablation endpoints rise consistently (e.g. APB 24.3%→31.4%) but the A50 midpoint is not always monotone (§5.2). BQAA interpretation (proposed, not demonstrated in BQAA/ADK): add a transport check to evaluator qualification — same sessions scored from full trace, composed-tool view, delegate-collapsed view and compacted view — and store verdict, coverage/abstain and provenance-restored verdict as separate fields.

arXiv:2611.17632 — Generated Evaluation Suites as Diversity-Driven Search for Real Agent Regressions

Pulkit Yadav (independent); arXiv v1 submitted Nov 13, 2026; NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper. Catalog searches for the exact id and title found no existing item.

Full PDF read: all 9 pages, extracted with pdftotext -layout and read completely: abstract; §§1–6; references; Appendices A–G (operators, grader validity, full airline/retail tables, ablation protocol, threshold sensitivity, run configuration); Tables 1–6. Full paper.

What it contributes. A generated suite is an archive indexed by requirement × persona × scenario-kind; scoring it by mean pass rate discards the index (§§2–3). The paper's reusable object is the coverage ceiling: if a defect's traced footprint contains b baseline-passing scenarios in a suite of N, its within-footprint contribution to the suite drop is at most b/N — computable from baseline runs before any defect exists (§3.3). Detection is pre-specified as Δ > θ = 0.10 with a 2,000-sample paired-bootstrap interval excluding zero. Setup: τ²-bench airline (90 generated scenarios, 30×3 grid; 50 hand-authored) plus a retail replication for two operators; 7 single-instance defects (delete/invert policy clause, remove/rename tool, always-escalate, truncate policy/history); one model family as agent, simulator and judge (§3, Appendix G).

Results worth acting on:

  1. The ceiling binds before the defect is run. The generated suite detects 1 of 7 airline defects and the hand-authored suite 0 of 7 under the specified score; 4 of 5 traceable defects have ceilings at or below θ on both suites (§4.1, Table 1). R1 (deleted 24-hour cancellation clause) has generated-suite coverage 0/90, ceiling 0.00 — a hole the archive can report about itself. The ceiling count is θ-sensitive (only R1 bound at θ=0.05; Appendix F, Table 6), so publish the ceiling curve, not a single threshold verdict.
  2. A saturated breakage can read as an improvement. Removing cancel_reservation (T1) fails every covered baseline-passing hand-authored task (3/3 airline, 6/6 retail — exactly its ceiling), yet whole-suite pass counts move 25→27 of 50 and 20→21 of 48 (§4.1). Scoped (per-footprint) scoring recovers it: Δ +0.43 airline S1, +0.67 airline S3, +0.43 retail S1, all intervals excluding zero (Table 1) — with a rerun caveat below. Averaging does not merely dilute; it inverts sign.
  3. Two regimes need two units of scoring. Trace-aligned defects (requirement/tool-shaped) need per-cell scores; trace-orthogonal defects (behavioural, e.g. always-escalate O1: transfer share 36%→84%) can be detected whole-suite even at traced coverage zero (retail O1 Δ +0.17 [+0.04, +0.31]), while programmatic end-state grading misses O1 and the transcript judge sees it (+0.22 / +0.20) (§4.2). Judge–checker agreement is only 75%, κ=0.21 airline (Appendix B), and judge strictness concentrates exactly on O1 — disagreement itself is a cheap missing-cell pointer (§5, unevaluated proposal). Kind diversity helps where it aligns: at fixed size 30, happy-path-only detects 0/7; kind-diverse draws detect O1 in 5 of 20 (§4.3, Table 5). Persona count is confounded with size and claimed as no curve.

Limits. Pilot scale by the author's own framing: one instance per operator per domain, one rollout per configuration, same model as agent/simulator/judge, S1 intervals about ±0.15 (§6). Scoped scores, grading views and ablations are post hoc; T1's three-rerun diagnostic shrinks scoped Δ to 0.14 (mutant 0/21, baseline only 3/21) (§4.2). Retail covers 2 of 7 operators, run after airline results were known (Appendix D). BQAA interpretation (proposed): store requirement/tool/behaviour descriptors per eval scenario, publish per-cell Δ and the b/N ceiling alongside any suite mean, and treat a zero-coverage cell as a finding, not a pass.

arXiv:2611.17440 — Interpreting Coding Agents Through Their Behavioral Trajectories

Ibragim Badertdinov and Alexander Golubev (Nebius); arXiv v1 submitted Nov 12, 2026. Catalog searches for the exact id and title found no existing item.

Full PDF read: all 18 pages, extracted with pdftotext -layout and read completely in chunks: abstract; §§1–6; references; Appendices A–F (codebook and metric dictionary, consensus-audit scope, split-half null, retrieval features/ablations, paired case studies, effort decomposition, compute); Tables 1–12 and Figures 1–2. Full paper.

What it contributes. A deterministic rule-based parser normalizes heterogeneous coding-agent traces into shared actions (primary operation among ten categories, plus target-file role, mutation type, read mode/scope) and six workflow metrics, applied to 143,209 trajectories (134,329 Python + 8,880 multilingual) from 125 configurations / 117 models on SWE-rebench (§3). Parser, pipeline and an annotated subset are released (§1; §3 fn.2). Reasoning text is explicitly not inspected; claims are about observable tool behaviour (§3).

Results worth acting on:

  1. Matched outcomes hide different workflows — calibrated against a null. All 60/60 scaffold-matched pairs within 1.0pp solve rate (≥50 shared tasks) separate on ≥2 of six metrics; 58/60 (96.7%) on ≥3, versus 0.78% of same-stack split-half null replicates (12,000 splits) separating on ≥3 (§4.1, Tables 1–2, Appendix B). A solve-rate tie is not behavioural equivalence; BQAA comparisons need an A/A null before calling two stacks "the same."
  2. Stack = model + scaffold + effort, measurably. Higher configured effort buys exploration and post-write inspection, not more tests: Opus 4.8 in Claude Code, Low→xHigh, total actions 31.3→54.4, exploration/read +12.71, post-write inspection 12.7%→66.8% of rollouts, formal tests flat (+0.71, CI includes zero), unproductive tail unchanged (−0.50) (§4.2, Table 3; Appendix E, Table 12). Scaffold dominates interface mechanics (native-edit share 37.0% generic vs 92.4% in the displayed Claude Code stack, Table 4). Attribute behaviour to the deployed stack, never to the model name alone.
  3. Behavioural profiles retrieve the stack without outcomes or tool names. An 8-feature profile (action shares + entropy/switch/inspection/unverified-termination; outcomes, token counts, action magnitudes and native tool names excluded) retrieves the exact stack across disjoint repository halves at 85.1% Top-1 / 96.8% Top-3 (111 stacks, chance 0.9%), and a 12-feature profile across languages at 90.3% / 98.8% (16 stacks, chance 6.25%) (§4.3, Table 5; Appendices C.2–C.3, Tables 7–8). Action composition carries most signal (87.5% Top-1 alone multilingual). Useful for drift/attribution dashboards — and a privacy/governance warning the authors flag themselves (§5).

Limits. The parser audit is a six-LLM consensus (≥5/6) on 500 events, not human labels: primary-operation agreement 82.4%, target-role 77.0%, mutation 68.8%, read mode 96.6% (§3.3/§4.1, Table 1; Appendix A.3) — path/mutation-derived metrics are diagnostic proxies, and sequence metrics (repeated failures, tail) are not validated by the audit. Cross-repository retrieval does not fully control task composition, so it does not prove task-independent signatures (§4.3); worst multilingual split Top-1 is 56.3% (Table 8). Single benchmark regime; taxonomy is SWE-specific (§5). BQAA interpretation (proposed): adopt the pattern, not the taxonomy verbatim — a versioned action codebook over BQAA spans, outcome-matched stack comparisons with a split-half null, and profile-drift monitoring keyed by full stack identity.

Six-week EV

Highest expected value: one evaluator-qualification experiment combining all three papers, not three separate projects. This is a proposed plan, not work performed:

  • Weeks 1–2: freeze a paired session set with known outcomes; define a versioned BQAA action codebook (per 2611.17440's pattern) and per-scenario descriptors (requirement, tool, behaviour) for the eval suite (per 2611.17632). Publish baseline coverage and b/N ceilings per cell.
  • Weeks 3–4: score the same sessions under four views — full trace, composed-tool, delegate-collapsed, compacted — with direct vs evidence-gated vs provenance-requesting judges (per 2611.18471). Report wrong-verdict, abstain/coverage, per-cell Δ and restored verdict separately, each against a split-half same-stack null.
  • Weeks 5–6: keep only what survives: a judge/view combination whose transport failure and false-certification stay inside a predeclared budget at acceptable provenance-fetch cost; otherwise fix capture/provenance first and re-qualify. Kill criterion: if per-cell scoring and exact provenance do not change any shipping decision on the paired set, stop and keep the simpler judge.

Payoff: evaluator changes stop being confounded with trace-representation changes. Risk: synthetic view transforms may not match production compaction/delegation; validate on naturally occurring delegate spans before generalizing.

TL;DRs for BQAA

  • Verification Transport (2611.18471): qualify judges across trace views; keep exact, boundary-linked provenance restorable; never merge wrong-verdict and abstain into one accuracy number.
  • Generated suites ceiling (2611.17632): publish per-cell deltas and the baseline b/N ceiling with every suite mean; a zero-coverage requirement cell is a finding.
  • Behavioral trajectories (2611.17440): compare full stacks (model+scaffold+effort) on normalized action profiles with an A/A null; solve-rate ties prove nothing about behaviour.
  • Industry: no response warranted; watch the OpenAI Agents tool-approval alpha only at stable.
