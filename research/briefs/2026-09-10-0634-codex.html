ATR — NEW rank after full-PDF adjudication

From Version Conflicts to Decision Conflicts: Selective Revalidation for Long-Running AI Agents, Yongjian Lyu, Yang Ren, Ruofei Lai and Wenting Liu; Huawei/Xi'an Jiaotong University. September 7 v1; retained queue item. All six PDF pages were read, including algorithm, results, limitations, artifacts and references. PDF.

ATR maps recorded dependencies to executable conditions and returns KEEP, REFRESH, REPLAN or BLOCK. Commit safety requires sound, complete premises and atomic enforcement of the checked evidence; external policy tokens omitted from that binding remain a race. Its 210,000 executions repeat 15 deterministic cases, not distinct tasks. A scheduled policy-change example defeats target-only CAS; missing dependency edges produce false allows. These are mechanism tests, not production failure estimates. The reported microsecond timings exclude connector/network/model costs and other lifecycle work. No LLM or distributed production evaluation is established. PDF, §§2–7.

Admit narrowly: useful execution-control methodology, with dependency/binding limits attached; no general agent-safety or end-to-end speed claim. The paper identifies an artifact at 89c32fa; repository retrieval failed here, so no reproduction is claimed. PDF screenshots were unavailable; complete extracted PDF text was reviewed.

BQAA/ADK application — editorial proposal: use an analytical approval that waits before execution as the fixture. Keep the selected input facts fixed except for one controlled mutation. Test an irrelevant display change, a changed but still adequate budget, withdrawn permission, a changed required business rule, and a missing dependency. Record what was checked, the governing versions, the decision, and the actual execution outcome separately. First establish which system can enforce each condition at dispatch/commit. A log should retain an unresolved enforcement boundary instead of presenting a successful preflight as an execution guarantee.
