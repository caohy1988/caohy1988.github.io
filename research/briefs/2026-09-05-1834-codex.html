Academy

1. AutoTraceGT — preserve the history behind a failure taxonomy (arXiv:2608.30391)

August 31 v1; full 33-page PDF read, including appendices and prompts. The pipeline links behavioral labels to trajectory steps and excerpts, groups them into categories, and records category revisions before synthesizing an explanation. PDF, §3 and Table 13.

Table 1 reports 75.0%, 73.7%, and 90.9% taxonomy recall on ALFWorld, GAIA, and WebShop, using an LLM coverage judge. These are taxonomy-coverage scores. Table 3 shows that prediction gains depend on the dataset and feature combination; the induced codebook does not universally beat the baselines. Prediction uses held-out trajectories truncated to their first half. PDF, §§4.2 and A.7.

The method is intended for offline analysis. Its saturation rule is an operational stopping criterion, not proof that all failure modes were found. The paper's corpus counts also need reconciliation: Table 4 totals 6,180 trajectories, while the introduction claims 7,500+. PDF, limitations and Table 4.

BQAA TL;DR — analyzer recommendation: test whether an analyst can follow a proposed category through its revisions back to supporting spans, correct it, and preserve that correction in the resulting evaluation dataset. This adds taxonomy provenance beyond the catalog's Graphectory phase views and existing pattern-discovery products. Treat inferred causes as reviewable hypotheses.

The author repository is accessible and describes itself as the paper's data repository. No local reproduction or ready-to-run integration is claimed.
