Academy

1. HookPry — a model-visible trace can miss a host-side action

arXiv:2609.03884 · submitted September 3 · full PDF, 18 pages, §§2.4, 5.1–5.2 and 6; Table 2.

Lifecycle hooks can dispatch commands when runtime events fire, without the model selecting those commands. HookPry tests malicious updates to previously accepted plugin configuration. Across 40 cases × 25 harness/backend combinations, the authors report 770 fully successful, 34 partial and 196 failed runs. Full success requires the prescribed external effects to be observed; starting a process or emitting a tool call is insufficient. Partial outcomes receive no success credit. The headline 92.5% is the best per-harness aggregate; 77.0% is the overall run-weighted success rate.

These are controlled experiments with synthetic assets. Installation and update adoption are assumed, and success depends on event occurrence, authorization and subprocess permissions. The study excludes sandbox escapes. Its findings establish a capture boundary under the tested configurations, not the compromise rate of installed production agents.

BQAA + ADK judgment. Extend the existing capture-completeness example with a benign callback that changes a test artifact outside the model's tool-selection path. Preserve the configuration version, triggering event, execution identity and independent effect evidence. Keep initiation, partial effect and confirmed completion distinguishable. This gives the earlier Flight Recorder completeness concern a concrete runtime fixture.

2. DuplexSpeechBench-IFEval — preserve timing evidence beside the transcript verdict

arXiv:2609.03423 · submitted September 3 · full PDF, 27 pages, §§3–6 and 9; Table 3; Appendices E.7 and F.

The benchmark expands 240 conversation setups into 1,038 cases across eight roles and five conditioning protocols. It separately reports deterministic instruction adherence from synchronized user/model audio and persona adherence from a transcript judge. For F-Actor, explicit behavioral instructions yield 35.5% instruction adherence, versus 25.8% when behavior must be inferred from the persona: a 9.7 percentage-point gap. Those scores measure timing/action compliance, not overall conversational quality.

The useful measurement detail is retained per-case timing: verifier thresholds can be changed without rerunning the model or voice-activity detector. Threshold sweeps preserve the complete six-model ordering of the explicit-versus-persona gap in only 20% of configurations. This is a gap ranking, not a ranking of overall model quality.

The benchmark uses English, scripted synthetic speech and two-turn interactions; the cases are expansions of shared setups, not 1,038 independent conversations. Results include architectural turn-taking constraints. Persona scores depend on a GPT-4o judge and do not validate timing or safety-critical domain competence.

BQAA + ADK judgment. In the existing streaming-evaluation example, retain the observed timeline, target action, timing measurements and verifier version beside the transcript and content score. Make a timing-threshold change explainable without changing the recorded episode. Use this as a measurement fixture within existing work; it does not justify a voice-evaluation product start.
