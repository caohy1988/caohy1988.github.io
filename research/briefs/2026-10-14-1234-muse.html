What Counts as the Same Task? Executable Contracts for Robustness Evaluation of Tool-Using Agents

  • Full PDF read. v1 [cs.SE] 10 Oct 2026 (announced in last night's batch). Shahriyar, Iftee, Barton & Wright (RIT / Naval Postgraduate School).
  • The question: robustness and agent-security evals score agents on modified task instances (injected notes, perturbed fields). A result on a modified instance only says something about the original task if the modification is still a valid test of it. The paper's running example is exact: a hostile invoice note saying "pay B" is a valid hostile candidate (correct payee still A); changing the trusted payee field to B is a different task wearing a small edit; disabling the usual payment endpoint changes the plan, not the task. Scoring all three as one perturbation class makes it impossible to tell agent failure from task change from vanished solution.
  • Method: an executable task contract states, before any agent runs, the protected facts and relations that define the task, the permitted edits, the authorized outcomes, and the accepted completion plans. A candidate is valid when it passes four checks — well-formedness (K), permitted-edit compliance, protected-fact preservation, and feasibility (an accepted plan still completes it). The authors compare validity policies by scoring the same 14,336 stored agent runs under each rule (four agents × prompts over 1,792 instances), plus finite-state diagnostics and an AgentDojo adapter study (3,168 trajectories) where the contract protects entity-identifying fields at injection points.
  • Results: across four encoders, embedding-threshold policies admitted 75–100% of task-changing substitutions — invoices naming different payees sit close in embedding space, so similarity bounds structurally cannot protect task-defining facts. Schema policies (any well-formed edit to an injection field) fail the same way when the editable field is the task-defining one. Replaying a saved reference solution is shown to be neither necessary nor sufficient evidence of validity (reference success ⇏ same task; reference failure ⇏ infeasible; Table 2). Contract authorship choices matter measurably: on the same 192 candidates, a primary-route-only specification admits 64, an either-route specification 80, and a recipient-ignoring negative control 128 (Table 3) — the contract makes that evaluator judgment explicit and auditable instead of hiding it in a threshold.
  • Limits (stated by the authors): contracts do not discover the correct task definition — they require structured specifications naming protected facts, allowed edits, authorized outcomes and feasible completions, which open-ended tasks may not provide; they do not establish semantic equivalence or agreement with every human judge; the controlled corpus uses deliberately structured cases that show the tested policies can fail, not how often they fail in practice; specification counts reflect three specifications the authors wrote, not measured evaluator disagreement.
  • Why it matters for BQAA: this is the ingest-side twin of last night's oracle-failures paper. Warehouses routinely join perturbed/robustness runs back to their base tasks and aggregate them as one task family. If the validity decision that admitted a perturbed run was an embedding threshold, the aggregate silently mixes different tasks. A stored, executable contract (protected fields + permitted edits + authorized outcomes) is a schema-level artifact BQAA can version alongside the task — and "which contract version admitted this run" becomes an answerable query instead of folklore.

Trace Evolution Bench: Measuring Whether Agents Improve from Their Own Environment Traces

  • Full PDF read. v1 [cs.AI] 10 Oct 2026 (announced in last night's batch). Li, Zhao, Wang, Tian & Guo (MatrixOrigin).
  • The question: self-evolving agents are usually judged by endpoint Gain (final held-out success minus round-0 success). The paper argues — and then measures — that Gain conflates true evolution with the variance of simply re-scoring a frozen agent, plus protocol leaks (teacher traces substituted for self-traces, held-out IDs entering the update, the evaluated checkpoint differing from the artifact evolve() returned, text and weight updates compared on one budget axis).
  • Method: Trace Evolution Bench (TEB) is an attribution protocol with four controls: locked held-out identifiers (never visible to evolve()), validation-gated accept/rollback per round, repeated frozen no-evolve rescoring to measure each seed's own noise band (max−min across four passes), and seed-matched paired excess — a method's Gain minus the same model+seed's no-evolve Gain, only credited when it exceeds that seed's band. Three frozen 80/20/40 packs (140 tasks each; held-out n=40, 2.5pp per task; Table 3): coding (primary; hidden unit tests in a closed workspace), office, and manufacturing as secondary lanes.
  • Results: frozen DeepSeek v4 Pro under no-evolve keeps version M0 yet its held-out score range across four passes spans 7.5, 5.0 and 12.5 percentage points across seeds on coding (Table 6) — i.e. double-digit "improvement" is available from rescoring alone. Under paired excess, seven of nine DeepSeek text-update cells exceed their own seed bands (Table 8/Figure 4), but the pattern does not reproduce on GLM or MiniMax (Table 9/Figure 6), and no method stays above its band on every seed of all three models. On office, DeepSeek does not exceed its bands (widest 17.5pp); on manufacturing there is no paired positive excess beyond band at all. The screen round's gains (0, −5.6, −11.1pp) did not predict held-out excess (Table 4).
  • Limits (stated by the authors): coding held-out is one task family (fix-bug, n=40) so per-task granularity is coarse; validation is n=20 (5pp per task); office effects under the 17.5pp band are unresolved by this design; some cells are single-seed or impaired (crossed evolution-token cap, unpaired round-0) and are flagged rather than hidden; closed hosted models were not weight-updated, and the weight track is a different budget class — historical weight-grid cards are excluded over a trace-provenance mismatch the appendix documents in detail.
  • Why it matters for BQAA: "the agent improved after we fed it its own traces" is exactly the claim trace warehouses will be asked to substantiate. TEB's controls translate directly into stored artifacts: the frozen-rescore band per seed, the locked held-out ID set, accept/rollback records, and paired excess instead of raw Gain. A warehouse that stores only endpoint scores cannot distinguish evolution from rescoring weather — this paper puts a number on that weather (up to 12.5pp) and a contract around the claim.
