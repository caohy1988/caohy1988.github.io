ClawTrack — process scores filter lucky passes (arXiv 2607.28037, submitted Jul 30)

Wu, Zhu, Liu, Liu, Wang, Guo, Li, Cao, Cai. A trace-level agent benchmark that scores every run twice: a Task Score (did it achieve the outcome) and a Process Score (was the reasoning sound), the latter graded per step on goal alignment, efficiency, information utilization, and result verification, anchored by 12,541 task-specific rubric items across 320 tasks, 8 domains, and 25+ deterministic mock services; 21 models evaluated over 16,000+ trials. The point of the split: outcome-only metrics can't see "lucky passes" — right answer, broken reasoning — and the paper reports result verification as the systematic bottleneck across models. They also show trajectory filtering on process scores yields consistent post-training gains across model scales. The wrong belief to kill: "a golden match is a pass." Judgment: peer evidence, and the third independent paper in four days (after Fidelity Is Not Enough and CURA) saying the trajectory, not the answer, is where truth lives.

TL;DR for BQAA. Same lane as EV #5 but one step further: trajectory_verified beside match_golden_qa gives you a dual-threshold "reliable pass" — outcome AND process — which is exactly ClawTrack's contract. The 12.5k-rubric scale also says EvalBench's 8-metric rubrics are the right genre at modest scale; no schema change needed, cite this when defending the two-column design.

AgentEval — attribution works when the schema records the edges (arXiv 2604.23581, submitted Apr 26)

Guo, Wu, Yiu. Formalizes agent executions as evaluation DAGs: each node carries LLM-judged quality metrics, failures are classified through a 21-subcategory taxonomy, and nodes link to upstream dependencies so root cause attribution runs along recorded edges. Numbers, on 450 test cases across three production workflows: failure-detection recall 0.89 vs 0.41 for end-to-end checks (2.17×); dependency modeling alone adds +22 points of failure detection and +34 points of root-cause accuracy; 0.84 Cohen's kappa with human experts; root-cause accuracy 72% against an 81% human ceiling; a 4-month pilot cut median root-cause time from 4.2 hours to 22 minutes. The wrong belief to kill cuts against our own pessimism: LongRCA's 24.1% exact-step number is what attribution looks like without recorded structure — when the schema captures dependency edges, RCA reaches 72% against an 81% human ceiling. Attribution isn't hopeless; it's schema-starved. Judgment: peer evidence, the strongest yet for edge-grain attribution as a warehouse feature.

TL;DR for BQAA. Direct support for EV #7 (fault_edge + fault_side) and a concrete addition worth folding into that row rather than adding a new one: persist a parent_step_id on events so blame can travel edges — the +22/+34-point deltas came from dependency links existing at all, and agent_events is where they'd live.
