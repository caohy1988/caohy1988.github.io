3. DNative-Twin: Decision Graphs and Digital Twins for Reconstructable Agentic Decisions (submitted Sep 3, cs.AI)

Junjie Pang, Zhenzhen Xie, Haoke Han, Ying He, Jing Wang, Gang Liu; Qingdao University, Shandong University, Changchun University of Technology, and a Hangzhou partnership.

The problem statement is the one BQAA lives in: "A final output alone cannot show which evidence, tool state, rule, authorization, or action path produced it," and existing records "leave three parts disconnected: what the agent observed, how it acted, and what authorized the resulting action." The paper's answer is a typed, time-attributed heterogeneous graph with four entity families (decision context, execution, authorization, decision) and a minimum node set: DecisionObject, EvidenceArtifact, KnowledgeUnit, PolicyRule, Agent, Skill, ToolCall, DecisionEvent, AuthorizedActor, DecisionRole, ReviewEvent, AuthorityGrant, Recommendation, CommitmentEvent, OutcomeSignal. Two design rules carry most of the weight. First, a Recommendation and a CommitmentEvent are distinct nodes: "an agent recommendation and the action that gives it practical effect" are recorded separately, and a committed trajectory exists only when an applicable authority path (AuthorityGrant authorizes AuthorizedActor, who commits) is reachable. Second, every node records identity, time, type, environment and source, with extra references only where reconstruction needs them: version references on evidence, execution references on tool calls, authority references on commitments. Table II is the comparison a TL will want: it rates the "agent trace" record type Strong on model and tool execution path, Partial on evidence version, and Weak on both authorization/commitment and outcome feedback.

The replay half is a digital twin: copy the decision-relevant state, replay the mechanism with tool calls in one of three declared modes (reuse the recorded response, execute a read-only copy, or a consequence-preserving stub), perturb one declared dependency, diff the trajectories, and classify each difference as benign, material, critical or unresolved. The ToolCall node records which replay mode was used. The evaluation builds graphs from three public process logs (OCEL 2.0 procure-to-pay, BPI 2020, BPI 2019; up to 225,290 nodes on 5,000 BPI 2020 cases, median 8.889 s end to end at that size) and runs controlled injections. The result that generalizes: a graph-only detector classified all 40 injected unobserved tool-timeout cases as benign, because "an unrepresented tool state leaves no structural evidence from which to determine its consequence." In the 300-instance three-condition experiment, adding replay-contract tool state lifted unresolved-divergence recall from 0 to 0.667 and adding verification results lifted it to 1.0. Repeated replay under a fixed temperature-zero contract matched exactly 30 of 30 times, while the external model probes (DeepSeek, Kimi) reproduced their own labels 70 to 96 percent of the time. Limits stated by the authors: enterprise process logs only, overlay fields supply what the logs lack, no held-out critical-class instance, one platform for timing.

TL;DR for BQAA. Two schema notes, one new. New: an agent's proposed action and the committed action are different events, and the committed one should carry an authorization reference (the approver, role, or standing grant that let it happen) and a link back to the proposal. BQAA today can record a tool call, but it cannot distinguish "the model said approve" from "the approve was executed under grant X" without the application inventing fields. Strengthened: the evidence-version references on every node are the source-event-ids note from 1001, now with an authorization edge added. The tool-timeout result is a reminder that the tool-call event must record the tool's failure state as a value, not as an absence, or downstream analysis will call the timeout benign.

4. Clean Engineering, Unstable Measurement: A Preregistered Reliability Failure of Black-Box LLM Observers on Shared Endpoints (submitted Sep 3, cs.AI and cs.LG)

Haoyuan Zhu (University of Sheffield) and Jie Zhang (Ranplan Wireless, Cambridge AI+).

This is a 35-page negative result with an audit chain, and the most useful eval-methodology paper this week. Two preregistered campaigns tried to use an LLM judge (a Qwen 3.7 endpoint at temperature 0) to read solution progress from partial arithmetic traces; neither got past validating the judge. Same-window repeat rankings agreed at Spearman 0.400 against a frozen gate of 0.90; byte-identical next-day replays of 100 frozen requests reproduced the exact ranking 78 times against a gate of 0.99, while "every recorded metadata field" (response model string, system_fingerprint, finish reason) was constant. Three mechanisms: a label-to-meaning mapping bias as large as the signal (flipping which letter means "correct" inverted rankings, median Spearman −0.632); candidate score gaps seven orders of magnitude below the instrument's noise floor (median adjacent gap 3.04e-10 versus median repeat delta 0.0067); and byte-identical inputs returning different rankings. Neither metric substitution (the same 22 drifted replays read 0.78 exact, 0.95 top-1, 0.986 pairwise) nor sampling (a 748,000-call simulated design passed 0 of 500) repaired it.

The prospective supplements are the part to keep. Same-day versus cross-day replays differ by 0.005 (0.805 vs 0.800), replicated over five more days, so the drift is an instantaneous nondeterminism floor, not overnight model rotation. Four providers in three jurisdictions (DeepSeek, Mistral, OpenAI gpt-4.1 dated snapshot, Qwen) sit on the same floor (same-day full-ranking agreement medians 0.74 to 0.88), and the fingerprint field predicts none of it: absent on two, churning through 19 values in a day on one, stable and information-rich but still insufficient on the fourth. A self-hosted batch-invariant vLLM arm reached 0.987 while quiet and fell to 0.89 under eight concurrent streams. A constructed-error battery shows the judge separates wrong from right by error type, not error magnitude. The paper converts this into a three-level snapshot-identity ladder (L0 model name, L1 provider-pinned snapshot, L2 self-hosted weights hash plus batch-invariant kernels plus pinned hardware), eight design rules, and a 24-item reporting checklist whose first four items are collectible "by instrumentation alone": snapshot-identity level, requested versus response-side model string distribution over the run, fingerprint status measured over the run, and probed parameter handling per provider. Rule 9.5 is the operational one: on shared infrastructure "a single call is one draw," so fix k, the aggregator and the gate before execution, keep every draw, and gate on the aggregate (Borda-of-5 reaches 0.98 agreement on their measured spectrum where single calls sit at 0.80).

TL;DR for BQAA. Eval rows produced by an LLM judge need three fields they mostly do not have today: the response-side model string as returned by the provider (not the requested name), the fingerprint or deployment id as returned (including null), and the draw index plus aggregation definition when a score is one of k calls. Without the first two, a score drift between Monday and Tuesday cannot be classified as judge drift versus platform drift. The same fields belong on BQAA's ordinary LLM-call events, where the requested model and the served model already diverge for aliases. This is a column note for the eval-events table, not a feature.

5. Interface-Induced Trajectory Censoring (submitted Sep 3, cs.AI)

Wenbo Wang, City University of Hong Kong, single author.

The claim: "The observed tool-call rate is not a property of the model alone; it is a property of the model–interface stack that measures it." On BFCL v4's own data, executor and scorer, changing only the vLLM serving adapter moves the same Qwen2.5-Coder model from 0.00 to 0.96 (simple_python) and 0.19 (multi_turn_base); a 2×2 over chat template and parser finds both main effects exactly zero and the entire effect in their interaction, so "no component is defective." On tau-bench retail, the same swap moves server-parsed calls from 0 to 636 and tasks reaching any tool execution from 0 to 103, with 789 assistant turns in the unrepaired arm carrying a call-shaped payload the server discarded. The scale ladder is the striking table: across Qwen2.5-Coder 1.5B to 32B under the documented configuration, the server parses 0 of 100 at every size while well-formed emitted calls rise 0, 4, 21, 36, 80 (about 72 at 32B after a human-validated correction factor). Under a matched envelope (Qwen3) the silent fraction stays at 0 to 2. Four families fail at four layers (template, parser, schema, token), and only Mistral's failure is loud; the other three return HTTP 200 with an empty tool_calls array, "byte-identical to a model that declined to call the tool." Llama-3.1-8B's 23 percent rate of calling the task function as a tool falls to 0 under one strict:true flag, which reassigns two fifths of an apparent protocol effect to the interface. Inside verl's training loop at 7B, 45 of 115 generations carry a complete call and 0 execute, so the tool branch gets no on-policy gradient. Repairing the adapter restores the mechanism (parse 0 to 84, rescues 0 to 9) but the pass-rate gain does not survive multiple-comparison correction, and the author says so. The discussion asks evaluations to report four numbers, not one: server-parsed calls, emitted-but-unparsed calls under a stated criterion, request errors, and the exact serving configuration. A 98-line preflight script is released.

TL;DR for BQAA. BQAA's tool-call events are post-parser by construction: they exist only when the runtime recognized a call. Two consequences. First, the raw model response content must be retained on the LLM-call event even when the tool-call list is empty, because "emitted but unparsed" is a query only if the bytes are there (the paper's criterion is a regex over content). This matters for ADK users on LiteLLM or self-hosted backends more than for Gemini on Vertex, but the schema should not assume the provider parsed correctly. Second, the serving configuration (parser, chat template, strict mode, server version) belongs on the LLM-invocation event as attributes for self-hosted models, or a tool-call rate cannot be compared across deployments. The preflight check is a docs example: alert when a tool-bearing agent shows zero parsed tool calls over its first N sessions.

6. Fresh Memory, Stale Plans: Dependency-Scoped Validation for Distributed LLM-Agent Memory (submitted Sep 3, cs.AI)

Evan Chen and Christopher G. Brinton (Purdue), Shiqiang Wang (University of Exeter).

The failure they name is stale-plan execution: an executor can hold the latest requirement r4 and still act on a plan derived from r3, because "state freshness does not establish that the plan authorizing an action remains valid." PlanFence fixes it at the action boundary. Every derived public record carries the exact immutable record IDs it was produced from; each tool wrapper declares the keys that can affect its action; before an external call the executor walks the plan's parent chain to the declared keys and asks each key's owner for its current head, replans once on mismatch, and blocks on any missing parent, missing declaration, or unavailable owner. In 30 live five-agent Qwen3.5 workflows with one requirement revision inserted after planning, a freshness-only executor acted on the obsolete plan in 30 of 30 tasks while PlanFence and centralized lineage completed 30 of 30 with one replan each. Controlled replay across 32,700 scheduled actions found no invalid action for any exact-lineage policy and 4,143 for the stale or delayed-sync controls. The cost boundary: per-update synchronization has lower stall at low churn, PlanFence wins from about four updates per action upward (1.5× to 7.1×), and against an equally safe all-key check it holds traffic near 8 KiB per action while the all-key check grows to 82 KiB at 128 keys. An appendix shows that omitting half the true dependencies from a tool's declaration raises unsafe issuance to 37 percent, and that a learned policy selector does not dominate a two-line rule out of distribution. The authors are explicit that these are safety and systems-cost results, not accuracy gains.

TL;DR for BQAA. Fourth paper this week (after EAL-Bench, MCP-to-A2A, and Sybil) whose mechanism is "this record cites the exact records it derived from." The additional element here is the tool-side declaration: a tool call carries the list of state keys its action depends on. For BQAA that is the same nullable repeated field of source event ids on the plan or message event, plus an optional list of declared dependency keys on the tool-call event. Schema note strengthened; still not a roadmap row.

7. Judging LLM-as-a-Judge: Concerning Rubric Artifacts in LLM-based Automated Text Generation Evaluation (submitted Aug 31, cs.CL; cross-listed to cs.AI in the Friday batch)

Anshul Bagaria, Sowmya S Sundaram, Gokul S Krishnan, Balaraman Ravindran; CeRAI, IIT Madras.

Short paper, one clean experiment. Train a classifier on rubric text alone, with no access to the evaluated response, to predict the LLM judge's pass/fail label. On HealthBench (clinician-written per-conversation rubrics, about 43k rubric instances) and ResearchRubrics, PubMedBERT reaches held-out balanced accuracy of 0.80 to 0.88 across four candidate-model cohorts; TF-IDF plus logistic regression also beats the majority baseline, and the signal transfers across the HealthBench Eval and Hard splits. The lexical story is that rubrics with fail, not, should and justification language predict negative labels. The counterfactual half is the part that bites: with conversation and rubric fixed and the candidate response reversed by construction, the Qwen2.5-7B judge flipped its verdict in only 37.7 percent of 493 pairs; with response fixed and the rubric criterion reversed, it flipped in 16.8 percent (Gemma responses) and 32.2 percent (Llama responses). One judge model, two benchmarks, binary labels, no causal mechanism claimed.

TL;DR for BQAA. If eval rows carry a rubric id and version alongside the score, then "how much of this evaluator's score is predictable from the rubric alone" and "does it flip when the answer flips" become offline queries over the eval table rather than a paper. Column note for eval events (rubric_id, rubric_version); the counterfactual flip rate is a docs example, not a feature.

Multi-agent governance; the observability content is that inter-agent messages were logged and readable, which BQAA already does.
