IBIB/IB2: measuring enterprise AI systems by serving route — arXiv:2609.10494

NEW Academy rank, narrowly scoped: evaluation protocol and reporting discipline for served systems — methodology card, not leaderboard evidence. Blake Stenstrom, Charangan Vasantharajan, Brian Sathianathan (all Iterate.ai — an enterprise-AI-systems vendor). History: September 9 v1, 17:31:29 UTC, no revisions, cs.CL/cs.AI/cs.LG, 42 pp., "Preprint. Under review." The complete PDF was read (adjudication note at Hunt/agent-analytics/2026-09-10-0034-ibib-2609-10494-full-pdf-adjudication.md). PDF.

What survives review. Table 1 audits exactly 18 capability benchmarks and finds them scoring advertised model identifiers while usable capability depends jointly on weights, serving route, precision, output contract, and harness (MLPerf binds the stack, but for throughput — route binding is unique to IB2 among capability benchmarks). The three-part protocol is real and specified: (1) gold-blind capability-binding preflight (Algorithm 1, 10 predicates verifying the route actually executes before tasks run); (2) reliability-inclusive first-pass scoring (failures stay in denominators; unsupported work is never zero-filled); (3) structurally score-blind adjudication (gold and scores absent by type from the adjudicator's projection). The strongest measured result: accuracy-only rescoring flips the cohort leader (GPT-5.6 Luna 78.65→90.10) and reorders the field — reliability inclusion is substantive, not cosmetic.

Evidence and limits from the PDF:

  • The headline 77.38→82.54 (interval [0.11, 10.60]) is verified verbatim but is a joint endpoint effect, not a route effect: arms (Qwen3.8-27B, identical weights/revision/BF16, RunPod fair-v5 vs AkashML fair-v6) differ simultaneously in route, harness generation, and serving tool-call parser; the paper calls it a "joint endpoint effect" and refuses attribution. n=1 per arm; the interval is a task-set bootstrap, not run variance; the 0.11 lower bound is smaller than the paper's own 1.30–2.86-pt unattributable Stage-0 leverage. Pack correction: "serving-arm choice moved precision" overstates a comparison the paper itself declines to attribute.
  • Unmitigable conflicts: authors' employer sells competing enterprise AI systems; the benchmark bank was built against its own systems; the corpus is sealed (reader-unfalsifiable, conceded by the authors).
  • Saturation: 4 of 7 suites are saturated — discrimination lives only in the governed-DB suite (S7) and multi-tab joins (S2), the most BigQuery-relevant cells. Leader gap 0.97 vs interval [−2.13, 4.26]: no ordering within the top nine systems should be cited.
  • Self-reported defects (credit for honesty, but they bind the card): the PARTIALCOVERAGE branch is unreachable in v0.13.2; the full binding gate was first attested 2026-08-13, after two of the three route runs (those findings are retrospective); the gate demonstrably missed once (GLM passed all 10 predicates, then failed strict-JSON in production); authors demoted their own T1–T3 tiers (fail multiplicity/BCa).
  • The paper found no documented instance of actual benchmark misattribution — the defect it names is an unreportable quantity, not a caught fraud.

Why admit: the procedural contribution — bind the evaluation to the served route before scoring, keep failures in denominators, adjudicate score-blind — survives the paper's unusually aggressive self-criticism and maps directly onto how agent-analytics products should report served-system quality. The empirical cohort is illustrative only.

BQAA TL;DR — proposed application: any BQAA model comparison must name the serving route, precision, output contract, and tool-call parser as part of the measured system, or the number is unattributable — and a "model upgrade" that silently changes the harness is a confounded experiment. Reliability-inclusive scoring (failures in denominators) should be the default presentation, with accuracy-only shown as the variant, since the choice flips leaders.

IdeaAMBIG: specification blockers are the bottleneck, not clarification — arXiv:2609.10539

NEW Academy rank, narrowly scoped: specification-readiness as a measurable pre-codification gate, with quantified clarification-loop upside. Yiling Ma, Yilun Zhao, Sihong Wu (Yale), Manasi Patwardhan (TCS Research), Arman Cohan (Yale). History: September 9 v1, 17:59:04 UTC, no revisions, cs.CL only (correction: the pack's cs.SE/cs.AI cross-list claim is refuted), 74 pp.; an under-review ACL submission (not peer-reviewed). The complete PDF was read (adjudication note at Hunt/agent-analytics/2026-09-10-0034-ideaambig-2609-10539-full-pdf-adjudication.md). Dataset/code released (github.com/Yiling-Ma/IdeaAMBIG); released JSONLs spot-checked: 163 real + 497 synthetic = 660 lines, matching Table 3. PDF.

What survives review. IdeaAMBIG tests whether research-method specifications carry enough information for a coding agent to build the intended system without unsupported assumptions: 660 evidence-grounded instances (163 real — 121 GitHub issues + 42 reproducibility reports; 497 controlled synthetic gaps injected into reconstructed codification-ready specifications backed by verified reproductions), 13 LLMs, three tasks (readiness assessment, defect localization, clarification generation). Headline, verified with page-level evidence: the strongest model (GPT-5.6-Sol) achieves 9.6% Macro Defect Recovery Rate on real-world instances from raw specs (95% CI [3.1, 18.7]) versus 80.6% Macro Clarification Action Success when the defect location is given (CI [70.1, 89.0]).

Evidence and limits from the PDF:

  • The 9.6% is a strict conjunctive metric (same-blocker localization plus both taxonomy labels, matched by Claude Opus 4.8). Raw localization (Loc-Acc) is 16.0%; taxonomy-free human-judged blocker identification on a 50-sample is 40%. Do not read 9.6% as "finds nothing."
  • The contrast the pack omitted: end-to-end clarification without the defect is only 13.6% Macro-CAS (Table 9) — "given-defect 80.6% vs raw-spec 13.6%" is the paper's actual thesis, and it is stronger than the pack's framing.
  • 14%→98% oracle readiness is an upper bound (n=50, blind human-judged, p<0.001), per the paper itself.
  • Ranking instability: 5 of 6 GPT-5.6-Sol vs Claude Sonnet 5 paired comparisons are not significant — the top-2 ordering should not be cited.
  • Validation is solid for a preprint: judge–human agreement 85–93% (κ 0.69–0.81), annotation κ 0.84–0.92, human baseline 52% Macro DRR (models far below experts), target-uniqueness audit. Caveats: construction used DeepSeek-V4-Pro/GPT-5.5 while DeepSeek-V3.2 is evaluated; a Claude judge scores Claude Sonnet 5; no pretraining-contamination analysis.

Why admit: every collector claim survived the full-PDF read; the dataset is released and count-verified; the methodology (conjunctive metrics, source-clustered CIs, human baselines, judge validation) is unusually careful. The finding — blocker discovery, not clarification, is the bottleneck — is directly actionable for agent evaluation design.

BQAA TL;DR — proposed application: build specification-readiness as a measurable pre-flight gate for analytical tasks: before the agent runs, score whether the request pins the partition filter, date range, aggregation granularity, and authorization scope. The paper quantifies the upside — pointing at the defect is worth ~67 points of clarification success — so the gate's job is finding the blocker, and cheap deterministic checks (missing filter detection) beat asking the model to notice.

TrajMark: ownership and tamper localization for agent trajectories — arXiv:2609.10416

NEW Academy rank, narrowly scoped: provenance primitives for exported trajectory datasets, under an explicitly bounded threat model. Bokang Zeng, Zheng Gao, Xiaoyu Li, Xiaoyan Feng (UNSW), Jiaojiao Jiang (Griffith). History: September 9 v1, 16:33:02 UTC, no revisions, cs.CR/cs.SE, 41 pp. The complete PDF was read (adjudication note at Hunt/agent-analytics/2026-09-10-0034-trajmark-2609-10416-full-pdf-adjudication.md). PDF.

What survives review. Two layers over coding-agent trajectories (targeting the case where exported trace datasets lose wrapper metadata): a sparse owner layer encoding a 6-bit deployment ID by rewriting an HMAC-keyed subset (p=0.30) of READ-action basenames into masked GF(2)^6 linear equations — zero added actions, batch-level recovery with abstention; and a fragile localization layer of linked keyed-HMAC seals (ordinary/group/terminal) chained over critical-action segments. Verified numbers: 95.5%–100% edit detection, 95.8% localization, Pass@1 26.9% watermarked vs 26.3% unwatermarked.

Evidence and limits from the PDF:

  • Detection is single-site only (Fig. 3's exhaustive attacks); the one proved theorem covers a fixed role-preserving segment substitution — insert/delete/carrier cases are empirical.
  • 95.8% localization is pooled region coverage, not action-level (Table 7): the narrowest accepted region is median 15 / mean 19.8 visible actions ≈ 37–48% of an average 40.9-action trajectory; OpenHands collapses to 34–49% at 50% corruption. "Localizes the tamper" overstates it by an order of magnitude.
  • The seals are keyed HMAC commitments, but the visible check is digest mod 12 — a ~1/12 collision floor; authors explicitly disclaim freshness, replay, and non-repudiation, and say signed audit logs are preferable when a sidecar survives. Not "cryptographic seals" in the audit-log sense (pack wording corrected).
  • Pass@1 26.9 vs 26.3 is statistically unchanged (sign test p=1.00; 1,350 matched outcomes/method across SWE-bench Python + SWE-PolyBench Java/JS; DeepSeek V4 Flash / GPT-5 mini / MiniMax M3) — a benign-overhead result, not an improvement.
  • Threat model is a keyless, one-shot, non-adaptive editor; compound/multi-site attacks, splicing, replay, oracle attacks, and key compromise are explicitly excluded. Owner label fixed at 0x3f (label invariance untested live); baselines are adaptations (AgentMark-U), not originals; no code/data release anywhere in 41 pages; AI-assisted workflow disclosed.

Why admit: the problem — proving ownership and localizing tampering in exported agent-trace datasets after wrapper metadata is stripped — is squarely on-brief and otherwise untreated; the paper is honest about its bounds; and the design (embed provenance in the actions themselves rather than the envelope) is a genuinely different point in the space. All three collector claims verified; the card carries every qualifier above.

BQAA TL;DR — proposed application: if BQAA ever exports trajectory/trace datasets for external benchmarking or dispute resolution, envelope metadata will not survive the export — provenance has to live in the records. But take the paper's own hierarchy seriously: where a signed audit sidecar exists, use it; embedded watermarking is the fallback for when it doesn't, and a mod-12 visible residue is a tripwire, not a proof.
