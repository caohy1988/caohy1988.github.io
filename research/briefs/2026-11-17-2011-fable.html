Field Brief — 2026-11-17-2011: Leaderboard ranks are weak evidence for buying a SWE agent (add-on)

Writer: Fable (claude-fable-5-2) For: Haiyuan Cao, TL of BQAA (BigQuery Agent Analytics) + ADK Delivery: Markdown handoff only; Collection owns HTML, catalog and push to https://caohy1988.github.io/research/. Scope: user-requested add-on (Haiyuan, 20:11 PT Tue Nov 17, 2026); one Academy card.

Read this first

  • On public Holistic Agent Leaderboard (HAL) traces, paying more often buys no proven accuracy gain: 27 of 35 steps along seven benchmarks' accuracy-cost Pareto frontiers are statistically unresolved.
  • Reported costs differ from standardized token-based estimates by up to 11.7×.
  • Successive halving reaches the paper's selection target on SWE-bench using 6.6% of exhaustive evaluation spend, if task costs are known in advance.

Academy

Beyond Leaderboard Ranks: Selecting Software Engineering Agents Under Budget Constraints

Srinath Srinivasan and Tim Menzies (North Carolina State University). arXiv 2611.21515, v1 submitted 2026-11-16. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21515

Data and method. The authors reconstruct 58,197 run-task records from public HAL trace archives; the main case is SWE-bench Verified Mini (34 runs, 48 shared tasks). Costs are recomputed from token logs on one standardized price sheet. Run pairs get an exact McNemar test with Benjamini-Hochberg correction; "unresolved" means the test cannot separate two runs, not that they are equal. Selection policies are then replayed on the recorded outcomes under dollar budgets.

Findings.

  1. Accuracy. Across seven benchmarks, 27 of 35 frontier steps are statistically unresolved. On SWE-bench, a run costing $34.16 trails the $170.78 leader by 14.6 percentage points, but the available tasks cannot statistically distinguish their accuracy. The paper estimates 80% power there needs a true gap of 20.7 percentage points.
  2. Cost. Across 333 runs, the published-to-reconstructed cost ratio has a median of 1.00 and a maximum of 11.7×; the extreme is consistent with cached tokens being counted twice. Keeping only mid-difficulty tasks (oracle filter) underestimates full-benchmark cost by 20.4% on SWE-bench.
  3. Selection. With task costs known in advance, successive halving reaches the uncertainty-aware selection target (Q=0.80) on SWE-bench after $353.62 of a $5,399.89 exhaustive evaluation, 6.6% (6.55% in Section VI). It is cheapest on six of seven benchmarks and ties on the seventh; without advance cost checks, on four of seven.

Limitations the paper states. Policies are replayed on fixed historical outcomes, not fresh runs; advance cost knowledge is generally unavailable in live evaluations. HAL is a convenience sample; the software-maintenance conclusions rest on SWE-bench alone. The target credits any affordable pick the tests cannot separate from the best, which can favor policies on low-resolution benchmarks. The evidence is comparative, "not universal superiority of successive halving".

Why it matters for BQAA/ADK. Writer's reading: three questions for any agent-evaluation record. Is the gap between two agents larger than the task count can resolve? Is cost computed from token counts, cache included, on one price sheet? Is cost estimated from a representative task sample?
