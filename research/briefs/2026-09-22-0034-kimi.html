NEW rank: RRSI: Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses (arXiv:2609.24972) — harness self-editing with an acceptance ledger: every proposed edit must clear a noise-adjusted score floor and a Ridge-style cost-justification rule before becoming permanent state; unregularized baselines gain on the evolve set and regress out-of-distribution (two of four end below the unevolved harness)

v1 2026-09-21 (single version; 24pp). Peng Xia (Google Cloud AI Research / UNC), Rujun Han, Zifeng Wang, …, Tomas Pfister, Chen-Yu Lee (Google Cloud AI Research). Claim-checked against Tables 1–5.

  • Verified: Terminal-Bench 2.1 evolve split +14.1 (64.6→78.7) with the weaker Gemini 3.5 Flash policy, +6.0 with Claude Opus 4.8; five OOD benchmarks all improve (JobBench +4.7, GDPval +3.5, APEX-Agents +3.7, SWE-bench Verified +1.8, Frontier-Eng +4.3 medal points) — no held-out regression, while baselines AHE/TTHE end OOD below H0 (the overfitting phenomenon demonstrated, not asserted). Token cost 2.42M vs 3.80M/trial = 36% fewer than unregularized evolution (the pack/abstract "30%" understates). Cross-policy transfer to an unseen backbone (+30.4% rel) shows mechanisms aren't backbone artifacts.
  • The machinery is governance-shaped: L0-style annealed edit budget, leakage critic screening benchmark-specific edits before scoring, noise-adjusted acceptance floor calibrated from repeated base evals, ΔC ≤ β0 + β1ΔS cost-gain rule, Lasso-style pruning of unproductive components. Hyperparameters fixed without consulting held-out sets.
  • Qualified: the intro's "up to 22.9% over average prior baseline" does not reconstruct from Table 1 (max ~+14.8% vs weakest baseline's OOD average) — not carded. No error bars/significance on finals (k=2 trials/task); Google-authored with Gemini judges in places (mitigated on GDPval by a 3-vendor panel with position-swap); Harvey LAB evolve gain only +1.1 (disclosed as the intended tradeoff).

TL;DR for BQAA: the closest thing yet to a governance framework for self-modifying agent harnesses — a per-edit attribution ledger (component, hypothesis, diff, Δscore, Δtoken-cost) with acceptance gates. The adaptive-overfit framing (they cite Dwork et al. on holdout reuse) and ΔS/ΔC bookkeeping are a blueprint for harness-change observability and eval-integrity monitoring.

2. NEW rank: Harness-Zero: Harness Distillation via Agent-as-Harness (arXiv:2609.24974) — use a frontier agent as a private reviewing harness to distill harness-induced behavior into a 9B student's weights (23.3% → 44.3% macro-avg, beating 41.7% with the harness attached) — and the striking negative result: teacher trajectories collected under the harness distill worse than base (3% vs 12%)

v1 2026-09-21 (single version; 25pp). Haoran Ye, Yuxing Lu, Haonan Dong, Zhaochen Su, Guojie Song (PKU / Google / HKUST). Claim-checked against Tables 1–4.

  • Verified: agent-as-harness 81.1% vs code-as-harness 78.1% averaged over 6 settings — but the win is average-only (loses AppWorld on DeepSeek-V4-Pro, ties on GPT-5.6 Sol). Distillation macro-avg 23.3%→44.3% vs 41.7% harness-attached (arithmetic re-verified); 82.3% behavior recovery across 28 patterns (unweighted mean recomputed: 2305/28 — note pattern selection makes base=0% by construction, denominators as small as 10 tasks). Deployment keeps only a minimal one-bash-tool harness.
  • The negative result that matters for eval design: harness-specific traces are non-portable supervision — teacher-under-h⋆ distillation lands at 3.0% vs 12.0% base due to action-space mismatch. Agent-as-harness is net-harmful on weak models (Qwen3.6-35B: 36.0 vs 48.0 plain meta-harness, 66% intervention rate — disclosed).
  • Qualified: single seed (42), single-run pass@1, no error bars; small eval splits (100/168/100); requires a frontier-grade reviewer (GPT-5.6 Sol); collection cost doubles (extra call per step, 2.4× latency on USPTO: 237.2s vs 100.1s).

TL;DR for BQAA: quantifies the model-vs-harness attribution problem (which layer owns the gain) and prices harness mediation in trace terms (one full-trajectory review call per step, 2.4× latency — a concrete per-step cost multiplier). Its appendix is effectively a trace-attribution workflow: deterministic detectors over trajectories checking whether harness-induced behaviors actually fired — a template for "did the harness component fire and did it help" telemetry.

v1 2026-09-21 (single version; 27pp). Zhilin Wang, …, Yi Dong — all NVIDIA. Claim-checked against Tables 1–2 and Fig. 5.

NEW rank: GRUET: Quantifying Uncertainty of Agentic Reasoning-and-Acting Processes (arXiv:2609.24831) — per-turn reasoning modeled as a DAG with uncertainty propagated through it beats all 10 standard LLM uncertainty baselines on multi-turn agent trajectories — every one of which is near-random at 40 turns — and the first turn alone carries nearly all the signal at 1/L of the sampling cost

v1 2026-09-21 (single version; 29pp). Shuang Liang, Xin-Yu Hu, Shao-Qun Zhang (Nanjing University — academic). Claim-checked against Tables 2/3/6/7.

  • Verified: averaged over the LLM×benchmark grid with 1,000 bootstrap resamples: GRUET all-turn AUROC 66.11 / AUPRC 61.10 / AUARC 51.91 vs strongest contender (Semantic Entropy) 58.38 / 44.68 / 40.13 — AUPRC +16.42 and AUARC +11.78 match the text. Minor internal wrinkle: the claimed AUROC gain 17.11 doesn't equal the table-mean difference (66.11−58.38 = 17.73); aggregation-order artifact, flagged.
  • The negative result is the headline for practitioners: all 10 contenders (P(True), verbalized confidence, perplexity, semantic entropy, eigenvalue/degree methods…) fail the authors' 3σ-above-random rule on agent trajectories; only GRUET passes. Standard LLM UQ does not transfer to 40-turn ReAct processes.
  • First-turn variant: comparable AUROC using only the first turn's K−1 extra samples — 1/L of the all-turn cost.
  • Qualified: "nine LLMs × five benchmarks" is a subset grid, not full factorial (small models omitted from SWE-bench at 0% solve; large from InterCode at >0.9 accuracy); code-gen benchmarks only, binary rewards; white-box (needs token logprobs) + K=10–15 samples/turn + NLI and embedding models — heavy per-trace overhead; absolute AUROC ~0.66 is moderate.

TL;DR for BQAA: a concrete recipe for a calibrated credibility score per agent trajectory — the "trace → reliability" primitive for flagging high-uncertainty runs for review or abstention. The first-turn variant makes it cost-plausible, and the all-baselines-near-random finding is a citable negative result for anyone shipping trace-level confidence dashboards.

NEW rank: One to More, More to One: Category-Aware Iterative Expert Training for Software Engineering Agents (arXiv:2609.23377) — the "category see-saw" is measured, not rhetorical: under pooled RL, per-category gains move oppositely across 50 checkpoints while the aggregate stays flat (30.3% of training instances regress even as averages rise); category-aware expert training doubles the gain (+5.39pp vs +2.7–2.9pp)

v1 2026-09-20 (single version; 44pp). Jie Zhao, Ziyu Jiang et al. — Alibaba Group. Claim-checked against Tables 7–14, 20.

  • Verified: Pro-618 58.04% MOPD vs 52.64% base = +5.39pp exact; SWE-bench Multilingual 59.00% vs 56.22% = +2.78pp exact (3-round means); paired instance-bootstrap 95% CI vs base [0.33, 5.22] excludes zero — but vs the strongest baseline (Balanced RL) [−0.78, 4.78] is not significant. Repair SFT recovers 61–73% of regressed instances. No external teacher trajectories (verified — experts self-generate from verifier-approved rollouts).
  • The analytics finding: aggregate resolution metrics conceal per-category regressions during agent improvement; only segmented longitudinal scoring with minimum-segment lift (Gsim: MOPD 4.76pp vs +2.44/+2.11) reveals it. Their Table 7 reward-integrity protocol (repo sanitization, fresh-sandbox patch replay, trajectory audits against reward hacking) is a concrete checklist.
  • Qualified: vendor self-evaluation (Alibaba training its own Qwen3.6-27B; labels by Qwen3.7-max; no external frontier comparison); Pro-618 is the authors' audited 618-of-731 subset of SWE-bench Pro — scores are not comparable to official SWE-bench Pro numbers (paper warns itself); 3 eval rounds only; no ablation isolating RRE vs MOPD.

TL;DR for BQAA: a training paper whose contribution to this mission is measurement discipline: segment-level drill-down vs aggregate KPIs, paired bootstrap CIs, fixed denominators, retained failure audits. The category see-saw is the argument for per-segment eval dashboards in any agent-improvement loop.

NEW rank: Jev for Scientific Decisions: Evaluating Semantic Choices and Their Consequences (arXiv:2609.24965) — the first independent academic evaluation of TypeSafe Jev: full semantic correctness (100/100) at ~4× lower median latency and ~50× lower per-request cost than the cheapest equal-correctness frontier model — on a deliberately small dev set, with a three-level eval contract that exposes errors label-only scoring hides

v1 2026-09-21 (single version; 11pp). Boyuan Deng (JHU), Shuyi Fan (Columbia), Hongyang Zhang (HK PolyU), Xinhong Xie (Penn State). Claim-checked against Tables 1/5.

  • Verified: 12 configs × 10 cases × 2 Choices × 5 reps; Jev 100/100 semantic, 50/50 downstream, 50/50 label — matched by five other configs at ceiling. Jev median 0.335s (p95 0.442s), $0.0595/1k requests (reported charges) vs next-best complete config Sol 1.385s, $3.047/1k (estimated). The durable finding: across 588 received responses, all 588 final labels correct but only 581 downstream outputs exact and 1,169/1,176 Choices correct — all 7 errors hit one Luria–Delbrück question, producing wrong reused quantities while the final label stayed green.
  • Qualified (heavily): 10 cases, 20 model-routed Choices, ceiling-saturated (6/12 configs at 100%); all reference labels "inconsistent" by design; every observed error on one question; no significance tests; Jev spec cited to the vendor; mixed reported/estimated costs; total workflow cost undetermined (authors' words).

TL;DR for BQAA: read the performance numbers as directional-only (tiny dev set), but the methodology is the contribution: a three-level eval contract (semantic selection → downstream exactness → final label) with planned denominators and missing-vs-wrong separation — demonstrating that label-only scoring hides wrong intermediate quantities that downstream analytics would reuse. That is the same failure class as an agent harness whose verdict is green while a traced intermediate value is wrong.
