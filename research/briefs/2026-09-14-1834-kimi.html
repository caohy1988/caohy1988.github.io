1. NEW rank: LAST-CQ — in text-to-Cypher recovery, the detector and retry router carry the loop; LLM-synthesized critique buys ~1 point over the raw DB error string

What Drives Recovery in Agentic Text-to-Cypher? LAST-CQ: An LLM Agent Self-Refinement Framework, Ioannis Prokopiou, Athanasios Aidinis, Panagiotis-Christos Kyrmpatsos, Pantelis Vikatos (Athens University of Economics and Business; Orfium). v1 2026-09-11, Sunday cycle; no v2. Full text read including Tables 1–6, ablations, Limitations, Appendices A–C. (PDF)

Dissects a five-agent execution-grounded pipeline (parse → generate → validate → correct → execute) on the executable subset of Neo4j's Text2Cypher benchmark — 2,471 question–query pairs per backbone × 6 backbones (14,826 model–query pairs; GPT-4o-mini, Gemini 1.5 Flash/Pro, Claude Sonnet 4, GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3) — with counterfactual ablations replacing each component. Verified findings:

  • Raw error strings ≈ LLM critique (Table 4): schema-grounded LLM-synthesized feedback scores EM 20.9% vs 19.9% for a naive retry on the raw Neo4j error string; end-to-end GLEU 0.4729 vs 0.4718 (<0.2%); TOST equivalence within ±0.075 set-F1 (p=0.002) on the 214-query paired subset — though not within ±0.05 (p=0.053).
  • The paper's own reversal, which the pack missed: 230/1,917 corrected queries (12.0%) have empty reference results that GLEU scores 1.0 for any non-empty output; excluding them, the naive retry beats LAST-CQ (GLEU 0.365 vs 0.350; set-F1 0.104 vs 0.070). The 1-point grounding edge is an artifact of forgiving cases.
  • Recovery driver (Table 2): 91.7% per-model mean recovery of single-pass failures, driven by failure detection routed to execution-grounded retry; first-pass successes cost exactly 1 LLM call; 53–97% of queries need no correction. Cost is metered in calls/stages (mean 1.1–2.5 calls), not tokens or dollars.
  • Parallel sampling degrades: best-of-3 at equal budget drops GLEU −10.8% (GPT-4o-mini) / −11.4% (GPT-4o) — with the authors' own caveat that temperature is confounded with selection and only 2 backbones were tested.
  • Metric and judge integrity (§7): n-gram serial overlap (GLEU) exceeds set-F1 on 65.9% of retained pairs, and 2.9% of set-identical pairs score <1.0 from ordering alone; a blind human calibration (n=122, single annotator) shows the LLM judge optimistic by 9 points (74.6% vs 65.6% valid), concentrated in PARTIAL-over-INCORRECT inflation.

Mandatory qualifications: judge circularity — GPT-4o-mini is both primary judge and an evaluated backbone, and the judge never scored baseline outputs, so refinement improving semantics is unproven; equivalence evidence rests on 214 paired queries / 2 of 6 backbones; single engine (Neo4j only); Gemini 1.5 retired mid-study (2.5 Flash substituted in the retry arm); no fine-tuned baselines (authors acknowledge fine-tuning gains exceed every inference-time result here).

BQAA/ADK application: the direct blueprint for BQAA's text-to-SQL/GQL recovery loop: spend the retry budget on detection + routing to execution-grounded retry, feed the raw engine error string, and skip the expensive critique-synthesis stage — then verify that on your own workload with the paper's honesty checks: exclude empty-reference forgiving cases, use set-equivalence metrics (order-insensitive), and calibrate the judge against blind human labels before trusting any "judged-valid" rate.

2. NEW rank: Skill Issue — GEPA-optimized repository SKILL.md files gain +4.9pp that the authors' own sign test cannot separate from run noise; carded as a measurement paper

Skill Issue: Lessons from Optimizing Repository SKILLs for Coding Agents, Mykhailo Kozyrev (TU Munich + JetBrains Research), Andrei Kozyrev (JetBrains Research), Anton Podkopaev (Constructor University + JetBrains Research). v1 2026-09-11, Sunday cycle; no v2. Pack correction: the 1532 pack's alternate author list (Kiselev/Gusarov/Veprev/Tankov/Bryksin) is fabricated — that roster appears nowhere in the paper. Full text read including appendices A–D. (PDF)

Builds a rigorous harness for a question every repo with a SKILL.md/AGENTS.md implicitly asks: does an optimized skill document actually help the agent? Tasks are mined by reverting merged PRs at one frozen base commit (three revert tiers, mostly an LLM reverter), graded by FAIL_TO_PASS tests, scored paired against the empty-seed rollout on the same task; Claude Code harness with Sonnet 4.6 frozen, Docker-isolated, .git stripped. Verified findings (Table 1, §4.2):

  • GEPA-optimized docs: +4.87pp mean paired score across three Kotlin repos (koog 0.525, kotest 0.554, ktor 0.567); resolved-task deltas +1/+3/+4 out of test splits of 23/20/26.
  • SkillOpt: +0.07pp mean — hiding a −6.3pp regression on ktor (0.437) that the flat average conceals.
  • The honest null (RQ3): a sign test over disagreeing tasks clears p=0.05 for no run (best p=0.29); the power bound says that at n=20–26 tasks the test detects a real effect only if the doc wins 4 of 5 disagreements. Important precision: the paper never measures run-to-run variance — inseparability is inferred from sign-test power, with one appendix anecdote (10 reruns of one task spanning CodeBLEU 0–1).
  • Costs and baselines: avg rollout $0.84, total $2,013.98 / 69.2h; empty-SKILL agents already resolve 53% of pooled tasks (vs gskill's saturated SWE-smith tasks); the seed is an empty header-only SKILL, so deltas are vs no document, not vs a handwritten AGENTS.md. A single maintainer review (n=1, koog) confirms the synthesized doc captures real tacit knowledge and would be merged as a draft.

Mandatory qualifications: headline +4.9pp is statistically null by the paper's own test — do not read this card as "GEPA improves skills"; GEPA-vs-SkillOpt confounds optimizer with budget (200 scored attempts vs 3 epochs); ~60–80% of graded tasks depend on an LLM reverter whose defects dynamic validation cannot see (caught only by static check, App A); single train/val/test split; Kotlin/JVM only; live-issue speed win is n=2 issues × 1 run.

BQAA/ADK application: the methodology is the card. BQAA ships and curates repository skill/context documents; this paper hands you the evaluation discipline: paired rollouts against a frozen empty seed, FAIL_TO_PASS-mined tasks, an explicit power floor stated beside any claimed gain (at n≈20–26, anything under ~80% win-rate on disagreements is undetectable), and per-repo breakdowns so a −6.3pp regression can't hide inside a +0.1 average.

3. NEW rank: Expert Re-Grading — 95.2% of audited frontier-model physics rejections are benchmark or grader defects; corrected ground truth lifts GPT-5.6-Sol 47.3%→78.7% on HLE-Physics

How Good Are Frontier Models at Physics? Expert Re-Grading Reveals Broken Evaluations and Near-Saturation of Leading Benchmarks, Ali Ansari, Haoran Sun, Andy Zeyi Liu, Mark Jabbour et al. (51 authors; Yale, Jump Trading, Cambridge, USC). v1 2026-09-11, Sunday cycle; no v2. Full text read including appendices A–G. (PDF)

A 51-author expert audit of six frontier physics benchmarks (HLE-Physics, CMT-Benchmark, CritPt, UGPhysics, PRISM-Physics, PHYBench), re-grading rejected model responses against problem statements and reference solutions. Verified findings:

  • Attribution (Table 2 / App. C): across 250 audited rejections on 4 benchmarks — 143 benchmark errors (57.2%), 95 grader errors (38.0%), 12 model errors (4.8%): 95.2% of audited rejections are benchmark/grader defects. Per benchmark the mix varies wildly: HLE-Physics 87.8% benchmark-error; PHYBench 71.4% grader-error (rule-based EED grader); PRISM 64.9% grader-error with zero model errors in the audited 74.
  • Score repairs (Table 1): GPT-5.6-Sol (model name as printed) mean@4 on HLE-Physics 47.28%→78.66% (pass@4 55.94%→91.38%); CMT-Benchmark mean@4 61.00%→87.24%; CritPt corrected pass@4 94.44% on 54 retained challenges.
  • Evaluator-choice signal: the HLE-adapted LLM judge had the lowest grader-error rate of audited evaluators at 4.08%; rule-based graders dominated the grader-error class.
  • Independent corroboration: Anthropic's own CritPt correction (Fable 5.1 system card; 31/71 problems revised; mean@16 = 88.4%) is genuinely independent support, noted by the authors.

Mandatory qualifications (load-bearing): (1) Failure-only selection — for 4 of 6 benchmarks only rejected questions were audited, so 95.2% is a rate among rejections, not a benchmark defect rate; (2) denominator shift — corrected scores are computed on retained/repaired subsets (HLE-Physics drops 86/202 questions, 42.6%, before the 78.66% is measured): the delta conflates grading fixes with question-set curation, and the paper itself flags this; (3) CritPt's pre-audit figure (Artificial Analysis mean@5 32.29% on 70 challenges) is a different metric and set — never compared directly with corrected mean@4/pass@4, and no pre-audit pass@4 exists; (4) auditors for 4 benchmarks were physics PhD students, not faculty; double-review raw agreement 71.43% with no kappa reported; two authors are Jump Trading-affiliated (funder).

BQAA/ADK application: the transferable audit design for any BQAA eval suite: classify every scored failure into {benchmark defect, grader defect, model error} on a sample before drawing capability conclusions; report the corrected score with its retained-set denominator beside the original; and track grader-error rate as a first-class property of evaluator choice — an adapted LLM judge at 4.08% beat rule-based graders here. When a BQAA benchmark says a model "can't reason," the base rate to beat is now: most audited rejections aren't the model.

4. NEW rank: Judging by the Cover — a 6-feature question-blind probe beats the TruthfulQA leaderboard (0.689); Audit-Prune cleans the benchmark to AUC 0.528 while preserving model ranking

Judging by the Cover: Cleaning LLM Truthfulness Benchmarks to Avoid Surface-Level Feature Leakage, Foad Namjoo (Utah), Remy Ogasawara (Utah), Amirali Abdullah (Thoughtworks), Cullen Anderson (UMass Amherst), Narmeen Fatimah Oozeer (Martian AI), Jeff M. Phillips (Utah). v1 2026-09-11 (cs.CL), Sunday cycle; no v2. Pack correction: only three of six authors are University of Utah. Full text read including appendices. (PDF)

Shows binary-choice truth benchmarks leak their labels through surface form, and ships a repair. Verified findings:

  • The leak (§2.1, Tables 1/13): a logistic probe on six surface features — negation-led, not punctuation/capitalization as the pack claims (the real six: neg_lead, neg_cnt, hedge_rate, word_count, avg_token_len, type_token; dropping the negation group cuts AUC 0.715→0.592) — scores accuracy 0.689 / AUC 0.715 [0.688–0.738], p=0.0001, on the 790-pair binary TruthfulQA re-release, without ever seeing the question. That beats the best llm-stats leaderboard model (66.9%).
  • The repair (Algorithm 1): Audit-Prune greedily removes the pair whose within-pair feature gaps most align with dataset-wide class gaps, refitting the audit each step with add-back passes. The released TruthfulQA-476 (60.3% retained, all 37 categories covered) drops residual leakage to AUC 0.528 (accuracy 0.522, p=0.048) — where fixed-prefix removal cannot reach ≤0.58 at all and AFLite at matched N=476 leaves 0.603.
  • Ranking fidelity (§3.2): TruthfulQA-476 vs the full set on a 14-model open-weights panel: Spearman ρ=0.915 [0.81, 0.99] — but note the paper reports no per-model score deltas, only ranking fidelity, and Audit-Prune sits at the lower edge of random-476-subset fidelity (ρ=0.961±0.034).
  • The strongest evidence the leak is exploitable (Table 3): classifiers trained on the full set collapse below chance on a surface-inverted adversarial cohort (0.267–0.427); the same classifiers trained on TruthfulQA-476 recover (0.450–0.634; McNemar p<0.001–0.008). The SURFACE6 probe itself scores exactly 0.000 on the flipped set.
