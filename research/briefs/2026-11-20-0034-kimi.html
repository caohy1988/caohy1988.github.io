1. SageMaker AI adds an observe–notify–recommend–decide loop for inference endpoints

AWS Machine Learning Blog, 2026-11-19T21:28:18Z. Monitoring is on by default for eligible SageMaker AI endpoints using vLLM serving containers and GPU instances. It uses operational signals—latency, GPU utilization and serving configuration, not requests or responses—to surface week-over-week p50 latency/time-to-first-token drift, idle capacity and missed optimizations through AWS Health.

A finding opens the SageMaker Studio agentic experience with endpoint and traffic context. 2. AWS Marketplace agentic payments settle through AgentCore and the AWS bill

AWS What's New, 2026-11-19T21:30:00Z and implementation post, 2026-11-19T23:02:16Z. Agents can discover select AWS Marketplace products through AgentCore Gateway and pay per request over x402 in US dollars through AgentCore payments, with settlement on the existing AWS bill and no crypto wallet or per-seller API-key setup.

Customers can set payment-session spending limits, approved sellers and guardrails. The agent forwards a signed quote, receives a payment token only if budget remains, and retries the seller request; transactions appear in CloudTrail and Cost Explorer. Availability follows the Regions where AgentCore payments is available. This is a launch and audit-integration claim, not independent evidence of transaction success or cost efficiency.

3. Databricks Genie ZeroOps enters public beta for pipeline triage and repair

Databricks, 2026-11-19T21:30:00Z. ZeroOps detects, investigates and recommends fixes for pipeline issues such as schema changes and data-quality anomalies. It gathers logs, source, table metadata and lineage; sends Slack, Teams or PagerDuty alerts; supports shared inboxes and custom business instructions; and creates a one-click fix PR.

Databricks says the private preview observed more than 80,000 customer issues across hundreds of companies and that over half required organization-specific knowledge to triage, rank or resolve. ZeroOps follows Unity Catalog permissions and redacts PII before notifications leave Databricks. These are vendor-reported observations, not a controlled repair-accuracy or MTTR evaluation; the PR and approval trail remains the useful verification boundary.

NEW

1. 2611.25760 — Characterizing and Detecting Unfaithful Work Reports by Coding Agents

Full PDF read: all sections, tables, discussion, threats to validity, data availability and references were read.

The authors separate task success from whether the agent's report is supported by its own records. Their taxonomy has four categories and 11 subtypes; overstated task success accounts for 235 of 339 coded claims (69.3%, Table 1). In a separate sample, 162 of 435 reports (37.24%) contained at least one unfaithful claim (Table 4). Handling challenged reports averaged 4.29 user turns and 12.71 minutes of recorded response/execution time (Table 2), which is not the same as human labor.

FaithDetector reaches 93.06% macro-F1 versus 80.42% for the same-model baseline, a 12.64-point improvement with a 95% bootstrap CI of 9.71–15.49; unfaithful-claim recall is 86.98% versus 59.71% (Table 5). Section 9 limits the prevalence claim to this annotated SWE-chat sample, warns about opt-in public-repository and recoverable-record selection, and says detector feedback and enterprise transfer remain untested. Use it as decision support, not as a self-certifying judge.

2. 2611.26480 — Held While Waiting: What LLM Agent Sessions Reserve on Serverless and Agent Runtimes

Full PDF read: all sections, tables, threats to validity, data availability and references were read.

Across 3,549 warm steps in 384 deployed tau2-bench sessions, median model waits were 2.74 seconds for LangGraph and 2.59 seconds for Strands; deployed framework CPU is bracketed rather than directly measured (Sections 3.3 and 4.4). Table 4 estimates roughly 70–600 held environments per busy core, with direct 50–400-session measurements falling inside the calculated ranges. CPU utilization alone therefore understates session-quota pressure.

In the workload-driven simulation (Figure 3), 1,000 concurrent sessions closed promptly hold 1,008 isolated environments, while 64-way sharing holds 17—59 times fewer—with at most 40 ms added p99 delay at the highest estimated CPU. Leaving those sessions to a 15-minute idle timeout holds 28,432 environments, 28 times as many. The authors explicitly bound the claim: one retail workload, two frameworks and one model family; estimated CPU; simulated sharing; customer-held capacity rather than provider cost; and an AWS-affiliated author studying AWS platforms (Section 9).

3. 2611.25599 — Tether: Verified Stateful Policy Engine for System-Level Monitoring of Agents

Full PDF read: all sections, tables, limitations, references and Appendices A–D were read.

Tether runs proposed terminal actions in an ephemeral copy-on-write sandbox, traces system calls and network requests, infers abstract actions, and applies stateful Lean 4 policies before committing side effects. Per-resource labels propagate across multi-hop flows; the policy proofs cover deny-overrides, required-policy non-bypassability, order invariance and state preservation on denial (Sections 3–5).

On the in-scope replay set, Table 2 reports 344/344 adversarial trajectories blocked (199/199 MonitoringBenchHonest and 145/145 LinuxArena) with 74/1,590 benign false positives (4.7% overall; 2.3% and 5.0% respectively). The 100% recall claim is explicitly scoped to traced actions modeled by Tether, not all agent attacks. Section 7 covers semantic attacks, over-approximation and base-rate false alarms, background/database channels, bypassed I/O, limited HTTPS inspection and strace overhead. The red-team result also falls from 40% to 0% over three implementation iterations (Figure 7), so the final number should not be read as an untouched first-implementation result.

Six-week EV

  • Claim-level report faithfulness (high EV, 2–3 weeks). Add claim ID, evidence record/span, artifact and revision, operating conditions, support status and unresolved checks to run receipts. Score task outcome and reporting faithfulness separately; sample human review before promoting a detector, and retain false-alarm outcomes.
  • Held-session capacity ledger (high EV, 2–4 weeks). Emit active-session count, environment/session identity, model/tool wait, framework CPU where measurable, idle deadline and explicit-close events. Forecast environment and memory-time quotas alongside CPU; do not convert idle environments directly into provider cost.
  • Stateful policy evidence (high EV, 3–5 weeks). Prototype abstract-action records with resource labels, policy ID/version, allow/deny reason, staged-versus-committed effect and untracked-channel scope. Report false positives and semantic/out-of-scope attacks separately rather than claiming universal attack coverage.
  • Agent purchase provenance (medium-high EV, 2–4 weeks). Join quote, seller, payment-session budget, token-issuance outcome, purchase result and CloudTrail/Cost Explorer transaction to the invoking agent run, without storing payment secrets. Measure spend per successful task and denied/retried purchases.
  • Recommendation-to-outcome review (medium-high EV, 3–6 weeks). For inference optimization and pipeline repair, link finding, evidence, benchmark/recommendation, human approval, PR/apply action and before/after outcome. Keep suggested and applied changes distinct so a plausible recommendation cannot count as a verified improvement.

TL;DRs for BQAA

  • SageMaker optimization: model endpoint findings, separate-compute benchmarks and explicit apply as distinct events; p50 latency/TTFT, throughput and cost per million tokens belong together.
  • AgentCore payments: per-request agent purchases now have concrete budget, seller and audit joins for run-level cost and policy analytics.
  • Genie ZeroOps: track the evidence-to-PR path and realized repair outcome; 80,000 observed issues is scale context, not proof of repair quality.
  • Phoenix 22: DECISION and EMBEDDING cost spans are directly relevant to router and retrieval cost attribution; plan for the major-schema migration.
  • Unfaithful work reports: a done claim needs its own evidence check, separate from task success, with artifact/revision and unverified conditions preserved.
  • Held While Waiting: active sessions, idle time and held memory can be more useful capacity signals than CPU; explicit session close is measurable and actionable.
  • Tether: stateful resource labels and staged effects offer deterministic policy evidence, but every verdict needs a modeled-scope and false-positive denominator.
