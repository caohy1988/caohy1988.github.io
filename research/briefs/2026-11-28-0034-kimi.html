Read this first

MeterRouter is the clearest cost-control result. It chooses each agent’s role, model, incoming edges and stopping point against a per-query dollar budget and realized spend. Across five benchmarks it averages 66.72% accuracy at $0.00256 per query, versus MasRouter’s 66.80% at $0.01415—about 82% less spend for a headline accuracy difference of only 0.08 points. This is not a universal routing guarantee: the workloads are public reasoning/code tasks, prices are fixed accounting rates, tight budgets truncate some answers, and changing prices or adding a model uses retraining rather than magic zero-shot adaptation.

HoMe and AgentLens address two other parts of the evidence loop. HoMe versions session-level memory, diagnoses construction/retrieval/answering failures and optimizes the whole memory harness. It improves BEAM-1M from 0.650 to 0.704 while cutting query-stage tokens from 101.8K to 60.4K; LongMemEval-S falls from 43.9K to 17.9K tokens but its 0.888→0.916 score gain is not significant (p=0.25). AgentLens connects static weaknesses and guardrails into audit paths, then proves exploitability in the agent’s real workflow: 44 manually validated vulnerabilities across five coding agents, with 12 CVEs. Its total remains a lower bound, not a census, because the five-agent evaluation has no independent ground truth.

Industry has no confirmed NEW rank but three useful Progress moves. Pydantic AI 2.77.0 adds compaction and model-attempt provenance, incomplete-stream states, fallback-subagent usage accounting and durable conversation stores. The Strands monorepo train adds evaluation controls and per-turn ACP usage. LiteLLM’s LTS patch repairs cancelled-stream billing so provider-reported usage is not replaced by zero or an estimate. Mastra’s encrypted-secrets launch is held because only a day-level Nov. 27 timestamp is available; Langfuse’s organization-model feature shipped before the window; the remaining registry moves are patches or prereleases.

NEW

1. HoMe: Toward Practical Harness Optimization of Memory Systems for AI Assistants — https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34624

Full PDF read: yes — entire 29-page PDF read via pdftotext, including the formal framework, diagnosis and sampling design, all experiments, conclusion, references and Appendices A.1–A.9 with stability, execution traces, token accounting and all agent prompts. arXiv v1 is dated 2026-11-25; the live catalog has no card for this id.

Problem and method. HoMe treats the memory harness as code, prompts and configuration around a fixed model. It commits memory after every session, then traces failed answers backward through retrieved evidence, final memory and session diffs to distinguish construction, retrieval and answering failures. A separate diagnoser aggregates case studies into shared mechanisms; the optimizer can branch from any evaluated harness. Smoke tests and structural gates screen candidates, adaptive sampling focuses formal evaluation on informative cases, and construction-equivalent candidates can reuse persisted memory (§3; Figure 1).

Results to reuse. With six formal candidate evaluations per benchmark after the full initial evaluation, HoMe improves LongMemEval-S from ReMe’s 0.888 to 0.916 while reducing average query-stage tokens from 43.9K to 17.9K; on BEAM-1M it improves 0.650→0.704 and 101.8K→60.4K (§4.2; Table 1). The BEAM gain is significant (paired bootstrap 95% CI +0.028 to +0.082, p=0.0002), while the LongMemEval score gain is not (95% CI −0.016 to +0.072, p=0.25); both token reductions are significant (Appendix A.1). A second independent BEAM search reaches 0.720 at 68.3K tokens and also improves significantly over ReMe (Appendix A.4). Total BEAM-100K optimization consumption falls 44.1% versus MemPro, 185.2M versus 331.2M tokens (Appendix A.8; Table 12).

Qualifications. LongMemEval-S is randomly split into 250 training and 250 test cases; BEAM optimizes on BEAM-100K and tests on independent BEAM-1M cases. Adaptive subsets deliberately favor difficult units and their aggregate scores are not unbiased full-set estimates, so candidate selection needs shared-case comparisons and final full-set evaluation (§3.3; Appendix A.7). Query tokens include input and output without deducting cache hits and exclude the initial construction/optimization cost; BEAM calls increase from 4.5 to 6.7 even as tokens fall. Some categories regress, including LongMemEval multi-session and BEAM knowledge update/information extraction (Tables 4–5). BQAA can reuse the versioned evidence, failure-stage labels and candidate lineage, but should report full-set quality, total optimization spend and per-category regressions rather than only the winning query-token curve.

2. MeterRouter: Budget-Aware Dynamic Routing for Multi-Agent Systems — https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34327

Full PDF read: yes — entire 31-page PDF read via pdftotext, including the architecture, meter, training algorithms, all main and ablation results, AI/ethics/reproducibility statements, references and Appendices A–G. arXiv v1 is dated 2026-11-25; the live catalog has no card for this id.

Problem and method. MeterRouter incrementally synthesizes a DAG of at most five agents. Each decision conditions on the query, remaining monetary budget, existing graph and earlier outputs; the router selects termination, role, backbone and incoming edges, then executes and meters that agent before the next decision. Budget-conditioned imitation initializes the graph policy and Dr. GRPO refines topology and model allocation (§4). A byte-based estimator removes unaffordable backbones and converts the remaining budget into a completion-token cap (§4.2).

Results to reuse. Across 3,654 test queries from Omni-MATH-2, MMLU-Pro, MuSR, BBEH and MBPP+, MeterRouter averages 66.72% accuracy at $0.00256 per query, compared with MasRouter at 66.80% and $0.01415 (§5.1–5.2; Tables 1 and 5). It is strongest on MuSR (72.2%) and MBPP+ (86.6%), but trails LLM-Debate with gpt-oss-120b by 4.5 points on BBEH. At least 99.98% of queries stay within budget at each tested level (§5.3; Table 8). Removing RL drops average accuracy to 57.34% at essentially the same $0.00255 cost; fixed chain and complete-graph variants lose 7.03 and 7.51 points respectively (Table 2; Appendices D.2–D.3).

Qualifications. Costs use provider-reported tokens and fixed Artificial Analysis rates for three Ollama Cloud models, not enterprise invoices or latency/queueing (§5.1; Table 6). Rare overruns arise because one conservative byte estimator is shared across tokenizers; at the tightest budget 16.93% of answers are truncated, and truncated or budget-blocked answers count as incorrect (Appendix D.4). Price-paired adaptation and adding a fourth backbone rerun training, so these are not zero-retraining online updates (Appendices D.5–D.6). Training uses 200 queries and validation 40 per benchmark, with domain-specific role profiles; the reproducibility statement says code will be released. BQAA should log budget, actual cumulative cost, graph/role/model choices, stop reason and truncation, then replay on enterprise workloads before treating a budget as a hard guarantee.

3. AgentLens: Graph-guided Security Auditing for LLM-based Agents — https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34380

Full PDF read: yes — entire 13-page two-column PDF read via pdftotext, including the threat model, graph/payload/scenario methods, every evaluation and ablation, case study, threats to validity, conclusion, data availability and references. arXiv v1 is dated 2026-11-25; the live catalog has no card for this id.

Problem and method. AgentLens builds an Audit Knowledge Graph from source code, configuration, invocation/data/control dependencies, weakness annotations and guardrail constraints. It derives paths from attacker-controlled entries to security-critical operations, generates path-specific payloads, and embeds them in realistic multi-turn tasks so the target’s original workflow and defenses remain in place (§II–III). Execution outcomes are checked against predefined violation conditions and security experts manually review the findings (§IV-A).

Results to reuse. Across Aider, MetaGPT, Goose, Cline and Roo-Code, AgentLens tests 70 audit paths and validates 44 vulnerabilities, averaging 2,396.44 seconds per agent; 12 reported issues have CVE IDs (§IV-A–B; Tables I–II). On Langflow it validates 11 vulnerabilities versus AgentFuzz’s 3—the source of the 3.67× comparison—and on Aider it validates 8 versus Agent Audit’s 3 and AgentFuzz’s 0 (§IV-C; Table III). On a separate 20-public-CVE OpenClaw benchmark it records 15 true positives, 0 false positives and 5 false negatives (75% recall), while standalone Claude Opus 4.6 finds 0, 2 and 2 known vulnerabilities in three runs (§IV-B).

Qualifications. The five-agent total has no independent ground truth, so undiscovered vulnerability counts and false-negative rates are unknown; the OpenClaw set contains selected public CVEs that the underlying model may have seen (§V). Evaluation is confined to coding agents, baseline configuration and manual outcome review affect comparisons, and undisclosed findings are not detailed. Repeated Aider runs produce 7–8 validated vulnerabilities, showing some generation variance (§IV-D). BQAA should preserve the audit path, payload provenance, guardrail state and execution receipt, distinguish statically suspected from execution-validated risk, and never turn “44 found” into “44 total” or “safe after these tests.”
